Sample flyers: how to attack the promotion

Three examples to learn from. Each flyer is for a different audience, so read the notes beside it. Select a flyer to expand it.

How to use this flyer

Best audience: Nadia only. Send to the people only Nadia knows. Text or DM it with a personal line, because the flyer is Nadia speaking to them.

  1. Lead with the person. The headline is “Spend Your Wednesday With Me” and the host’s name sits under it. The invitation feels like a friend asking, so send it one to one, never as a mass blast.
  2. Say the offer in seconds. Free admission, $5 shots 4–6 PM, Jamaican dinners, weekly guest DJs and dancehall at 11 PM each get their own gold line. Pull the one that fits that contact into your text.
  3. Use the food and drink photos. The cocktail and the jerk dinner show what to expect. Reuse them for the 6–8 PM dinner and takeout pitch.
  4. Make the next step easy. Address, phone and website are in the footer. Add “see you Wednesday?” and ask them to reply with how many are coming.

How to use this flyer

Best audience: Prestige only. Send to the people only Prestige Promotions knows. Post it where they already follow the brand, and share it in group chats.

  1. Sell the energy of the room. The DJ, the dance floor and the crowd carry this flyer. It suits people who come out for the party, so use it for the 10–11 PM warm-up and the 11 PM dancehall pitch.
  2. Name the promoter. “Promoted by Prestige Promotion” runs across the top. People who trust the brand see it first, so keep that line when you share.
  3. Highlight the offers in blocks. Free admission and $5 shots 4–6 PM are the hooks for early arrivals. Cut each block into a story or a text to push one reason to come.
  4. Post a clip with it. Pair the flyer with a short dance-floor video and the exact start time, so followers know what the night looks like.

How to use this flyer

Best audience: People both know. Send to people you both know, and use it for the wider community nobody has reached yet. Both names appear, so both of you share the exact same post.

  1. Show both names. “Presented by Prestige Promotion & Nadia” and “Hosted by Nadia” tell a stranger who stands behind the night. Post it from both accounts.
  2. Share it once, together. Agree on one posting time, tag each other, and keep one caption. This keeps mutual contacts from getting two different invitations.
  3. Give a stranger every detail. A new guest needs the days and hours, what is free, what the food is, and where to go. This flyer carries all of it with icons, so it works with no explanation.
  4. Send it to groups and venues. Offer it to workplaces, neighborhood groups and dance communities that welcome event posts, with the address and phone number in your message.

Wednesday lineup

Prestige and Nadia, answer these three questions for each time block before you promote it. Name the offer, pick a specific audience, and agree who will contact them and how.

4–6 PMHappy Hour

Come straight from work, grab a seat at the bar, and enjoy $5 shots.

What are you selling?
A free-admission after-work meetup with $5 shots from 4–6 PM. What makes it worth stopping in on a Wednesday?
Who are you selling to?
Nearby coworkers and people finishing an early shift. Which workplaces or groups can each of you name?
How will you reach them?
Prestige and Nadia each text their own after-work contacts. For people outside both networks, share a joint happy-hour flyer with nearby workplaces or neighborhood groups that welcome event posts.
6–8 PMJerk Dinner & Global Grooves

Meet over a jerk chicken leg dinner or pick up a plate to go. The music brings reggae together with soul, R&B, and Afrobeats.

What are you selling?
A jerk chicken leg dinner, with music if guests stay and takeout if they’re headed home. Which option should lead your pitch?
Who are you selling to?
Dinner groups, neighborhood residents, and commuters looking for a meal to take home. Who is most likely to come between 6 and 8?
How will you reach them?
Personally invite your dinner and takeout contacts. For new guests, share a photo of the actual plate, the address, and how to order. Confirm price and kitchen hours before posting.
8–10 PMGuest DJ & Sing-Alongs

Catch the weekly guest DJ and sing along to familiar hooks and throwbacks.

What are you selling?
A featured set from this week’s guest DJ, with familiar songs guests can sing along to. What can the DJ share that shows what the set will sound like?
Who are you selling to?
The guest DJ’s followers, people who enjoy sing-alongs and throwbacks, and dinner guests deciding whether to stay. Which group should get the first invitation?
How will you reach them?
Each host invites their own music contacts. Ask the guest DJ to share a short clip and set time with their followers, and announce the set to guests already in the lounge.
10–11 PMDance Floor Warm-Up

Step onto the floor for soca, dancehall crossover, and upbeat remixes.

What are you selling?
A chance to get onto the dance floor early with soca, dancehall crossover, and remixes. Why should someone arrive at 10 instead of waiting until 11?
Who are you selling to?
People who are ready to dance earlier and guests already at the lounge. Which contacts usually head home before the late set?
How will you reach them?
Send a 10 PM invitation to those contacts, and ask the DJ or host to announce the warm-up in the room. Use a dance-floor clip to show new guests what to expect.
11 PM–1:45 AMDancehall Party

Dance to classics, newer tracks, and crowd favorites.

What are you selling?
The main dancehall party from 11 PM to 1:45 AM. Which part of the set will give your crowd the strongest reason to come?
Who are you selling to?
Dancehall fans, late-night groups, and people getting off later shifts. Which Chicago dance communities could bring in guests neither of you knows?
How will you reach them?
Invite your own late-night contacts directly. Coordinate one shared announcement for mutual contacts, and ask the DJ and local dance communities to share the exact start time and address.
1:45–2 AMFinal Songs & Close

Catch the final songs before the night ends at 2 AM Thursday.

What are you selling?
A closing set for guests already in the room, plus a reason to come back next Wednesday. What should they remember about the night?
Who are you selling to?
Guests still there for the final songs, especially first-timers and people who brought friends. Who would welcome an invitation for next week?
How will you reach them?
Thank guests in person, announce next Wednesday before the final song, and ask who wants an invite. Follow up with the guests who opt in.
