Music

The decks are the curriculum

Inside a free Tuesday DJ lab on the South Shore, where students learn to mix with a guest woman DJ every week

By JALEN ODUYA-REESE (pseudonym)

Chicago gave the world house music, and it was made in rooms where people learned by watching someone who knew more than they did stand behind a pair of turntables.

The Rainbow DJ Lab at Rainbow Beach Park tries to put that kind of room on a schedule. Every Tuesday from 4 to 6 p.m., students in grades 6 through 12 work at 12 controller stations in the park fieldhouse, with two resident instructors and a different guest DJ each week.

The guests are women. That is deliberate. DJ booths are still a male-dominated space, and a student who sees a woman run a room, talk about her career and answer questions is learning something no worksheet teaches.

The program, free and run by Ras Tafari Inc., breaks the craft into 14 Missions and 42 skills: beat matching, phrasing, transitions, selection, reading a floor. Each skill gets a passport stamp when it is earned. There are no grades.

Kids join any Tuesday and move at their own pace. When all 42 skills are stamped and a final set is ready, they play the Rainbow Showcase, a public night for families and neighbors.

It is a small room with a big idea: if you hand a young person real equipment and real mentors, the music tends to follow.
