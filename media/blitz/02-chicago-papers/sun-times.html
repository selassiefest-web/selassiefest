Tuesdays at Rainbow Beach: kids earn passport stamps, not grades

A year-round DJ program in the park fieldhouse is free, open to students in grades 6 to 12, and lets a child join any week

By MARCUS TELLIS-BROOKS (pseudonym)

On a Tuesday in the Rainbow Beach Park fieldhouse, a sixth grader slips on headphones, nods to a beat only she can hear, and waits for the instructor’s stamp.

That stamp is the point. The Rainbow DJ Lab, run by the Chicago nonprofit Ras Tafari Inc., hands every student a passport of 42 skills. There are no letter grades. A skill is stamped when a student can do it, and a student who misses a week loses nothing.

Classes run from 4 to 6 p.m. every Tuesday, all year, and the program is free. Students in grades 6 through 12 can enroll on any Tuesday. The room holds 12 DJ controller stations, so each child has one.

Two resident instructors lead each session, and a guest DJ comes every week, including women who work in music professionally and can talk about what the job is really like.

For parents, the draw is simple: a safe, structured two hours after school in a neighborhood park, with a goal at the end of it. For students, it is a stage. The year closes with the Rainbow Showcase, where families watch young DJs play a set they built themselves.

Families who want to sign up can start at the Lab’s enrollment page. Organizers say a child can walk in on any Tuesday and start working through the 14 Missions.
