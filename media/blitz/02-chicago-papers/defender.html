Culture

From the home of house music, a new generation takes the decks

A free Tuesday program at Rainbow Beach Park trains South Side students in grades 6 to 12 to mix, perform and mentor

By OBADIAH TRENT-HOLLOWAY (pseudonym)

Chicago is the birthplace of house music, and the culture that made it was built by Black DJs and the communities that danced to them.

On Tuesdays, that lineage has a classroom. The Rainbow DJ Lab, a free youth program of Ras Tafari Inc., meets from 4 to 6 p.m. in the Rainbow Beach Park fieldhouse. Students in grades 6 through 12 learn the craft on 12 controller stations, with two resident instructors and a guest DJ each week.

The guests include women DJs who show students that the booth is a place for them, too, and that a career can grow from it: performing, producing, programming, running a business.

Students work toward a passport of 42 skills across 14 Missions. A skill is stamped when it is earned, and there are no letter grades. Graduation means all 42 stamped and a final set ready for the Rainbow Showcase.

The Lab is free, and a child can join any Tuesday. Its organizers say the aim is to keep a South Side tradition in the hands of South Side young people.
