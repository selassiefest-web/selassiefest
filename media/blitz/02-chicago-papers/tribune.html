Education

On the lakefront, a free DJ lab treats the booth as a classroom

The Rainbow DJ Lab at Rainbow Beach Park gives students in grades 6 to 12 a controller of their own, a passport of 42 skills and no letter grades

By DENISE ARMSTRONG-LEE (pseudonym)

Every Tuesday afternoon, the fieldhouse at Rainbow Beach Park on Chicago’s South Side fills with the sound of students learning to blend one song into the next.

The Rainbow DJ Lab, a free youth program of Ras Tafari Inc., a Chicago nonprofit, opened Tuesday, Jan. 12. It meets from 4 to 6 p.m. and runs all year. A student in grades 6 through 12 can join on any Tuesday and start where they are.

The room is set up with 12 stations, and each student works on a dedicated DJ controller. Nobody waits a turn. Two resident instructors teach every class, and a guest DJ visits each week, including women working in a field that has long been male-dominated.

Instead of grades, students carry a passport. It lists 42 skills across 14 Missions, from beat matching and transitions to reading a crowd and managing a set. An instructor stamps each skill when the student shows it. Graduation means all 42 are stamped and a final set is ready for the Rainbow Showcase.

The pitch to families is practical as much as musical. A DJ booth sits where music, technology, performance and small business meet, and the lab uses it to teach listening, timing, preparation and confidence.

The program is free to students. It depends on the equipment, instructors and partners that Ras Tafari Inc. brings together year after year, which is the part organizers say takes the most work.
