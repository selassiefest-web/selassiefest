Features

Twelve stations, forty-two skills, no grades

How a Tuesday DJ lab at Rainbow Beach Park is built, and why it works the way it does

By AMARA OKONKWO-DIXON (pseudonym)

The design is the story. The Rainbow DJ Lab has 12 stations because each student needs a controller of their own, not a turn at someone else’s. It meets Tuesdays because a program that happens every week becomes a habit.

It is a free youth program of Ras Tafari Inc., based in the Rainbow Beach Park fieldhouse. It opened Jan. 12, 2027, and runs year-round for students in grades 6 through 12, from 4 to 6 p.m.

The curriculum is 14 Missions, structured around seven colors, and 42 skills. Instead of tests, students keep a passport. When an instructor sees a student do something right (a clean transition, a good read of the room), they stamp it.

There are two resident instructors every week and a guest DJ, including women who work in the industry. The weekly guest keeps the lab connected to the working world of music.

Because a child can join any Tuesday and move at their own pace, the Lab does not have a start date to miss. Graduation is all 42 stamps and a final set, played at the Rainbow Showcase.

The lab is built around a simple idea: young people should not need to own expensive equipment to find out whether they love this work.
