1 · Summary

The request in one page

Your two parcels at the northeast corner of 63rd Street and Wabash Avenue (PINs 20-15-313-061 and 20-15-313-062) include a vacant corner lot. We propose to turn the corner into The Yawd, a Caribbean music, culture, food and community venue, for the 2027 outdoor season, under a written agreement you control.

What we ask: a seasonal license or short-term lease for permitted cultural and community programming during the 2027 season, with an option to renew each year.

What we offer: a seasonal fee to be agreed, plus we carry the full cost of upkeep, security, utilities, permits and insurance, and return the site clean at the end of each season.

What the owner gets

  • Income from an idle lot. A seasonal fee, agreed up front.
  • An occupied, watched site instead of a vacant one, which means less dumping, trespass, vandalism and code exposure.
  • No operating cost. We pay for maintenance, security, waste removal, utilities we use, permits and insurance.
  • Liability protection. You are named as additional insured on our policy, and we indemnify you for our use.
  • Full control. One season at a time, with renewal only if you agree. The agreement gives us no ownership interest or purchase right.
  • A better-known corner. A busy, cared-for venue raises the profile of the property and the 63rd Street corridor, and if you wish, you are recognized as the partner who made it possible.
  • Flexibility. You keep access at all times, and an early-termination clause protects a sale or development of the property.

Why a Caribbean cultural center

Chicago has dedicated cultural buildings for dozens of communities, but in our survey of 66 enclaves and cultural centers we found none for the English-speaking Caribbean: Jamaican, Trinidadian, Guyanese, Barbadian, Bahamian, Belizean and others. Community leaders cited by the Encyclopedia of Chicago estimated 40,000 to 50,000 Jamaicans in the area alone. Those communities gather at festivals, but have no permanent home. This corner can be that home, one season at a time.

The next step

A 30-minute meeting and site walk with you or your representative. We will bring a draft agreement for your counsel to review.

3 · The site concept

The Yawd: a permanent identity with changeable programming

"Yawd" is Jamaican for yard, or home. The venue keeps one recognizable look (gateway, stage, market row and community row) while each day's or weekend's program changes the signage, food and music inside it. Every element is temporary and removable.

Gateway & box office. Entry, check-in, security and guest information.
Main stage. Live music, DJs, fitness classes, talks and film.
Market row. Caribbean food, Ital and breakfast stalls, crafts and retail.
Community row. Culture House exhibits, Selecta Academy, wellness and play.
The commons. Open central floor for dancing, dining, classes and competitions.
Dominoes & oldies yard. Elders' tables, tournaments and storytelling.

5 · Operations and phasing

Run safely, opened in stages, closed out cleanly

Safety and compliance

  • City of Chicago special event, food vendor and amplified-sound permits for every public event
  • Licensed security and a first-aid plan scaled to each event
  • Clear, lit exits that no tent, queue or vendor may block
  • Written weather rules: activity stops for lightning, high wind, extreme heat or poor air quality
  • Health-department-compliant food service and handwashing stations
  • Fire extinguishers at every cooking station; cooking kept to a separate service edge

Daily routine

  • Pre-opening walk-through: site, exits, electrical, sanitation, accessibility
  • Opening only after a signed readiness check
  • Ongoing weather and crowd monitoring
  • Post-close sweep of the site and adjoining sidewalks
  • Overnight lock-up and incident log, available to the Owner on request

Good neighbor commitments

  • Event dates and sound checks posted in advance
  • Deliveries and teardown within approved hours
  • A named contact for residents and businesses

Phasing

  1. Agree (fall–winter 2026). Meet, walk the site, sign the agreement and deliver the insurance certificate.
  2. Prepare (winter–spring 2027). Survey, code review and permit applications; site clean-up and perimeter security. No public use yet.
  3. Build out (from about April 15, 2027). Temporary stage, market and community rows, fencing, planters and signage.
  4. Season (Memorial Day weekend through Labor Day 2027). Soft opening with small, permitted programs, then the full calendar, coordinated with SelassieFest.
  5. Close and review (by September 30, 2027). Tear down, return the site clean, and review the season together before deciding on renewal.

6 · Who we are

The people behind the proposal

Caribbean Cultural Center is being organized as Yawd LLC, doing business as Caribbean Cultural Center. Its mission is to give Chicago's Caribbean communities a cultural home built on unity, heritage and community.

Stephen Henry, founder, is President of Ras Tafari Inc., a 501(c)(3) public charity and the producer of SelassieFest, a roots reggae and cultural festival. Ras Tafari Inc. would serve as the programming partner for nonprofit and community activities on the site.
