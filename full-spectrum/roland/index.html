You asked for a deck. Here's the short version.

A neighborhood lab where access turns into musicianship, and musicianship turns into a performance.

What it is

A free, year-round DJ class. Every Tuesday, 4 to 6 pm, at Rainbow Beach Park, from January 12, 2027. Twelve stations, one instrument per child.

What's missing

Our 14 Missions teach students to play other people's music well. They don't yet teach them to make their own. That's where Roland comes in.

What we're asking

A 30-minute call to scope a first phase: production tools for the room, a training session for our two instructors, and a report your team can use.

One update to my October 21 note. The model is simpler now: one class for everyone, grades 6 to 12 and open to all ages, with a different woman DJ teaching every week instead of a separate girls-only track.

Question 2

What we've already designed

The curriculum

  • 14 Missions, 42 Passport skills, 7 colors. Red "Meet the Machine" through Violet "Rainbow Showcase."
  • Graduation is mastery, not a date: all 42 skills stamped, plus a final set played live.
  • Stamps never expire. Showing up doesn't stamp a skill; missing a week doesn't erase one.

The room and the people

  • 12 stations, each with its own controller, laptop and volume-limited headphones.
  • Two resident DJ instructors, one man and one woman, each coaching six students.
  • A different woman DJ every week teaches her signature technique in the last 30 minutes.

A class app

Rolling enrollment, make-ups and a personal Passport for every child. Families get a private link that shows only their own child.

Safety in writing

Release only to listed adults with an ID check; consent recorded before any check-in or performance; 85 dB headphones.

A published budget

$95,589 for the first year: $75,123 cash and $20,466 in stations we're seeking in-kind. See the full plan.

The heart of the 90 minutes is a loop:

Mission Card→ Watch Me Think→ Deck Time→ Spotlight→ Check Out

An instructor thinks out loud at the decks. Every child goes to work alone on their own instrument. Then the room comes back together and a few students play what they made.

It's the same cycle Berklee's SP-404 Lab runs: instruction, independent creation, then the group reconvenes to share. Because nobody shares a controller, each child gets the full 30 minutes of Deck Time, not 7½.

Question 4 · Where Roland fits

The Beat Track: make the beat, then play it out.

We'd like to add a production track alongside the Passport, designed with your team. It builds on the DJ curriculum rather than replacing it, starting from the Missions that already point toward making music.

Green · Mission 8

Make a Third Song

Students already blend two records into something new. The Beat Track lets them build the third part themselves.

Blue · Mission 9

Hot Cues and Loops

Loops and cue points are pad thinking. It's the natural door into sampling and sequencing.

Violet · Missions 13–14

Build Your Set, Rainbow Showcase

The payoff: a student drops a beat they made inside the final set they play for the neighborhood.

The Beat Track is optional and never required to graduate, the same way the weekly guest sessions add to the Passport without gating it.

Beat Track, step one · try it

A child's first ten minutes

Sixteen steps, four sounds, one tempo. Tap the squares, then press play. This is the first thing a student would make, on their own instrument, before anything fancier.

Sounds are synthesized in your browser; no samples, nothing recorded. The narration pauses while the beat plays.

Question 6 · What will students create?

From a pattern to a performance

Beat 1

First Pattern

Program a 16-step kick, snare and hat pattern and hold it steady while it loops.

Evidence: a saved one-bar loop at the student's chosen tempo.
Beat 2

Sample and Flip

Record or load a sound, chop it, and assign the pieces to pads. Play them by hand.

Evidence: a kit of the student's own chops, played live for an instructor.
Beat 3

Original Instrumental

A drum beat, chords, a melody and one sound of their own design, arranged into about a minute of original music.

Evidence: an exported track, plus a short "what I was going for" from the student.
Beat 4

Play It Out

Perform the beat live, or mix it into their final set at a Rainbow Showcase.

Evidence: a recorded performance, with family consent.

Each stage is stamped by an instructor against a short rubric we'd like to write with your team, so "done" means the same thing every Tuesday. Beat 3 borrows the finish line of your Roland Cloud course: a beat, chords, melody and sound design in one original instrumental, and a project that has to pass.

Question 5 · How do instructors get ready?

Two instructors who can teach it the Roland way

  • One training session in December for both resident instructors. Virtual is fine.
  • Teaching materials we can adapt for ages 11 to 17, including the SP-404 Lab course model, if it fits.
  • A named contact for questions during the first phase.
  • One Tuesday guest session in the format you ran at the GRAMMY Museum: a short look at the 808, then a woman producer from Roland's artists building a beat live, then every child tries it.

What our instructors already do

Both residents teach every Passport skill, coach six students each and keep the records. They're paid $250 a session, which covers setup, pack-up and stamps, so the Beat Track gets taught by people who are in the room every week, not by a visitor.

What the guest slot gives you

A fixed 30 minutes every Tuesday: 5 minutes of story, 10 of technique, 10 of every child trying it, 5 of questions. A Roland session would slot straight in.
