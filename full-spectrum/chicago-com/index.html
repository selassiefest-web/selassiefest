Young South Shore

Youth voices on chicago.com, starting with Full Spectrum at Rainbow Beach. Today's young contributors are tomorrow's members.

A proposal from Ras Tafari Inc., a South Side 501(c)(3)

The moment

chicago.com is building neighborhood habits right now.

South Shore went live this month as one of five pilot communities. The habits people form in a pilot year (where they post, who they follow, which neighbors they trust) tend to last.

Today the platform is built for adults. We think its fastest path to a loyal South Shore community runs through its young people.

Why youth contributors grow the platform

A young contributor never comes alone.

  • Every post brings readers: parents, grandparents, cousins and classmates sign up to see it, comment and share
  • Steady, local, positive content: a new youth story every week, rooted in a South Shore park
  • Loyalty that starts early: the teenager who first publishes on chicago.com at 15 is the member, donor and steward at 25
  • A story funders support: youth voice, local arts and news literacy, in a South Side pilot community
A natural next step

chicago.com already makes room for this.

  • Kids & Family is already a section of the site
  • Verified partners already publish guides, including a South and West Side youth organization, My Block, My Hood, My City
  • A community calendar where anyone can share events

What is missing is a supported way for young people to contribute in their own voice. We propose to pilot it in South Shore.

What young people would publish

Their work, shown to their neighbors.

  • Lab notes: a first blend, a breakthrough, a skill explained in their own words
  • Mix of the month: a student set, with the story behind the song choices
  • Remix the Record (February): songs about real Black history, every fact checked against two sources, published with the sources
  • Beach night recaps: photo stories from Rainbow Wednesdays, with permission from everyone pictured
  • Interviews: young reporters talk with each week's guest woman DJ
A sample contribution

"The first time I made two songs fit together."

A student explains what they tried, what sounded wrong, and what finally worked, with a short clip or an approved photo. It closes by asking neighbors to share a musical memory or a word of encouragement.

An illustrative story idea. No real student quotes or results are implied.

A six-week pilot

One youth story a week, starting with the Lab.

  • Before launch: agree on the publishing route, permissions, editorial roles and moderation with the chicago.com team and the South Shore steward
  • Weeks 1–6 (January–February 2027): one contribution each week, rotating authors and formats, timed to the DJ Lab's first weeks and Remix the Record
  • After week 6: review youth feedback, neighbor response and staff time, then decide together what comes next
Safe and supported

Young people own their stories. Adults keep them safe.

  • Permission first: written parent or guardian permission and each young person's own agreement; participation is always voluntary
  • An adult editor from Ras Tafari Inc. coaches contributors, checks permissions and prepares each post; teens publish through an approved adult route
  • Privacy: first-name or group credit, no contact details or schedules, no photo without permission (written permission for anyone under 13)
  • chicago.com's standards apply: your team chooses the publishing location and how comments are handled
