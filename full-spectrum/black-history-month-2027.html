Four Wednesday Listening Nights

A global, all-ages DJ playbook. Four 75–90 minute sets that move from spirituals and civil rights through soul, Afrobeat and the diaspora, into hip-hop and Afrobeats, building to the Feb 27 Journey Set. Tracks from Africa, South America, Central America, the Caribbean, Europe, the UK and Asia.

Read this before you load a single crate

How the four nights fit together, and how to use this page.

On the calendar

Four Wednesday listening nights in the Rainbow Beach Park fieldhouse: Feb 3, 10, 17 and 24, 2027. On the Saturdays in between, family teams build their own songs in Remix the Record, and everything comes together in the Journey Set on Saturday, Feb 27. See February on the calendar.

How to use this page

Each night has a running order with a cue note per track, a DJ panel (arc, mixing, what to do if you run long), and a bench of alternates to swap in. The Global Crate is a larger pool for longer slots or the Journey Set. It is not meant to be played top to bottom.

Use “Copy running order” to drop a set into a notes app, and “Show caution flags only” to see every track that needs a clean edit, a content warning, or a version check before you play it.

Check every track before the night

Artists, versions, running times and clean-edit availability all need confirming in your library before load-in. Tracks marked Verify or Clean edit must be checked first. See Before the Night.

Night 1 of 4 · Wed, Feb 3, 2027

Arc & pacing

  • Minutes 0–20: listening set. Nobody dances yet, and that's correct.
  • Minutes 20–55: heavy middle. Strange Fruit → jazz elegies → Brazil/Cuba as relief.
  • Minutes 55–90: lift and land. Sam Cooke to Glory is a straight line up.

Mixing notes

  • This night is not beatmatched. Crossfade, don't mix. Let songs end.
  • Keep vinyl crackle or room tone between tracks rather than silence.
  • If you must mix, do it under applause, not under Billie, Nina, or Coltrane.

Running long or short

  • Long: cut 8 and 18 (both long, both swappable).
  • Short: add Keep on Pushing (Impressions) or Zumbi (Jorge Ben) from the bench.
  • Emergency filler: a second instrumental from the Ramsey Lewis or Ahmad Jamal catalog.
Bench · 5 alternates

  • Zumbi – Jorge Ben (Brazil). Zumbi dos Palmares, Black resistance. Best South American addition to this night.
  • Asimbonanga – Johnny Clegg & Savuka (South Africa). Currently on Night 3; also works here as an early anti-apartheid echo.
  • Biko – Peter Gabriel (UK). Frame it explicitly as an ally tribute to Steve Biko, not a Black artist's own testimony.
  • Canto das Três Raças – Clara Nunes (Brazil). Beautiful, but the "three races" framing needs a spoken introduction.

Arc & pacing

  • Tracks 1–6: wall of anthems. Keep transitions fast; don't let the energy sag.

Mixing notes

  • Everything here sits around 100–120 BPM except the Fela tracks (~105 but feels slower).
  • Fela and Manu Dibango run on long vamps; you can mix in and out of them anywhere.
  • Revolution Will Not Be Televised is your reset button. Use it to buy time.

Running long or short

  • Dance-floor filler: Back to Life (Soul II Soul) or Sorriso Negro (Dona Ivone Lara).

Arc & pacing

  • Tracks 1–4: Jamaica, steady. One-drop groove, no rushing.
  • Tracks 5–9: UK and South Africa. Political turn; the room gets quieter and more focused.
  • Tracks 10–14: the Americas. Rhythms change fast; that's the point.

Mixing notes

  • Reggae sits around 70–80 BPM in half-time feel. Mix on the one-drop.
  • Free Nelson Mandela → Nelson Mandela is a tempo jump; use a short announcement or a drum fill between them.
  • Faraó is your peak. Everything after it is a cool-down, so don't put it too early.

Running long or short

  • Long: drop one of the two Haiti tracks, or La Negra if the room has cooled.

Checklists

Print these. Tape them to the case. Use them every single night.

Before you load a crate

  • Confirm every artist and version A single edit and an album cut are different tracks. Check Fables of Faubus, Move On Up and Haiti specifically.
  • Check every clean edit actually exists Dave, Sampa the Great, M.I.A., Burna Boy, Tyla, and every hip-hop track on Feb 24.
  • Time every track Add them up. If it's over 90 minutes, cut now, not at 9:40 pm.
  • Note BPM and key You don't need to beatmatch this material, but you need to know where the tempo jumps are.
  • Normalize your levels Gospel from 1969 and Beyoncé from 2020 are not the same loudness. Fix it in the software, not on the desk.
  • Have a backup copy Two drives. One offline. No exceptions.
  • Write down the heavy tracks Know exactly where Strange Fruit and Sonny's Lettah sit, and what comes after them.

Content safety for an all-ages room

  • No profanity on screen That includes song titles. Mississippi Goddam and F.U.B.U. need a display decision, not just an audio one.
  • Label the heavy songs out loud One sentence before Strange Fruit or Hell You Talmbout goes a long way.
  • Never two heavy tracks back to back Always put an instrumental or a celebration between them.
  • Have a quiet corner Some people will need to step out. Make sure there's somewhere to step out to.
  • No talk-over on the testimonies Billie, Nina, Abbey Lincoln, and Gil Scott-Heron get silence underneath them.

Running order rules for young DJs

  • Open with something known. Earn the room before you teach it.
  • Close with a message, not a banger. Every one of these four nights ends on purpose.
  • Keep three escape tracks loaded A dance track, an instrumental, and a sing-along. If the room turns, you have thirty seconds to fix it.
  • Watch the room, not the laptop. If the floor is empty during a long track, that's information; don't wait for the whole twelve minutes.
  • Say the country out loud. "This is from Colombia." "This is from Senegal." Never say "Africa" as if it's one place.
  • Credit the artists on screen Title, artist, country. Every track. It's the whole point of the program.
  • Leave 10% buffer Someone will talk, the projector will fail, or a track will run long. Plan for it.
