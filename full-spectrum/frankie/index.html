Whose shoulders they stand on.

A free, year-round DJ class for South Side young people, and a summer stage on the lake where they play for their neighbors. We want Frankie's legacy in the room from the first Tuesday, and we are asking you to help us get it right.

Rainbow DJ Lab and Rainbow Wednesdays · Rainbow Beach Park, South Shore · from Ras Tafari Inc., a Chicago 501(c)(3)

Why we wrote to you

Our students were born decades after the Warehouse. Their music grew from it.

"…the advancement of Frankie Knuckles' mission as the global ambassador of house music through media, conservation and public events…"

The Frankie Knuckles Foundation. Its initiatives include music in schools.

"For most of the people that went there, it was church for them."

Frankie Knuckles on the Warehouse, 1995 interview (Red Bull Music Academy). The record-store request for "Warehouse music" became "house music."

Ours is a classroom in a park, not a school: free, every week, in the city where house began. The young people in it, about grades 6 to 12, will learn to mix this winter. We want them to learn whose music they are mixing.

Play · Rainbow Wednesdays · June 2 to Sept. 1, 2027

Tuesday they learn it. Wednesday they play it for the beach.

  • 14 free summer nights, 6:00 to 8:30 pm, on the lakefront at Rainbow Beach. All ages, no tickets, no alcohol, clean lyrics.
  • Five segments every night: Open Decks, the Youth Showcase, the Live Lab with young musicians, the Theme Clash, and Generations Back-to-Back, where a young DJ and a veteran trade records one for one.
  • June 9 is Chicago House night, the season's second Wednesday: "the sound this city gave the world, re-edited by kids who were born long after the warehouse days."

Rainbow Wednesdays are a proposal, like the Lab. Dates and the performance area are set with the Chicago Park District.

Our promise

At least 30 minutes of every Wednesday belong to the Lab's students.

The House Minute

  • Before each set, the student takes the mic for one minute and teaches the beach what they learned on Tuesday
  • One name, one place, one record, in their own words
  • Written and rehearsed in class, so no one is caught cold

Then they play

  • Three students, about ten minutes each, inside the Youth Showcase
  • They mix the record they just taught, and credit the artists
  • The guest mentor names one specific skill out loud, and the crowd answers

A child who can teach a crowd about Frankie Knuckles, then mix the music for them, walks off that stage a different kid.

Proposed role · Hula Mahone

The world stage, on our beach.

Lamar "Hula" Mahone: Chicago house producer and DJ, and one half of The Outhere Brothers, the Chicago duo with two UK No. 1 singles in 1995. The group still plays UK festivals and arenas, from Kubix in Sunderland (2024) to 90s Fest in Sheffield (July 2026). He knows what it takes to walk into a booth you have never seen and win over people who have never heard of you. That is exactly what a child faces on a first Wednesday.

5:30, before the gates open

  • Walks each student through the booth "like a club abroad": find the cue, the headphones, the volume, the exit plan
  • Checks their first record and their first ten seconds
  • Agrees on one quiet signal: "I'm here if you need me"

On stage

  • Stands at the student's shoulder, hands off the controls
  • Teaches the crowd-reading trick he uses abroad, live, between sets
  • Closes the night in Generations Back-to-Back with a Lab student, record for record
Proposed role · DJ Lil' John

The voice that makes a kid feel like the headliner.

John Coleman III: eight years as a top mixer at WGCI 107.5, then the evening host on 106.3 WSRB. Radio taught him to play for everyone at once and to be on time to the second. Commercial DJing is a skill set: clean edits, tight sets, talking to a crowd. He can teach all of it.

5:30, the radio rehearsal

  • Runs each House Minute like a radio break: say it in 60 seconds, land it on the beat
  • Teaches "talking up the intro": speak over the opening and stop before the vocal
  • Checks every set for clean edits and an on-time ending

On stage, the host

  • Introduces each student like a radio guest: DJ name, the Mission they just earned, what they will teach
  • Hands them the mic, then gives them the room
  • After each set, names exactly what they did well, and leads the applause
How every mentor helps on Wednesday

The child keeps the controls. The mentor keeps the faith.

"The feeling, the feedback that you get from the people in the room, is very, very spiritual."

Frankie Knuckles, 1995 interview (Red Bull Music Academy)
  • Hands off. The student makes every musical choice and finishes every transition. A mentor asks before touching the controls, unless staff must step in for safety.
  • One quiet signal, agreed before the set, so help never looks like a rescue.
  • When a mix slips, the beat keeps going, the mentor smiles, and the next transition is the fix. That is live DJing, and the crowd learns it too.
  • Praise that is specific: "Did you hear that blend land on the one?" means more than "good job."
  • Every student leaves with one thing that worked and one thing to try next Tuesday.

If Frankie had designed this class

Questions only you can answer.

  • The crate. Which records should every student hear before they graduate, and which should they hear first?
  • The story. How do we tell the Warehouse story honestly to 11- to 17-year-olds, including the Black, Latino and gay dancers who made that floor what it was?
  • The dance floor. What did Frankie believe a DJ owes the people in front of the booth?
  • The first time. What would he want a young DJ to feel the first time a crowd answers back?
  • The line. What should we never do with his name, his image or his music?
