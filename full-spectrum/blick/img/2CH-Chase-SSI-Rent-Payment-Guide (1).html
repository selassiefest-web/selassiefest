The portal takes debit cards only: a credit card payment is refunded automatically. Card rules do not allow a surcharge on debit cards, so the way to save is to move tenants to bank payments, not to add a card fee.

Questions for Al and 2nd Chance Housing

Each question with the answer we propose. Decide the open ones before tenants are told.

  • Will a tenant paying by bank pay any fee in the portal?

    Answer: No. The portal adds $0. 2nd Chance Housing pays Stripe's processing and bank verification charges.

  • What happens if a bank payment fails or is returned?

    Proposed: 2nd Chance Housing absorbs Stripe's $4 failed-payment fee. We tell the tenant privately what happened and how to pay again. During the pilot, the tenant starts each payment; no automatic retries.

  • What if a tenant pays on time but the bank payment is still processing?

    Decision needed: The portal already shows a bank payment as “Processing” and keeps the time it was sent, so the tenant is not asked to pay twice. Decide how the due date and late fee are treated when an on-time payment later fails, and check it against the lease and any program rules.

  • What if a tenant's SSI is still on the government card, or they cannot open an account yet?

    Proposed: Keep a way to pay while they wait for ID, account approval or the first SSI deposit (debit card in the portal, Zelle or money order). Any bank account the tenant already has works too. Check the lease and program rules before changing the accepted ways to pay.

  • Does staff need the tenant's bank login, account numbers or Social Security number?

    Answer: No. The tenant enters their own bank details and approves the payment in Stripe's screens. Staff can help find the right buttons in the portal and check the payment status, nothing more.

Questions for Chase Brown Deer

Chase Brown Deer · 8800 W. Brown Deer Rd., Milwaukee, WI 53224 · 414-365-6306. Ask for a personal banker and individual appointment times for tenants.

Hi, I'm Al Harris with 2nd Chance Housing. We're helping tenants open their first bank accounts so they can pay rent through our portal. Some live entirely on SSI, get it on a government benefits card, and have $0 to spare. Could you walk us through opening an empty account, moving the SSI deposit without a gap, and paying the first two months of rent without bank fees?

1. Opening an account with $0

What Chase publishes
Chase Secure Banking has no minimum opening deposit. Chase asks for two original, valid IDs.

Ask the branch
  • Can I walk in with $0 and leave with an open account?
  • Will a signed lease or a letter from my landlord prove my current address if my photo ID shows an old address?
  • What can I use to prove my address if my utilities are included in my rent?
  • What can I use as a second ID if I don't have a standard one?
  • Can the account stay open at $0 until my first SSI deposit arrives?

2. Keeping the account free every month

What Chase publishes
No monthly fee for at least the first two statement periods on a new account. After that the fee is $4.95, waived with at least $250 in qualifying electronic deposits per statement period, which can include government benefits.

Ask the banker
  • Does my SSI deposit count as a qualifying electronic deposit?
  • What are my statement dates?
  • What happens if my benefit is less than $250?
  • What happens if my deposit posts in a different statement period than usual?
  • Can you waive the monthly fee while I switch over?

3. Moving SSI from the benefits card without missing a payment

Ask the banker
  • Can you give me a direct-deposit form?
  • Can you show me my routing number and account number?
  • Who can help if I have a representative payee?
  • How should the account be titled if I have a representative payee?
Ask Social Security (800-772-1213)
  • Who can ask for the switch: me, my payee, or the bank?
  • Where will my next payment go?
  • When does the change take effect?
  • How do I report the new account?

4. If the rent payment arrives before my SSI does

What Chase publishes
No overdraft fees on Secure Banking.

Ask the banker
  • If a rent payment from my account is refused or returned, does Chase charge me anything?
  • How do I tell a pending deposit from money I can actually spend?
  • How do I set up a low-balance alert?

5. What can still cost me money

Ask the banker
  • What do I pay to use an ATM that isn't Chase's?
  • Does taking out cash at this branch cost anything?
  • Do paper statements cost anything?
  • What does a replacement debit card cost?
  • How can I check my balance without a smartphone?
  • Can I have the written fee schedule?
  • Which charges am I most likely to run into, and how do I avoid them?

From the first appointment to the first bank payment

  1. Before the visit. 2nd Chance Housing gets the banker's name, the list of documents, and appointment times. The tenant checks that the account fits their SSI amount and payment date.
  2. At Chase. The tenant opens Secure Banking if it fits, and leaves with the direct-deposit form, the written fee schedule, and how to check money available. A tenant who already has a bank account can skip to step 4.
  3. With Social Security. The tenant or their representative calls Social Security at 800-772-1213 (or uses my Social Security) to move the deposit to the new account. Keep the benefits card, and a way to pay rent, until the first deposit shows as available at Chase.
  4. In the portal. The tenant chooses bank account, follows Stripe's steps to link the bank, checks the amount, and approves one rent payment. Save the confirmation. A bank payment can take up to four business days to clear.
  5. After paying. Check that the portal moves from Processing to Paid. If it fails, contact 2nd Chance Housing to confirm what is still owed before paying again (each failed try costs $4).

Before telling tenants about the $0 option: confirm that the tenant can open and use the account without fees in their situation, and that they know what to do if their benefit or rent payment is late.
