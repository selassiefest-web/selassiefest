1 Summary

Full Spectrum is a year-round music and culture program for Rainbow Beach Park, with five parts: (a) DJ classes, (b) Rainbow Wednesdays, a free weekly summer series, (c) live stage shows pairing DJs with local and major recording artists, (d) festivals, including SelassieFest and the heritage days of the Caribbean and African diaspora, and (e) fall and holiday parties from October through December. The kind of music programming people now travel downtown to Grant Park for comes to the South Side lakefront, free, built for every age and every background.

We are presenting all five parts together on purpose. We ask that Full Spectrum be reviewed and approved once, as one program. Then, when funding and dates line up for any part, scheduling it is a conversation with park staff about dates and logistics, not a new proposal to add a new program.

2 Purpose: bring it home

Why this program, and why at Rainbow Beach?

Chicago's biggest free music happens downtown, at Grant Park and on the lakefront around it. For a family at 77th Street that is a trip of about nine miles each way: transit fares or parking for everyone, an hour or more of travel, and a late ride home with small children or elders. Many South Shore families make that trip once a summer, and many never do.

Rainbow Beach already has what those downtown stages have: the lake, open lawn, a summer crowd, and a neighborhood full of musicians. Full Spectrum puts the programming where the people already are.

  • No trip required. Neighbors can walk, bike or take one bus to every part of the program.
  • Not just a show to watch. Downtown, residents are the audience. Here, they are also the performers. The DJ classes feed Rainbow Wednesdays, Rainbow Wednesdays feeds the stage shows, and the stage shows feed the festivals.
  • The neighborhood's own music. House, footwork, soul, gospel, reggae, Afrobeats and hip-hop all live in these blocks. The program is built from that music, not imported.
  • A reason to use the park all year. Classes run when the beach is closed, so the park stays active through fall, winter and spring.

3 Diversity of participants and audience

Who takes part, and who comes to watch?

The program is designed so that no single group owns it. Diversity is built into the format, not added at the end.

Kind of diversityHow Full Spectrum builds it in
AgeClasses start with young children (with a grown-up) and run through teens, adults and elders. Every Rainbow Wednesday closes with Generations Back-to-Back, a youth DJ and a veteran trading songs.
ExperienceFirst-timers at Open Decks, class graduates in the Youth Showcase, working local artists on the stage shows, and major recording artists as headliners. Everyone has a next step.
Musical cultureEvery genre is a color: house, footwork, soul, gospel, reggae, Afrobeats, hip-hop, Latin and more rotate through the season. Music belongs to whoever loves it, so a young person can take on any sound from any part of the world.
HeritageGrandparents bring the songs of their era; young DJs carry them forward in ways the elders have never heard. Each generation teaches the other.
GenderDJing has long been mostly men, and most young people who come forward are boys. Classes hold seats for girls and women, lineups are built to be balanced, and girls get a focus of their own (see Empress Decks).

4 Inclusivity

How do we make sure everyone is welcome and able to take part?

Everyone is welcome at Full Spectrum, whatever their race, ethnicity, nationality, religion, gender, gender identity, sexual orientation, disability, age, income or immigration status. Saying that is the easy part. These are the ways the program makes it true:

Cost is never the barrier

Every part is free to attend. Classes are free. A lending library of DJ controllers and headphones means students don't need their own gear. Young performers are paid stipends.

Physical access

An accessible viewing area on a firm surface, seating for elders and people with disabilities, and accessible classroom space. ASL interpretation at stage shows and festivals.

Sensory-friendly

Sound levels metered to park limits, free ear protection for children and performers, and a quieter zone at stage shows and festivals for anyone who needs a break.

Language

Flyers and open calls in English and Spanish, with other languages added as neighbors ask.

Family-friendly, every time

Clean-lyrics policy (radio edits only), no alcohol sold or served by the program, and guardian consent and an adult of their choosing on site for every performer under 18.

An open door, not a guest list

Open Decks sign-ups at the table that night, open calls with no experience required, and selection panels that include a council member and a music educator, not just us.

Every Full Spectrum event follows Chicago Park District rules and its nondiscrimination policy.

5 A focus on girls: Empress Decks

Who is missing, and what will we do about it?

In our own experience with young DJs and musicians, most of the young people who come forward are boys. We will count it rather than assume it: every season report shows how many girls enrolled, performed and worked crew. Girls are just as interested and just as talented, but far fewer step up to the decks, and fewer still to the sound board. If we run an open program and simply wait, it will mostly serve boys.

So Full Spectrum makes girls a deliberate focus, called Empress Decks, after the Empress Empowerment Village that Ras Tafari Inc. runs for girls at SelassieFest. Its theme carries over: see it, learn it, become it. Girls are more likely to step up when they see other girls and women already doing it, when they can learn among other girls first, and when there is a clear next step.

PartWhat Empress Decks adds
(a) DJ classesA girls-only Empress Decks track (about ages 9–17), taught by women DJs, alongside seats held for girls and women in every other track. A Live Sound track trains girls as sound and stage crew, where women are rarest of all.
(b) Rainbow WednesdaysAt least one girl or young woman DJ on every night's lineup, a woman among the featured DJs each month, and one Empress Night each season where girls and women hold every slot, the MC included.
(c) Live stage showsA girl or young woman opens every stage show, and girls work on the crew.
(d) FestivalsThe season's strongest girl performers get a set at the Empress Empowerment Village at SelassieFest.
(e) Fall & holiday partiesEmpress Decks students DJ the Kids Kostume Party and the Christmas Party for their own families.
RecruitingOpen calls go directly to girls' groups, girls' sports teams, church youth groups and school music programs, and say plainly that girls are wanted. Parents can sit in on a first class.
MeasuredEvery season report counts how many girls enrolled, finished a class, performed and worked crew, so the park can see whether the gap is closing.

The park welcomes it. Rainbow Beach Park staff have told us the girls’ focus is welcome: girls’ participation has long trailed boys’ across the Park District. We will put Empress Decks in writing in the park agreement, and girls are always welcome in every other class too.

The examples we point to already show it can be done: DJ RINOKA and DJ Michelle are both girls who started young.

6 The five parts

What exactly are we proposing?

a

DJ classes

Year-round · indoors in the off-season, outdoors in summer

Free, hands-on classes where neighbors learn to DJ: beatmatching and blending, building a set, live looping and editing, playing with live musicians, and the wordplay and mashups of the Theme Clash. The course is 14 weekly 90-minute sessions, or a six-workshop spring fast track. One core curriculum is taught to every age group and adapted by age and by challenge level (Mild, Medium or Spicy). It's taught by experienced Chicago DJs, using program-owned gear so no one needs their own.

Age groupWho it's for
Little DJsAges 5–7, with a parent or grandparent learning alongside
Junior DJsAges 8–11
Teen DJsAges 12–17; the main pipeline into Rainbow Wednesdays
Grown-Up & Elder DJs18 and up, including the parents and grandparents who grew up on house and the South Side sound systems
Empress DecksGirls, about 9–17, taught by women DJs (welcomed by park staff; see section 5)
Live instruments: young drummers, steel pan, horn, keys and string players join through session 12 (Mic, MC and Live Lab), the fast-track “Playing with Live Instruments” workshop, and the Live Lab segment at Rainbow Wednesdays, not as a separate class.

The full curriculum already runs in the Rainbow DJ Lab, our live classroom app: fourteen 90-minute sessions (or a six-workshop fast track), one shared class clock on kid, coach and parent screens, rotating station jobs, Passport stamps, and an emailed recap to families after every class. Open the DJ Lab · Register a child

From the park: a room at the park in the off-season, as available, and a regular time slot. Graduation: every class ends with a set at Rainbow Wednesdays Open Decks or a park event.

b

Rainbow Wednesdays

Summer · 14 Wednesday evenings, June 2 – Sept. 1, 2027 · fully planned

A free weekly youth DJ and live-instrument series on the beach. Each Wednesday is one color of the rainbow and one sound; the season goes around the spectrum twice and ends in full spectrum. Every night includes Open Decks, a Youth Showcase, the Live Lab, the Theme Clash (a theme everyone knows blended with a well-known song), and Generations Back-to-Back.

This part is already written out in full, with a nightly schedule, season calendar, production plan, safety rules and a $34,220 budget.

Series overview · Full Rainbow Wednesdays proposal · Examples of the format online

c

Live stage shows

Summer · selected evenings, dates set with park staff

Concert nights on a stage, where DJs perform alongside live musicians, local recording artists and a major recording artist as headliner. The stage shows are the top of the ladder: each lineup opens with young performers who came up through the classes and Rainbow Wednesdays, so a teenager who learned to blend in February can open for a national act in August.

  • Format: youth openers, then local artists with a live band and DJ, then the headliner.
  • Artists: no artists are booked yet. Headliners are booked only once a show is funded and its date is set with the park.
  • Production: stage, professional sound and lighting, security and crew supplied by the producing partner, under whatever permit the park requires for the show's size.
d

Festivals

Selected full days · including SelassieFest

Full-day, all-ages festivals of music, food, culture, vendors and family activities. Ras Tafari Inc. already produces one: SelassieFest, Chicago's roots reggae and cultural festival, with its 2027 edition planned for Saturday, July 24. The 2027 venue is not yet confirmed, and Rainbow Beach Park is under consideration. SelassieFest connects to the rest of the program: Rainbow Wednesdays on July 21 is the SelassieFest Warm-Up, and the strongest young performers of the season are offered a set at the festival.

At Rainbow Beach, the festival part includes:

  • A Full Spectrum end-of-summer festival, growing out of the Rainbow Wednesdays Finale: every class, every young performer and a headliner on one day.
  • Heritage festival days that celebrate the Caribbean and African diaspora of the South Side, planned with the communities that already celebrate them (see the table below).
  • SelassieFest at Rainbow Beach. Either SelassieFest 2027 itself, if Rainbow Beach becomes its venue, or a SelassieFest community day at Rainbow Beach, bringing the festival's music and culture to the South Shore lakefront.

7 How the five parts fit together

Why five parts in one program?

The first four parts are a ladder. A neighbor can start as a spectator and, step by step, end up on a festival stage, all without leaving the park. The fall and holiday parties keep the ladder going in the off-season: class students play for their own families while the beach is closed. Empress Decks runs up every rung, so girls climb the whole ladder too.

  1. Learn

    DJ classes. Free, with gear provided, for every age.

    Empress Decks: a girls-only track taught by women DJs

  2. Play

    Rainbow Wednesdays. Open Decks, then a paid Showcase set in front of a real crowd.

    Empress Decks: a girl on every lineup, plus Empress Night

  3. Open

    Live stage shows. The best young performers open for local and major artists.

    Empress Decks: a girl opens every show

  4. Headline

    Festivals. A set at the end-of-summer festival or at SelassieFest.

    Empress Decks: sets at the Empress Empowerment Village

8 Approve once, schedule as ready

What are we asking the park to approve?

We are asking that the park and its leadership review all five parts now and recognize Full Spectrum as one program at Rainbow Beach Park. Once that happens, adding a class cohort, a stage show, a festival day or a holiday party is a scheduling conversation, not a new proposal and a new approval each time.

Decided once, now

  • That all five parts belong at Rainbow Beach Park
  • Their purpose, audience and the standards in this document
  • The producing partner and how the program is led
  • The general park spaces each part would use

Confirmed each time, with park staff

  • Specific dates and times
  • Site plan for that event
  • Certificate of insurance naming the Chicago Park District
  • Any special event or amplified-sound permit the event's size requires

9 Standards for every event

What can the park count on, every time?

  • $1,000,000 general liability insurance naming the Chicago Park District as additional insured.
  • Background checks for every adult who works with young people; guardian consent for every performer under 18.
  • Clean-lyrics policy for every set, MC and artist. No alcohol sold or served by the program.
  • Public performance licensing for recorded music (ASCAP, BMI, SESAC and GMR, or confirmation that the park’s licenses cover it) is in place before any public performance.
  • Sound metered to park limits; generators and equipment supplied by the producing partner unless the park offers otherwise.
  • Load-in and load-out on the agreed schedule, nothing left overnight unless agreed, and the site left cleaner than we found it.
  • A short report to the park after each season: attendance, performers, where the audience came from, and what to change.
