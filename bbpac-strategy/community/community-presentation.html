Written for: every neighbor, beachgoer, and community member
Section 1 of 11

What is BBPAC?

BBPAC stands for the Bongo Beach Park Advisory Council. It's a group of neighbors, swimmers, drummers, families, and longtime beachgoers who came together to ask the Chicago Park District (CPD) for one simple thing: let 63rd Street Beach — the beach many of us call Bongo Beach — have its own dedicated community advisory council, the same way many other Chicago parks and beaches already do.

Section 2 of 11

Why is it needed?

63rd Street Beach isn't a small patch of sand. It has a historic 1919 Beach House, a full swimming operation with lifeguards, concessions, restrooms and showers, a boat launch, parking, and about 12 acres of dune and shoreline habitat that's home to more than 200 bird species. It also carries a real cultural identity, rooted in the drumming tradition that gave it the name Bongo Beach.

Right now, for advisory purposes, all of that is treated as just one piece of the much larger Jackson Park — 551 acres that also include harbors, a golf course, museum-area grounds, a wooded island, a Japanese garden, and playing fields. A beach this large, this historic, and this distinct deserves a group whose whole job is paying attention to it.

Section 3 of 11

Why doesn't Jackson Park's current council (JPAC) solve this?

This isn't about JPAC — the Jackson Park Advisory Council — doing anything wrong. JPAC represents all of Jackson Park, and its work does touch the beach sometimes, from parking discussions to beach cleanups. JPAC has even said publicly that the beach and Beach House are "managed separately as a Special Event Venue," which is really our whole point: the beach already runs somewhat on its own track day to day.

The honest question is simply whether one council, covering an enormous regional park with dozens of very different features, can also give a major, independently run beach the focused, ongoing attention it needs. We think the beach needs its own dedicated voice — not a replacement for JPAC, but a partner alongside it.

Section 4 of 11

What exactly is Bongo Beach?

Bongo Beach is the community name for 63rd Street Beach, on Chicago's South Side lakefront. It has a historic Beach House built in 1919, at a site that exists because South Side residents demanded more beach access. Today it includes a designated swimming beach and distance swimming area with lifeguards, concessions, public restrooms and showers, a water play feature, a non-motorized boat launch, parking, and accessible beach access.

It also has about 12 acres of dune and shoreline habitat used by more than 200 bird species, including some that are threatened or endangered. And for generations, it has been a gathering place for drumming and community celebration — the tradition the beach is named for.

Section 5 of 11

What would BBPAC control — and what would it NOT control?

This is important, so we want to be exact: BBPAC would not control the beach. It would be an advisory council — just like JPAC, and just like the South Shore Cultural Center Advisory Council. The Chicago Park District would still own the beach, run it, staff it, maintain it, and make the final decisions.

What BBPAC would do is advise CPD, advocate for the beach's needs, and help organize community input and volunteer stewardship — things like cleanups, fundraising ideas, programming suggestions, and keeping community concerns in front of the people who run the park. That's the same role every existing park advisory council plays.

Section 6 of 11

How will this improve the beach?

A dedicated council means someone is always paying attention specifically to Bongo Beach — not squeezing it in as one item on an agenda covering an entire 551-acre park. That means a steadier community voice on things like restrooms, cleanliness, lighting, and accessibility; stronger advocacy for preserving the historic Beach House; support for cultural programming, including drumming; a real partner for environmental stewardship of the dunes and shoreline; and a direct, organized channel between the community and CPD.

Section 7 of 11

Is this privatization?

No. BBPAC would not buy, own, lease, or take over anything. It wouldn't charge admission or run the beach in place of CPD. It would simply be community members volunteering to advise the public agency that already owns and operates the beach — exactly the same arrangement that already exists at parks all over Chicago.

Section 8 of 11

Will the beach remain public?

Yes. The Chicago Park District would continue to own, operate, and maintain 63rd Street Beach, and it would remain free and open to the public, exactly as it is now. The whole point of BBPAC is to make that public space stronger for the community that already relies on it.

Section 9 of 11

How can I participate, and what are we asking CPD to do?

You can add your name to the community petition supporting dedicated recognition for the beach, tell your alderman or CPD that you support it, and come to community meetings as they're scheduled. If you're part of a group — a drumming circle, a block club, an environmental or historical organization, a swim group — your organization's endorsement helps too.

What we're asking CPD to do, in plain terms, is to formally determine whether 63rd Street Beach can have its own advisory council, given that CPD's current rule is one council per park and the beach is currently counted as part of Jackson Park. We're not asking to take the beach away from anyone — we're asking CPD to give the beach its own seat at the table.

Section 10 of 11

What matters to different parts of our community

BBPAC is meant to represent everyone who cares about this beach. Here's what we understand matters most to each group — and we want to hear from you if we're missing something.

Bongo/drumming community: Protecting the drumming tradition that gave the beach its name, keeping access to gather and play, and getting that history formally recognized as part of the beach's identity.

Beach users & families: Clean restrooms, reliable access, safety, enough parking, and facilities that actually work.

Swimmers: Lifeguards on duty, good water quality, and dependable swimming access.

Environmental community: Real stewardship of the dunes, shoreline habitat, and the birds and wildlife that depend on them.

Historic-preservation community: Preserving the 1919 Beach House and making sure it gets the capital investment it needs.

Youth, parents, and schools: Good programming, leadership opportunities for young people, and a beach that's safe to recreate at.

Seniors & disability advocates: Real, usable ADA access — not just a ramp on paper, but facilities people can actually use.

Event and community organizers: Fair, transparent access to permits and space, and continued support for cultural programming.

Neighbors: Accountability from whoever manages the beach, and a direct, dependable voice when something isn't working.

Businesses & institutions: Good partnerships, healthy visitor activity, and responsible, well-managed activation of the space.

The ask, in one sentence

Recognize 63rd Street Beach as distinct enough to have its own community advisory council — so the people who love this beach have a dedicated place to be heard.

Section 11 of 11

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to pay to join?

No. There's no cost to support this effort or to get involved. If BBPAC is eventually recognized and formally organized, any membership process would follow CPD's standard, no-cost rules for park advisory councils — the same ones every existing PAC follows.

Will there be an election?

Eventually, yes — that's how CPD advisory councils work, with officers chosen by the community. But that comes later, after CPD agrees the beach can have its own council. Right now we're still working on winning that recognition.

What if I just want to attend meetings, not join anything?

That's completely fine. Advisory council meetings are open to the public, and just showing up and listening — or speaking up when something matters to you — is a real and valuable way to participate.

Will this change who can use the beach?

No. The beach would stay open to everyone, free, exactly as it is today. BBPAC would be a voice for the community, not a gatekeeper.

What happens to events already scheduled?

Nothing changes for existing events. CPD still runs the beach and its special-event permitting today, and that wouldn't change while this recognition process plays out.

How long will this take?

We honestly don't know yet. This is a multi-step process, and we're still early in it — asking CPD to determine whether the beach can even have its own council before any formal council can be formed. We'll keep the community updated as it moves.

What if CPD says no?

Then we'll ask CPD to explain why, and what would need to change for the answer to be different — and we'll keep building community support in the meantime. This is a long-term effort, not a single ask-and-done.

Is this connected to any political party?

No. This is a community-governance and representation issue, not a partisan one. We're working with our alderman and with CPD as the public agency that runs the park, not with any political party or campaign.

Who's organizing this?

A group of neighbors, beachgoers, and community members who care about this beach — swimmers, drummers, families, environmental advocates, and others. It's a grassroots effort, not a single person's project.

Where do I sign up?

Honestly, we don't have a formal sign-up system built yet — we're still in the early stages of this effort. For now, the best way to help is to add your voice of support and stay in touch as we set up ways for people to get more involved.
