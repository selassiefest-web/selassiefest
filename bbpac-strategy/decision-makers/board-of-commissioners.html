Board of Commissioners — Decision Brief

Section 1 of 10

Purpose of This Briefing

This briefing presents the Bongo Beach PAC (BBPAC) recognition question to the Board of Commissioners and its three relevant standing committees. It follows, and depends on, the briefing already prepared for General Superintendent & CEO Carlos Ramirez-Rosa (see the Superintendent briefing). CPD Code Chapter 7 gives the General Superintendent authority to classify park property and designate classifications, but any such action is transmitted to the Board for approval, amendment, or rejection. This deck therefore assumes the Superintendent's office, Planning & Development, and Law have already reviewed the matter, and prepares the Board and its committees to receive whatever recommendation results.

Section 2 of 10

The Board's Authority and Vote Threshold

CPD Code states: "The management and control of business and property of the Park District shall be vested in the Board of Commissioners." Any formal reclassification of park property, or any policy amendment governing PAC eligibility, is ultimately a Board-level act. Procedurally, four of the seven commissioners constitute a quorum, and four affirmative votes are required to carry a motion. The current Board is Dr. Marlon Everett (President), Modesto Valle (Vice President), Sharon Bush, Robert Castaneda, Sean Garrett, Philip Jackson, and Coya Paz Brownrigg.

Section 3 of 10

Case Summary

63rd Street Beach is presently classified within Jackson Park (Park No. 19), which already has a registered PAC (JPAC), and CPD's February 2025 PAC Guidelines recognize only one PAC per park. The petition is not simply "may we form a PAC" but a request for CPD to determine whether 63rd Street Beach and its Beach House — a historically distinct, operationally independent 1919 beach complex with its own aquatics, cultural, environmental, and safety responsibilities within a 551.52-acre regional park — can support dedicated PAC representation. Full supporting detail, including the operational-complexity case, the neglect/conditions record, and the requested outcomes (A, B, or C), appears in the Global Presentation. This deck presents only what the Board needs to authorize or refer the matter.

Section 4 of 10

Precedent Summary

The full evidentiary record is maintained in one place, the Precedent Library, and is only summarized here. Four precedents most directly support the proposition that CPD can reorganize or create distinct park identity within a larger property.

  • South Park's 1880–81 subdivision into Jackson Park, Washington Park, and Midway Plaisance shows Jackson Park's own administrative lineage begins with a subdivision by CPD's predecessor agency.
  • Grant Park → Maggie Daley Park (2014–15) is the cleanest modern precedent for a distinctly named, operationally independent "park within a park" while remaining officially within its parent park.
  • Seneca Park (#1242) was carved from Lake Shore Playground and given its own park number and its own PAC.
  • Recent boundary transfers (including 2024 Board actions affecting Dooley Park and Washington Park) confirm that park boundaries are administratively adjustable, not fixed.
Section 5 of 10

Programs & Recreation Committee Module

This committee's jurisdiction covers relations with citizens, community groups, and advisory councils; park and facility naming; and recreational and cultural matters — the committee whose subject-matter is closest to the community-representation core of this petition. The case for BBPAC rests on structural underrepresentation, not on any failure by JPAC: JPAC's own published mission covers all of Jackson Park's 551.52 acres, and its own materials describe 63rd Street Beach and Beachhouse as "managed separately as a Special Event Venue." One general advisory council for a park of that scale and diversity of use may not provide adequate governance for a major, independently operated historic beach complex with its own aquatics, cultural, historic, environmental, and accessibility responsibilities. Naming (e.g., an official "Bongo Beach" designation) is a related but separate procedure from PAC classification and may proceed on a parallel track.

Section 6 of 10

Capital Improvements Committee Module

This committee's jurisdiction covers long-term facility plans and the sale, lease, transfer, or acquisition of District land — the committee whose subject-matter governs any boundary or park-number action this petition may ultimately require. If Outcome A (distinct park status) or Outcome B (facility-specific exception) requires a new or adjusted park number, boundary description, or property record for 63rd Street Beach, that is a Capital Improvements matter. The Precedent Library's Bucket A holdings — subdivision (South Park), sub-identity within a larger park (Maggie Daley Park), new numbering out of an existing park (Seneca Park), and routine boundary adjustment (2024 transfers) — establish that CPD has repeatedly taken exactly this kind of action administratively. Planning & Development (Director Heather Gleason) is expected to document the specific administrative/property mechanism and proposed boundary before any Board-level property action is drafted.

Section 7 of 10

Administration Committee Module

This committee's jurisdiction covers legal matters, government relations, and general policy — the committee whose subject-matter governs the underlying legal authority question. The central open legal question, still pending Law's review, is: what officer or body has authority to designate 63rd Street Beach as a distinct park or administrative unit, what procedure applies, and does the "one PAC per park" rule require formal Board action to amend, or only an administrative interpretation? Law Department / General Counsel Heather L. Keil's opinion on required legal instrument and Board-approval necessity is the governing input for this committee's role, and for whether this matter reaches the Board as a classification action, a policy amendment, or an acknowledgment of a staff determination.

Section 8 of 10

Procedural Note: Referral Is Not Yet Fixed

The Board President may suspend standing-committee referral, and matters originating with the General Superintendent can in some circumstances proceed directly to the full Board without committee hearing. Accordingly, this deck prepares all three committee modules — Programs & Recreation, Capital Improvements, and Administration — without assuming three separate committee hearings will occur. The Board Secretary and the Law Department, not this campaign, will determine the correct procedural route once the Superintendent's office and Law have completed their review.

Decision Brief

Decision Requested

The precise Board action is deliberately not yet fixed. Depending on Law's and the Superintendent's forthcoming determinations, the request will take one of three forms: (1) a formal park or administrative-unit classification/designation for 63rd Street Beach; (2) an amendment to the PAC Guidelines' "one PAC per park" policy to accommodate distinct major facilities within very large parks; or (3) Board acknowledgment/ratification of a staff-level determination already within the Superintendent's existing classification authority. This brief will be updated once Law's opinion is received.

Authority

CPD Code vests "the management and control of business and property of the Park District" in the Board of Commissioners. Four of seven commissioners constitute a quorum; four affirmative votes are required to carry any motion.

Why Now

The General Superintendent's office, Planning & Development, and Law are actively reviewing the classification question. Bringing the Board's committees up to speed now — before the specific procedural route is finalized — avoids delay once Law's opinion and the Superintendent's recommendation arrive.

Evidence

See the Global Presentation for the full evidentiary record and the Precedent Library for the classification precedents summarized in Section 4 above.

Options

  • Outcome A — Recognize/designate 63rd Street Beach, its Beach House, and defined grounds as a distinct park unit for PAC purposes.
  • Outcome B — Determine that a sufficiently distinct major facility within a very large park may have its own registered facility-specific PAC notwithstanding the existing Jackson Park PAC.
  • Outcome C — Amend CPD policy to accommodate unique large-park facilities generally (major beaches, cultural centers, other independently operated complexes).
  • Take no action at this time.
  • Request further departmental study before any Board action.

Recommended Option

Outcome A is the organizing committee's stated preference, contingent on prior review and concurrence by the Superintendent's office and the Law Department. The Board is not being asked to select among the options today; it is being asked to receive this briefing and be prepared to act once that review concludes.

Consequences of Action

A Board resolution formally resolving Jackson Park's internal classification would settle the immediate BBPAC question and would likely establish a template for how CPD evaluates future subdivision or facility-specific PAC requests within other large, multi-facility parks.

Consequences of Inaction

The representation gap for 63rd Street Beach remains unresolved. Community organizing, petitioning, and advocacy will continue without a clear administrative pathway, and the underlying classification question will recur.

Next Procedural Action

Referral to the appropriate standing committee or committees (Programs & Recreation, Capital Improvements, and/or Administration) — or, if the Board President suspends standing-committee referral, direct placement before the full Board — as determined by the Board Secretary and the Law Department once the Superintendent's recommendation and Law's opinion are complete.

Does approving this set a precedent other large parks will invoke?

Likely yes. CPD has other large, multi-facility parks with a single registered PAC, and a Board-level resolution of Jackson Park's classification would probably function as a template for how future facility-specific or subdivision requests from other large parks are evaluated. This is a foreseeable consequence, not a reason to avoid the decision, and it is one reason the Administration Committee's policy-interpretation role matters here.

What does four affirmative votes actually require procedurally?

Four of the seven commissioners constitute a quorum, and any motion — including a classification action or policy amendment arising from this matter — requires four affirmative votes to carry, regardless of how many commissioners are present above the quorum minimum.

Has JPAC been consulted?

Not yet at the Board level. The campaign's internal guidance calls for a dedicated JPAC-facing presentation seeking support, or at minimum neutrality/no objection, and frames the case as "two strong voices, one historic Jackson Park landscape" rather than a critique of JPAC. JPAC's consent is not treated as a legal precondition to Board action unless CPD determines otherwise.

Why hasn't the Board been given a single, final recommended action today?

Because the correct legal instrument — classification action, policy amendment, or acknowledgment of an existing staff determination — depends on Law's opinion on CPD's actual authority and required procedure, which has not yet been received. Presenting a fixed ask before that opinion arrives risks committing the Board to the wrong procedural vehicle.

Does this require a property transaction, such as a sale, lease, or transfer of District land?

Not necessarily. No transfer of land out of CPD ownership is contemplated; the question is internal administrative classification of property CPD already owns and operates. However, if the mechanism Law identifies involves a formal boundary or park-number change, that falls within the Capital Improvements Committee's jurisdiction over District land, which is why that module is included regardless of the final legal vehicle chosen.

What happens if the Board takes no action and the matter is not referred to committee at all?

The status quo continues: 63rd Street Beach remains part of Jackson Park No. 19 under JPAC's sole registered PAC, and no new administrative pathway exists for facility-specific representation. The underlying community-organizing effort and any related public-records or policy requests would continue independent of Board action.
