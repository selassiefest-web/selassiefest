Tier 3 — Decision-Maker

General Superintendent & CEO — Decision Brief

Carlos Ramirez-Rosa sits at the center of CPD's administrative decision chain. This briefing is deliberately short — seven sections plus a two-page-style Decision Brief.

Role: Decision-maker (D)
Format: 7 sections + Decision Brief + FAQ
Section 1 of 8

The Problem

CPD currently lists 63rd Street Beach inside Jackson Park (Park No. 19), which already has a registered PAC — the Jackson Park Advisory Council. CPD's February 2025 PAC Guidelines recognize only one PAC per park, so an ordinary Letter of Intent for a "63rd Street Bongo Beach PAC" can be rejected in a single sentence under the current classification. This is therefore not a request to form a PAC — it is a request to resolve a classification question first.

Section 2 of 8

The Evidence

63rd Street Beach operates as a self-contained complex within a 551.52-acre park: JPAC's own materials describe it as "managed separately as a Special Event Venue," and it carries a historic 1919 Beach House, dedicated aquatics and lifeguard operations, concessions, and roughly 12 acres of dune/shoreline habitat supporting 200+ bird species. The case is operational complexity, not a complaint about JPAC. Full detail: Global Presentation.

Section 3 of 8

The Precedents

CPD has repeatedly reorganized park identity and beach-specific numbering. Maggie Daley Park shows a distinctly named, operationally independent "park within a park" created inside Grant Park. Lane Beach Park #1265 shows a beach far smaller and simpler than ours carrying its own park number. Rutherford Sayre Park shows CPD consolidating three parks into one in 1999 — the same authority run in the opposite direction from our request.

Section 4 of 8

The Administrative Alternatives

Three outcomes are available under existing authority. Outcome A: CPD designates 63rd Street Beach and its Beach House as a distinct park unit for PAC purposes. Outcome B: CPD formally interprets the one-PAC-per-park rule to allow a facility-specific PAC within a sufficiently distinct major facility. Outcome C: if neither fits current policy, CPD amends the policy itself to accommodate unique large-park facilities.

Section 5 of 8

Departmental Findings So Far

Departmental review has not yet begun. This presentation is the mechanism to launch it — routing the classification question to LCA, Planning & Development, Law, and Operations before any recommendation is finalized. No department has yet issued a technical finding on record.

Section 6 of 8

Community Support Status

Community organizing is underway — Bongo Beach cultural stakeholders, beach users, environmental and preservation groups, and neighboring institutions are being engaged. A formal community support petition and organizational endorsements are in progress but not yet complete; none should be represented as finished today.

Section 7 of 8

The Decision Requested

Direct the appropriate CPD departments to establish a defined administrative status for the 63rd Street Beach complex that permits dedicated PAC representation, and submit any necessary action to the Board of Commissioners.

Decision Brief

Decision Requested

Direct the appropriate CPD departments to establish a defined administrative status for the 63rd Street Beach complex that permits dedicated PAC representation, and submit any necessary action to the Board of Commissioners.

Authority

CPD Code Chapter 7 gives the General Superintendent authority to classify park property and designate classifications, transmitted to the Board for approval, amendment, or rejection. This exact wording may need to change once the Law Department confirms actual authority and the required instrument.

Why Now

The operational complexity underlying this request already exists — the beach already functions as a distinct Special Event Venue with its own historic, environmental, and safety responsibilities — and community organizing is underway. This is a routine administrative question, not an urgent one.

Evidence

See the Global Presentation for the full evidence bank and the Precedent Library for the full set of classification precedents.

Options

  • Outcome A — Distinct park status for 63rd Street Beach
  • Outcome B — Formal exception/interpretation allowing a facility-specific PAC
  • Outcome C — Policy amendment to accommodate unique large-park facilities
  • Take no action

Recommended Option

Outcome A, with Outcome B as fallback.

Consequences of Action

Opens a routine internal departmental review; does not itself commit CPD or the Board to any outcome.

Consequences of Inaction

The classification ambiguity persists, and the community's underrepresentation concern remains unaddressed.

Next Procedural Action

Direct LCA and Planning & Development to open the internal review described in the Stakeholder Matrix.

What exactly am I being asked to sign off on today?

Only a directive to CPD departments to open an internal review of 63rd Street Beach's administrative classification. You are not being asked to approve a new PAC, a boundary, or a Board action today.

Does this commit the Board to anything?

No. This directs a departmental review; any Board action would come later, only if the review determines one is legally required.

What's the political exposure here?

Low. The ask is framed as administrative governance and community representation, not a dispute with JPAC, and JPAC's own materials already describe the beach as separately managed.

Has JPAC agreed to this?

JPAC has not yet been asked to take a position; a separate tailored presentation to JPAC is planned. Their consent is not being treated as a requirement unless Law confirms it is.

Why hasn't the community petition been submitted yet?

Organizing is still underway. The petition and organizational endorsements are being built in parallel with this administrative request, not presented as complete.
