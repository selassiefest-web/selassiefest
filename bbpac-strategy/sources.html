Sources & Methodology

An honest account of where this system's content came from, what that means for how it should be used, and exactly what needs independent verification before any of it reaches CPD, the Board, or an Alderman.

Where this content originated

The factual claims, precedent research, organizational structure, and campaign strategy in this system originate from a research and strategy conversation the organizing committee conducted with an AI assistant, later compiled into a single planning document. This system converts that planning document into a navigable, audience-specific presentation architecture — it does not add new legal research, new historical research, or new evidence.

That distinction matters. This system is very good at one thing: organizing an argument so the right person sees the right version of it. It is not a substitute for legal review, archival verification, or a records request.

What is directly verifiable right now

Two hyperlinks survived from the original planning document intact and point to live, checkable pages:

  • South Shore Cultural Center Advisory Council — official site
  • Chicago Park District — Park Finder

Beyond those two links, the planning document's other citations were footnote-style references that did not survive into a linkable form. Every other named fact below — personnel names and titles, park numbers, historical dates, quoted CPD language, the "one PAC per park" rule, committee jurisdictions — is treated in this system as a claim to verify, not a verified fact, until the organizing committee confirms it against a primary CPD, City, Illinois Comptroller, Chicago Public Library, or Illinois statutory source.

Do not skip this if you are about to send a deck to CPD, the Board, an Alderman's office, or the press

Presenting an unverified personnel name, an incorrect park number, or a misquoted policy line to a government decision-maker does more damage to this campaign's credibility than presenting nothing at all — it hands a gatekeeper a one-line reason to stop reading. Before external use of any deck:

  1. Confirm every named individual still holds the stated title (staff turn over; verify against CPD's current staff directory / org chart).
  2. Confirm every park number and historical claim against CPD's own published park history pages, the Chicago Public Library's CPD archive, or a FOIA response — not against this system alone.
  3. Have the Law-adjacent claims (statutory citations, CPD Code sections, "one PAC per park" rule language) reviewed by an actual attorney before they appear in anything addressed to CPD's Law Department, the Board, or in a public records request.
  4. Update the Evidence & Verification Status page the moment something is confirmed or corrected, so every deck downstream stays accurate.

The verification-status vocabulary used throughout this system

StatusMeaning
ConfirmedDirectly stated by a source the planning document treated as authoritative (typically CPD's own published materials) with no material gap identified.
Partially confirmedThe underlying fact is plausible and partly documented, but the full chain of evidence (e.g., the original assignment instrument, an exact date, a Board record) has not been produced.
Pending verificationIdentified as an open question in the planning document itself — explicitly not yet answered, and deliberately not guessed at.
Not applicableContextual or structural information (e.g., "no dedicated deck") rather than a factual claim.

This vocabulary is applied consistently on the Precedent Library and Evidence & Verification Status pages, and every individual deck's factual claims should be read against those two pages before being treated as settled.

What this system deliberately does NOT include

  • Dated photographic evidence of neglect — the planning document calls for a "Conditions and Neglect Dossier" with dated photographs and documented work orders. That evidence has not been collected yet; see the Evidence & Verification Status page for the collection plan.
  • Representation Gap Analysis — completed 2026-08-08: a document-by-document review of all 38 publicly posted JPAC agendas/minutes (Nov 2022–Jul 2026) comparing attention given to 63rd Street Beach against its scale and needs. Verdict: structural underrepresentation, not neglect — see the full analysis, including the counter-evidence, before citing it.
  • FOIA responses — the records requests this system references (see Evidence & Verification Status) have not yet been filed or answered as of this system's creation.
  • New legal analysis — the Law Department deck poses the questions the campaign needs answered; it does not attempt to answer them. That is Law's job, not this system's.
