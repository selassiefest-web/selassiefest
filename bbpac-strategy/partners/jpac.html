Section 1 of 7

Two Strong Voices, One Historic Jackson Park Landscape

Jackson Park Advisory Council has represented the full 551.52-acre landscape of Jackson Park for years — harbors, golf, the Museum-area grounds, Wooded Island, the Japanese Garden, playing fields, natural areas, and 63rd Street Beach among them. JPAC's published mission is to represent all Jackson Park users, and JPAC's own record at the beach is real: JPAC has weighed in on parking changes at 63rd Street Beach and has sponsored and promoted beach cleanups there. This is not a conversation that starts from the premise that JPAC has been absent from the beach. It starts from a much narrower question: as 63rd Street Beach's own operational identity has grown more distinct and more complex, is a single general council for an entire 551-acre park still the right structure to carry the full weight of that one facility's needs — alongside everything else JPAC is already responsible for?

What JPAC's own materials already say

JPAC's own website acknowledges that "63rd Street Beach and Beachhouse is managed separately as a Special Event Venue." We start from that acknowledgment, not around it.

Section 2 of 7

Why This Conversation Is Happening Now

This is a scale-and-specialization question, not a performance critique. 63rd Street Beach carries a distinct 1919 Beach House — built as CPD describes it, in response to South Side residents demanding more beaches — a designated swimming beach and distance swimming area, lifeguard operations, concessions, restrooms and showers, a water play feature, a non-motorized boat launch, parking and ADA beach access, and roughly 12 acres of specialized dune and shoreline habitat used by more than 200 bird species, including threatened and endangered species sightings. It also carries something no boundary map fully captures: the Bongo Beach drumming tradition and the cultural identity that has grown up around it. Layered on top of general park-wide advisory work, this is a genuinely large, specialized portfolio — aquatics, historic preservation, environmental stewardship, accessibility, safety, and cultural programming, all inside one facility that is already administered separately from the rest of the park as a Special Event Venue. Jackson Park's own boundaries have shifted before — the park's administrative lineage itself begins with a subdivision of the historic South Park in 1880–81 (see the South Park subdivision) — so a conversation about whether one part of a very large park might be organized differently is a conversation Jackson Park has had before, in a different form.

The frame we are asking JPAC to consider

Not "is JPAC doing its job," but "is one general advisory council, however capable, structurally sized to carry the specialized needs of a major, independently operated historic beach complex, on top of everything else it already carries across 550+ acres?"

Section 3 of 7

What BBPAC Would Represent, Precisely

The proposed BBPAC jurisdiction is deliberately specific, not a claim on Jackson Park at large. It would cover: the swimming beach and distance swimming area, the historic Beach House, courtyards and promenade, the dune/natural area (the roughly 12 acres of shoreline habitat), beach parking and access points, adjacent green space tied directly to beach use, the non-motorized boat launch, and the other public facilities that sit inside the Special Event Venue boundary JPAC's own site already describes as managed separately. A formal boundary map is part of the recognition materials this campaign is assembling, so that "what BBPAC represents" is a defined line on a map, not a vague expansion of scope.

The point of drawing the line this tightly

A precise boundary is what lets both councils know exactly where one council's responsibility ends and the other's continues — that precision is for JPAC's protection as much as BBPAC's.

Section 4 of 7

What JPAC Would Continue to Represent, Precisely

Nothing about this proposal touches JPAC's standing over the rest of Jackson Park. JPAC would continue to represent all of Jackson Park's other facilities and acreage outside the defined beach boundary: the harbors, golf operations, the Museum-area resources, Wooded Island, the Japanese Garden, playing fields, the park's broader natural areas, and every other piece of the park's 550+ acres and its programming, advocacy, and community-engagement work across all of it. JPAC's mission of representing Jackson Park users continues exactly as it is today for the overwhelming majority of the park's footprint and facilities.

What does not change

JPAC's registration, its standing with CPD, its meetings, its committees, and its advocacy for the rest of Jackson Park continue unchanged. This proposal asks for a defined carve-out at one facility — it does not ask JPAC to give up anything it currently holds elsewhere in the park.

Section 5 of 7

What Remains Shared, and a Proposed Coordination Model

63rd Street Beach does not exist in isolation from the rest of Jackson Park — visitors, traffic, parking overflow, environmental systems, and advocacy for the park as a whole all cross the boundary in both directions. We are proposing that BBPAC and JPAC coordinate directly and permanently on exactly those shared-boundary issues, rather than leaving CPD, the Alderman's office, or the community to sort out two uncoordinated voices. Concretely, that means: joint meetings between the two councils on any issue that touches the shared boundary (parking and access routes, environmental and shoreline issues that don't stop at a property line, and any capital or programming question that affects both areas); coordinated advocacy to CPD, the Alderman, and the Park District Board on matters affecting all of Jackson Park, including the beach; and a standing commitment that BBPAC recognition would not diminish JPAC's registration, its voice, or its standing with CPD in any way.

The ask embedded in this section

We are asking JPAC to help design this coordination model with us — not to have it designed for JPAC. The specifics of joint-meeting cadence, notice, and shared-issue protocols are exactly the kind of detail we want JPAC's leadership at the table for.

Section 6 of 7

An Honest Note: The Representation Gap Analysis

In fairness to JPAC, we want to be direct about something we have not yet done. Part of this campaign's planned documentation is a formal Representation Gap Analysis — a structured review of JPAC's own recent agendas, minutes, financial reports, committees, and activities, measured against the scale and specialized needs of 63rd Street Beach. That analysis is planned. It is not complete. We are not presenting conclusions about JPAC's record to CPD, to the Alderman, or to anyone else without it, and we are not asking JPAC to accept a characterization of its own beach-related work that hasn't been documented and shared first.

Our commitment on this specific point

When the Representation Gap Analysis is complete, we will share it with JPAC directly, before it goes anywhere else. Based on what we already know of JPAC's record — real parking and cleanup involvement at the beach — the likely finding is structural underrepresentation given the scale of the task, not an absence of representation. We are not going to assert that finding as fact until the analysis itself exists, and JPAC will see it alongside everyone else, not after the fact.

Section 7 of 7

What We Are Asking JPAC For

We are asking for one of three things, in this order of preference, and we want to be explicit that each step down this list is still a meaningful and welcome outcome:

First choice — support. JPAC's endorsement of the proposition that 63rd Street Beach would benefit from dedicated, facility-specific community advisory representation, coordinated with JPAC as described above.

Second choice — neutrality or no objection. If JPAC is not prepared to endorse the proposal, a position of neutrality, or a decision not to formally object to CPD reviewing the question, would still move this conversation forward in good faith.

Third choice — a documented opportunity to comment. At minimum, we are asking that JPAC have a clear, documented chance to weigh in on the proposal as it moves through CPD's process, so that JPAC's perspective is part of the record regardless of where JPAC ultimately lands.

What we are not asking, and not assuming

We are not treating JPAC's consent as a legal requirement for CPD to consider this petition — unless and until CPD itself tells us otherwise. Authority over park classification rests with CPD's administrative process and, where required, the Park District Board. This deck is an invitation to a direct conversation between two councils that both care about Jackson Park — it is not a decision that has already been made without JPAC's input, and nothing here is presented to JPAC as a fait accompli.

Is this an attempt to reduce JPAC's authority?

No. The proposal defines a specific boundary around 63rd Street Beach and asks that a dedicated council represent that boundary alongside JPAC's continued representation of the rest of Jackson Park's 550+ acres and facilities. JPAC's registration, standing with CPD, and advocacy for everything outside that boundary are not affected. The coordination model in this proposal is built specifically so JPAC's voice on shared-boundary issues is preserved, not reduced.

Why weren't we consulted before this campaign began?

Some of the early work behind this campaign — research into CPD's PAC rules, park classification history, and comparable precedents — happened before formal outreach to JPAC began. This presentation is intended to be part of that outreach: an honest, complete case shared directly with JPAC, at the start of a real conversation, rather than a partial idea. We would rather bring JPAC a fully worked-through proposal to react to than an early, half-formed one — but we recognize that means this is JPAC's first real look at it, and we want JPAC's input to genuinely shape what happens next, including the coordination model itself.

What happens to the beach cleanup and parking initiatives JPAC already runs?

Nothing in this proposal ends or takes over JPAC's existing beach cleanup sponsorship or its past work on parking changes at 63rd Street Beach. Those are real, valued contributions. If BBPAC is recognized, the proposed coordination model — joint meetings on shared-boundary issues like parking and access — is specifically meant to build on that existing work together, not replace it unilaterally.

Doesn't JPAC's mission already cover this — representing all Jackson Park users?

Yes, and we take that mission seriously; it's part of why this conversation is framed as a scale question rather than a mission question. The argument isn't that JPAC's mission excludes the beach — it's that one general council covering a 551-acre park, however committed to that mission, may not be structurally sized to carry the full specialized weight of one major, independently operated facility on top of everything else across the rest of the park. That's a capacity-and-structure question, not a claim that JPAC's mission is wrong or that JPAC has ignored the beach.

Can JPAC block this?

The decision about whether 63rd Street Beach can have its own distinct classification or PAC representation rests with the Chicago Park District's administrative process and, where legally required, the Park District Board — not with JPAC's consent as such. We are not treating JPAC's agreement as a legal precondition unless CPD tells us it is. That said, we are seeking JPAC's support, or at minimum its neutrality or a documented chance to comment, because we believe this should be worked out between the two councils in good faith, not decided around JPAC.

What if JPAC says no?

We hope that isn't where this lands, which is why we're having this conversation directly and early, and why support is only our first choice among three acceptable outcomes. If JPAC ultimately objects, we will still bring the underlying question to CPD, because the classification decision is CPD's to make — but we would much rather reach CPD alongside a documented record of good-faith conversation with JPAC, including JPAC's own comments and concerns, than without one. Any objection JPAC raises would be part of that record, not something we would leave out.
