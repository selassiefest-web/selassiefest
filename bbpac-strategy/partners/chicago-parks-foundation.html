Section 1 of 5

You Already Do This — Just Not With Us Yet

Chicago Parks Foundation currently offers fiscal sponsorship to Park Advisory Councils and other park partners across the city, giving grassroots park groups a path to receive grants and donations, manage funds, and operate with nonprofit infrastructure before or instead of incorporating on their own. That existing role — not any request to intervene in a park-governance dispute — is the entire reason we wanted to introduce the 63rd Street Bongo Beach effort to you now, well before we would ever ask you to act on anything.

What This Conversation Is Not

This outreach is intentionally not about whether 63rd Street Beach should be recognized as its own distinct park or PAC jurisdiction separate from Jackson Park No. 19 — the classification question we call "Gate 0." We are not asking Chicago Parks Foundation to weigh in on that fight, to take a position on Jackson Park's boundaries, or to comment on the relationship between a future BBPAC and the Jackson Park Advisory Council. That determination belongs with the Chicago Park District, and we are approaching it through CPD's own administrative channels, not through park partners like you.

Section 2 of 5

What Becomes Relevant After Recognition

If — and only if — 63rd Street Beach is formally recognized as its own distinct park or PAC jurisdiction and a Bongo Beach PAC is formed through CPD's ordinary process, that new PAC will face the same practical question every other PAC faces: how to receive donations, manage funds responsibly, and build fundraising capacity for stewardship, cultural programming, and capital needs. That is precisely the gap Chicago Parks Foundation's fiscal-sponsorship model exists to fill for PACs and other park partners today. We expect a future BBPAC to need the same kind of infrastructure other PACs already rely on through you.

What We're Asking Today

An exploratory conversation about what a future fiscal-sponsorship relationship with Chicago Parks Foundation could look like once BBPAC is formally recognized and formed — nothing more. We are not asking for a sponsorship agreement, a funding commitment, or any decision right now. We simply want you to know this effort exists, to understand how your fiscal-sponsorship model works, and to have an open door for that conversation when the timing is right.

Section 3 of 5

Why We're Coming to You Now, Even Though There's Nothing to Decide

We would rather introduce this campaign to Chicago Parks Foundation early and informally than show up asking for a fiscal-sponsorship decision the moment recognition is won. Meeting now, while there is no ask attached, lets you get oriented to the campaign on your own timeline and lets us understand your process well before we would ever need to use it.

Section 4 of 5

Timeline Expectations

This conversation is intentionally sequenced for after Gate 0 is resolved, not alongside it. Gate 0 — the petition for CPD to recognize 63rd Street Beach as a distinct park or PAC jurisdiction — is still working through the Chicago Park District's internal review, and its outcome and timing are not yet known. Chicago Parks Foundation should not expect any near-term action, follow-up, or request from us until that classification question is settled. You can track where the campaign currently stands on our evidence and status page.

Why are you reaching out now if this isn't an active ask?

We would rather build this relationship early and without pressure than arrive later needing a fast decision. Introducing the campaign now, with nothing on the table to approve, gives Chicago Parks Foundation time to understand what BBPAC is before there's ever a request attached to it.

What would fiscal sponsorship actually involve?

We don't have a specific proposal to offer yet, and we're not asking you to design one today. Our understanding is that Chicago Parks Foundation already provides fiscal sponsorship to other PACs and park partners, and this conversation is meant to explore, in general terms, how that model works and whether it could someday apply to a formally recognized BBPAC.

What if Gate 0 doesn't succeed?

Then there is no BBPAC to sponsor, and this conversation simply doesn't proceed to the next stage. We're not asking Chicago Parks Foundation to predict, influence, or hedge against that outcome — that determination rests entirely with the Chicago Park District, and we'll keep you informed of where things stand.

Do you need anything from us before Gate 0 is resolved?

No. We're not requesting a meeting commitment, a funding decision, or any formal step from Chicago Parks Foundation at this stage. If you'd like to stay loosely informed as the campaign progresses, we're glad to keep you posted; otherwise, we'll plan to reach back out once recognition is decided.
