Section 1 of 6

With Appreciation for What You've Built Since 1986

The South Shore Cultural Center Advisory Council has been advising the Chicago Park District on the operations of the Cultural Center since 1986 — nearly forty years of promoting maintenance and beautification of the grounds and developing the cultural, recreational, and educational activities that make South Shore Cultural Center one of the most beloved lakefront institutions on the South Side. Today your Council runs standing committees, produces and supports programming, raises funds, organizes volunteers, holds public meetings, advocates for the site, and keeps your community engaged in its stewardship. That is a genuinely difficult, sustained institutional achievement, and the organizing committee behind the 63rd Street Bongo Beach PAC (BBPAC) effort wanted to say so directly before asking you for anything.

Section 2 of 6

A Careful Clarification, Up Front

We want to be precise about why we're here, because it would be easy to blur this and we don't want to. South Shore Beach sits within South Shore Cultural Center Park (Park #429). As best as our research has been able to establish, there is no documented administrative connection between Park #429 and Jackson Park (Park #19) — we have found no evidence that South Shore's park was ever part of Jackson Park, and we are not asserting otherwise. That means South Shore is not a precedent for the specific administrative question BBPAC is raising with the Park District about splitting or reclassifying a single existing park number. We've written out the full, careful version of that distinction on our Precedent Library page at ../precedents.html#south-shore-comparison, and we'd rather you read that than take our word for it.

Section 3 of 6

What South Shore Actually Proves — and Why It Matters to Us

What your Council does prove, and prove convincingly, is something different and — to us — just as valuable: that a dedicated, specialized advisory council built around one distinctive South Side lakefront site can succeed for decades. Cultural programming, fundraising, preservation advocacy, active committees, and real community participation are not abstractions for you; they are what your Council does every year. That is the model BBPAC hopes to follow for 63rd Street Beach: not a claim about how Jackson Park's boundaries should be drawn, but a working example of what focused, site-specific advisory representation can accomplish when a community organizes around it seriously.

Section 4 of 6

Why We're Reaching Out to You

We are approaching your Council to learn from your institutional experience — how you structured your committees, how you built fundraising and programming capacity, how you sustained public engagement over nearly forty years, and how you've worked with the Park District as a specialized-site advisory body. We are not asking South Shore to take a position on Jackson Park's internal classification, on the relationship between 63rd Street Beach and Jackson Park's existing advisory council, or on any dispute that is specific to Jackson Park. Your Council's expertise is valuable to us precisely because it comes from your own site and your own history, not because it settles a question about a different park.

Section 5 of 6

What We're Specifically Asking

Our request to the South Shore Cultural Center Advisory Council is narrow and specific, and we'd rather ask it plainly than leave it vague.

Our Ask

Will you share your institutional experience and, if appropriate, support the proposition that specialized South Side lakefront assets benefit from dedicated community advisory representation?

Section 6 of 6

What We Are Not Asking

To be equally clear about the boundary on the other side: South Shore is not being asked to weigh in on Park #19's boundaries, on Gate 0 (the Chicago Park District's threshold question of whether 63rd Street Beach can be recognized as a distinct park or PAC jurisdiction), or on any Park District classification dispute. We are not asking your Council to comment on Jackson Park, on the Jackson Park Advisory Council, or on any internal Park District determination about how park numbers are assigned or divided. Those are questions for Jackson Park's stakeholders and for the Park District to resolve — not questions we would ask South Shore to adjudicate.

Are you asking us to take a side in a dispute with JPAC?

No. This outreach has nothing to do with the Jackson Park Advisory Council or any dispute involving them. We are not asking South Shore to comment on JPAC, on Jackson Park's boundaries, or on how the Park District should resolve that separate conversation. Our interest is in your Council's own decades of experience running a specialized lakefront-site PAC, full stop.

What would we actually be endorsing?

At most, a general proposition: that specialized South Side lakefront assets — like a major beach complex with its own historic building, environmental stewardship needs, and cultural identity — can benefit from dedicated community advisory representation, the way South Shore Cultural Center has for nearly forty years. You would not be endorsing any specific outcome for Jackson Park, any particular boundary, or any position on how the Chicago Park District should classify Park #19. And sharing your experience without formally endorsing anything is a completely acceptable response to our ask.

Is South Shore being used as evidence that the Park District splits parks?

No, and we've tried to be explicit about that. South Shore Beach is part of South Shore Cultural Center Park (#429), and we have no evidence that #429 was ever part of Jackson Park or any other park — it is not a "park was divided" precedent. We rely on separate, distinct precedents for that administrative question, documented on our Precedent Library page. South Shore's relevance to us is as a comparator — a successful model of dedicated advisory representation — not as a classification precedent.

How would our name or story be used?

Only in the way we've described here: as a comparative case study showing what a dedicated, specialized lakefront PAC can accomplish over time — cultural programming, fundraising, preservation advocacy, and sustained community participation. We would not present your Council as having taken a position on Jackson Park's internal classification or on any dispute specific to that park, and we would welcome your review of any material that references South Shore before it's shared more broadly.

What do you actually want from a meeting or conversation with us?

Practically, we'd value hearing how your Council organized its committees, built fundraising and programming capacity, and sustained public engagement and Park District cooperation over the years — the operational lessons of running a successful site-specific PAC. If, after hearing our full explanation, you're comfortable supporting the general proposition described in our ask, that would mean a great deal to us. If you'd rather just share experience without taking any position, that is entirely fine and appreciated on its own.
