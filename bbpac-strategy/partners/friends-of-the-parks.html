Section 1 of 6

Friends of the Parks and the PAC Tradition

Friends of the Parks has long been a voice for the idea that Chicago's park advisory councils are not administrative formalities — they are neighborhood-led community voices, rooted in the people who actually use a park day to day. The 63rd Street Bongo Beach PAC (BBPAC) organizing committee comes to Friends of the Parks from inside that same tradition. We are not asking you to accept a finished conclusion; we are asking you to look, as an independent parks-policy organization, at a governance question we believe the Chicago Park District (CPD) has not yet had to squarely answer: what happens when a single, general park-wide advisory council is asked to represent a facility as large, specialized, and operationally distinct as 63rd Street Beach.

Section 2 of 6

The Governance and Precedent Case, in Brief

63rd Street Beach is presently classified as part of Jackson Park (Park No. 19), and CPD's February 2025 PAC Guidelines recognize only one registered PAC per park — which is why an ordinary Letter of Intent for a BBPAC can be turned away in a single sentence under the current framing. We have compiled a fuller precedent record, organized in two buckets, at our Precedent Library. Taken together, the precedents in Bucket A (park subdivisions, reclassifications, boundary changes, and consolidations CPD and its predecessor agency have carried out over the decades) and Bucket B (numbered CPD beach parks, from the small Lane Beach Park #1265 on up, that exist independently of a larger neighboring park) point to one overall conclusion we ask you to weigh: CPD's park-identity system has never been immutable, and a beach is not automatically absorbed into whatever larger park happens to surround it. We hold each individual precedent to the confirmation status shown on that page — some are fully documented, some are partially confirmed, and at least one open research question remains unresolved — and we are not asking Friends of the Parks to treat any of them as settled beyond that status.

Section 3 of 6

The Community-Representation Case

Our argument is not that the Jackson Park Advisory Council (JPAC) has failed 63rd Street Beach. JPAC's mission covers all Jackson Park users, and its own record shows engagement with beach matters. Our argument is structural: Jackson Park is 551.52 acres, encompassing harbors, golf, museum-area resources, a wooded island, a Japanese garden, playing fields, natural areas, and a major historic beach complex with its own aquatics, cultural, environmental, historic-preservation, accessibility, and programming responsibilities — even JPAC's own website describes 63rd Street Beach and Beachhouse as "managed separately as a Special Event Venue." One general council for a park of that scale and variety may simply be too broad an instrument to give a facility like this the dedicated, specialized attention it needs — independent of how well any individual council performs. We have approached JPAC directly, not adversarially, with a coordination-first presentation asking them to help define what BBPAC would represent, what JPAC continues to represent, and how the two councils would work together going forward; you can see that approach at our JPAC partner page.

Section 4 of 6

Why This Matters Beyond One Beach

We want to be direct with you about the broader stakes, because we think an honest framing is the only one worth bringing to a policy-credible organization. If CPD establishes a clear administrative pathway by which a specialized sub-area of a very large park — a major beach, a cultural center, another independently operated complex — can obtain dedicated PAC representation, that pathway will not stay confined to 63rd Street Beach. Chicago has other large regional parks containing specialized sites that could reasonably ask the same question we are asking. We are not asking Friends of the Parks to pre-approve how that pathway should apply elsewhere, and we are not claiming to know how CPD would or should decide those future cases. We are asking you to consider this one governance question on its own facts, with full awareness that the administrative answer CPD gives here will not exist in a vacuum.

Section 5 of 6

What We're Specifically Asking

We are asking Friends of the Parks to review the governance and precedent case summarized above and, if you find it credible, to endorse the underlying proposition: that CPD should determine whether 63rd Street Beach is eligible for a defined administrative status permitting dedicated PAC representation, using whichever of the three Gate 0 outcomes CPD's own authority supports. Your organization's independence and its standing as an advocate for community-led park governance would lend this campaign a form of parks-policy credibility that no organizing committee can provide for itself.

The Ask

Review the governance/precedent case and, if you agree it has merit, lend Friends of the Parks' independent voice in support of asking CPD to determine BBPAC's eligibility for dedicated PAC representation.

Section 6 of 6

What We're NOT Asking

We are not asking Friends of the Parks to take a position for or against JPAC, whose mission and record we respect and whose coordination we are separately pursuing. We are also not asking you to independently verify every historical claim in this campaign before you engage with it. Some elements of our case — precedent years, prior park-number history, and other archival details — are still being confirmed through an active records-request and research process, and we track exactly what is confirmed, partially confirmed, or still pending at our Evidence Status page. We think it is more honest to show you that work in progress than to present it as finished.

Not the Ask

Not a position on JPAC, and not a request that you independently re-verify our historical or archival research — that verification work is ongoing and documented separately.

What exactly would our endorsement say?

We are asking for an endorsement of the governance question, not a finished conclusion: that CPD should formally determine whether 63rd Street Beach is eligible for a defined administrative status permitting dedicated PAC representation. We would work with you on exact language so it reflects only what Friends of the Parks is comfortable affirming.

Have you verified the precedent claims independently?

Not all of them, and we say so openly. Each precedent in our Precedent Library carries its own confirmed, partially confirmed, or pending status, and a targeted records-request campaign is still underway to resolve the open questions — including the single highest-priority open item, which concerns a post-1934 park-number split we have not yet confirmed. We ask you to weigh each precedent at the confidence level it currently carries, not as settled fact.

Does this set a precedent you'd want applied elsewhere in the city?

We think it might, and we would rather say that plainly than have it surface later. If CPD establishes an administrative pathway for specialized sub-areas of large parks to gain dedicated representation, other Chicago neighborhoods with similar large-park situations could reasonably invoke it. We are not asking you to decide in advance how that should play out elsewhere — only to evaluate this case on its own facts.

Are you asking us to take a side against JPAC?

No. Our case is built on structural underrepresentation — a 551-acre park with one general council — not on any claim that JPAC has failed. We are pursuing a coordination-first approach with JPAC directly, and we are not asking Friends of the Parks to weigh in on that relationship one way or the other.

What if CPD ultimately says no?

The Gate 0 petition itself is written to seek a written CPD determination under one of three possible outcomes, including a policy-change request if current rules permit neither distinct park status nor a formal exception. An endorsement from Friends of the Parks supports asking CPD to make that determination on the merits — it does not commit you, or us, to a particular outcome CPD might reach.
