Section 1 of 10

Overview: Who This Deck Is For, and Why

This deck serves four roles that CPD's own internal structure treats as one operational family around 63rd Street Beach: Chief Operating Officer Patrick J. Levar (executive integrator across Planning & Development, Facility Management, Environmental Services, Natural Resources, and Security), Community Recreation South Region Director James Sifuentes (South Region lists Jackson Park among its major properties), Jackson Park Supervisor/Area Manager Bobbie Beckam, and Park Public Safety (security and coordination with police/emergency agencies). None of these four offices decides whether 63rd Street Beach becomes a distinct PAC jurisdiction. All four run, staff, secure, or supervise the facility day to day — which makes them the people best positioned to confirm or refute the operational premise the campaign is built on: that the beach already functions as a specialized complex, not an ordinary corner of a 551-acre park.

They are grouped in one deck, in modules, because the ask to each of them is a variant of the same question — is this operationally distinct? — rather than four unrelated asks. The Jackson Park Supervisor module comes first because CPD's own PAC rules direct citizens to start there.

Section 2 of 10

The Operational-Complexity Case

63rd Street Beach is not a playground inside Jackson Park's 551.52 acres — it is a multi-function complex with its own distinct list of facilities and operations:

  • a designated swimming beach
  • a distance swimming area
  • lifeguard operations
  • concessions
  • public restrooms and showers
  • a water play feature
  • a non-motorized boat launch
  • parking
  • ADA beach access

The single strongest data point for this section does not come from the campaign — it comes from Jackson Park Advisory Council's own website, which states that "63rd Street Beach and Beachhouse is managed separately as a Special Event Venue." That is JPAC acknowledging, in its own words, that this facility already sits outside ordinary park-wide operations. Everything else in this section is corroborating detail; that sentence is the anchor fact.

JPAC's Own Site

"63rd Street Beach and Beachhouse is managed separately as a Special Event Venue." — cited as the strongest single operational data point in the case; it is JPAC's language, not the campaign's characterization.

Section 3 of 10

Module: Jackson Park Supervisor / Area Manager (Bobbie Beckam)

CPD's own PAC rules direct citizens who want to change or form PAC representation to start with the Park Supervisor and Area Manager. That makes this office the front door for this request, procedurally — not the decision point. This module asks the Supervisor/Area Manager's office to do two things: document current operating conditions at the beach as they actually are today, and elevate the request through the appropriate internal channel, alongside LCA.

The Ask

Review a short, factual presentation covering existing conditions, the representation gap argument, and the proposed boundaries — then document current operations and pass the request forward. This office is not being asked to approve or deny distinct PAC recognition; it does not have that authority, and this deck does not ask it to act as if it does.

Pending verification

No published CPD procedure has yet been located describing exactly how a facility is separated from an existing park number and given a new one — so part of what this office is being asked to elevate is itself a request to identify who holds that authority and what process applies.

Section 4 of 10

Module: Community Recreation — South Region (James Sifuentes)

South Region administers Jackson Park's ground-level recreation operations today, including the beach. This module lays out existing operations, staffing, programming, and community use as they currently run — and proposes a working division of responsibilities: BBPAC's proposed scope (beach stewardship, safety coordination, historic preservation of the Beach House, cultural programming, accessibility, environmental protection, community representation) alongside JPAC's continuing scope over the rest of Jackson Park's 551.52 acres — golf, harbors, the Museum-area resources, Wooded Island, Japanese Garden, playing fields, and natural areas outside the beach's defined footprint.

The Ask

An operational assessment and cooperation — not the classification decision. South Region is asked to confirm how staffing, programming, and community use function today, and to weigh in on whether the proposed division of responsibilities between BBPAC and JPAC's continuing scope is workable from a day-to-day operations standpoint.

The proposed boundary and division of responsibilities is a campaign proposal, not a CPD determination. It should be treated as a starting point for South Region's operational feedback, not a finished plan.

Section 5 of 10

Module: Chief Operating Officer (Patrick J. Levar)

As executive integrator across Planning & Development, Facility Management, Environmental Services, Natural Resources, and Security, the COO's office is positioned to see the whole operational picture at once: aquatics and lifeguard operations, Beach House facility condition, ~12 acres of dune/shoreline habitat stewardship, and security — all converging on one site. The case to this office is the same operational-complexity case in Section 2, but framed at the executive level: a facility this operationally layered is already being run as something more specialized than a standard park amenity, even though it is not yet administratively recognized as one.

The Ask

Is a distinct administrative unit operationally viable, and will Operations support the recommendation?

This is deliberately not framed as a request to rule on PAC eligibility. It is a request for an operational judgment — feasibility and support — that feeds into the broader internal pathway described in Section 7.

Section 6 of 10

Module: Park Public Safety

63rd Street Beach is a major open-water swimming beach with lifeguard operations and a separately managed Special Event Venue — which means it carries public-safety and coordination demands (with police and emergency agencies) that an ordinary park green space does not. This module is deliberately narrow.

The Ask

Validation of the specialized public-safety environment — nothing more. Park Public Safety is not being asked to weigh in on PAC recognition, governance, or classification. The ask is limited to confirming, from a security and emergency-coordination standpoint, that a major open-water swimming beach and special-event venue presents a distinct public-safety profile compared to general park acreage.

Police and OEMC coordination belongs in the beach-safety discussion generally, not in the PAC-recognition decision — Park Public Safety's role here mirrors that same boundary.

Section 7 of 10

What's NOT Being Asked of Operations

This deck is not a request for Operations, Community Recreation, the Jackson Park Supervisor/Area Manager, or Park Public Safety to decide whether 63rd Street Beach becomes a distinct PAC jurisdiction. That classification decision belongs to Planning & Development, the Law Department, the General Superintendent, and — if Board action is legally required — the Board of Commissioners.

Scope Boundary

Operations' role in this campaign is technical validation and cooperation: confirming what already exists operationally, and cooperating with the review process. It is not asked to interpret CPD Code Chapter 7, weigh precedent, or determine what legal instrument or Board action would be required — those questions go to Planning and Law.

The recommended internal pathway places Operations and South Region in a specific sequence: Park Supervisor/Area Manager and LCA first, then Planning, Law, and South Region/Operations together, then COO/executive review, then the General Superintendent, then the appropriate Board route. Operations is a technical-validation stop on that path, not the endpoint.

Section 8 of 10

Current Known Conditions vs. What's Still Being Documented

What is documented and citable now: the operational-complexity list in Section 2, and JPAC's own "Special Event Venue" statement. What is not yet documented: a Conditions and Neglect Dossier — dated photographs, unresolved maintenance problems, sanitation issues, Beach House condition, lighting, restroom conditions, accessibility gaps, safety or programming deficiencies, vendor problems, capital deficiencies, complaints, and work orders.

Explicit Gap — Not Yet Assembled

The Conditions and Neglect Dossier has not yet been assembled. This deck does not assert specific neglect claims, work-order histories, or facility deficiencies because the underlying proof — photographs, complaints, and work-order records — has not yet been gathered. Until that dossier exists, any statement about specific maintenance or condition failures at the beach would be an assertion this source material does not support.

Pending verification

Similarly, a formal Representation Gap Analysis — comparing JPAC's recent agendas, minutes, financial reports, committees, and activities against the scale and needs of the beach — has not yet been completed. The likely finding, per the source material's own framing, is structural underrepresentation, not an absence of any JPAC engagement with the beach; that finding should not be presumed before the analysis is done.

Section 9 of 10

Coordination With JPAC's Existing Operational Relationship to the Beach

JPAC's published mission covers all Jackson Park users, and JPAC's own records show real activity connected to the beach — including discussing parking changes and sponsoring or advertising beach cleanups. This campaign is built around structural underrepresentation, not JPAC failure: Jackson Park is too large, 63rd Street Beach is too specialized, and its community is too distinct for one general park-wide council to provide adequate dedicated governance. That framing is deliberate and should not be departed from in any Operations-facing conversation.

For Operations and South Region specifically, this means the proposed division of responsibilities in Section 4 is a coordination proposal, not a takeover: BBPAC's proposed scope over the beach complex, JPAC's continuing scope over the rest of Jackson Park, and a shared zone where the two councils would coordinate. See the JPAC coordination page for the fuller "Two Strong Voices, One Historic Jackson Park Landscape" framing and the specific outcomes being sought from JPAC (support, neutrality, or a documented opportunity to comment, in that order of preference).

Won't this create duplicate staffing or coordination overhead?

That is exactly the kind of question this deck asks Operations and South Region to answer, not something the campaign is asserting either way. The proposed division of responsibilities in Section 4 is a starting point for that operational feedback — South Region's assessment of staffing and coordination realities is the ask, not a conclusion already reached.

Who handles day-to-day operations during the transition, if recognition is granted?

The source material does not yet answer this. This is a Gate 0 recognition campaign — it has not reached the point of designing a transition plan for daily operations. That level of detail would follow, if and when CPD determines a path toward distinct recognition, and would properly involve South Region and Operations directly.

What changes for Park Public Safety response protocols?

Nothing is being proposed to change yet. The ask to Park Public Safety in this deck is limited to validating that the beach already presents a distinct public-safety environment — it does not request or propose any change to response protocols, chain of command, or police/OEMC coordination arrangements.

Does this deck ask the Jackson Park Supervisor or Area Manager to approve BBPAC?

No. CPD's PAC rules direct citizens to start there, so that office is the procedural front door, but it does not have authority to approve or deny distinct PAC recognition. Its role here is to document current conditions and elevate the request.

Is Operations being asked to take a position on whether JPAC has failed the beach?

No. The campaign's position is structural underrepresentation, not JPAC failure — and even that framing depends on a Representation Gap Analysis that has not yet been completed. Operations and South Region are not being asked to evaluate JPAC's performance.

What happens to concessions, vendor agreements, and special-event permitting in the meantime?

Nothing changes as a result of this deck. Revenue's existing role in special-event permitting and commercial activity at the Beach House continues; this module does not propose altering vendor agreements, concessions, or permitting while Gate 0 recognition is pending.

Why is this deck asking four different roles the same underlying question instead of a separate deck for each?

Because the ask to each of them is a variant of one question — is 63rd Street Beach already operating as a specialized complex? — rather than four unrelated requests. Grouping them lets each role see exactly its own module without needing the entire multi-department campaign record.
