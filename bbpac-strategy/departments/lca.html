Why LCA Is Receiving This First

Director Cuevas, this deck comes to Legislative & Community Affairs before any other CPD department because LCA is the PAC gatekeeper and process owner: the office responsible for working with communities to create Park Advisory Councils and for liaising between PACs, alderpersons, and other government bodies. In the recommended internal pathway that the organizing committee has mapped for this request, LCA sits at the front of the line — alongside the Jackson Park Supervisor/Area Manager — before the case is passed to Planning & Development, Law, and Operations, and eventually to the Chief Operating Officer, the General Superintendent, and, if required, the Board.

Concretely, that means LCA's read of "what pathway exists, and how does a community actually use it" will shape how every other department receives this case. We are not asking LCA to decide the classification question itself — we are asking LCA to tell us, and the rest of CPD, what the official pathway is.

Section 2 of 9

The PAC Problem in One Page

CPD currently lists 63rd Street Beach as being within Jackson Park, Park No. 19 — a 551.52-acre park that already has a registered PAC, the Jackson Park Advisory Council (JPAC). CPD's February 2025 PAC Guidelines are direct on this point:

CPD PAC Guidelines, February 2025

"The Park District recognizes only one (1) registered PAC per park." Where a park already has a PAC, CPD's standard instruction is to join the existing PAC.

That single sentence is why an ordinary Letter of Intent for a "63rd Street Bongo Beach PAC" can be rejected without any further discussion: 63rd Street Beach is in Jackson Park; Jackson Park already has a PAC. Gate 0 of this campaign is therefore not "may we please form a PAC?" It is a formal petition for distinct recognition of 63rd Street Beach as its own PAC jurisdiction — a classification question, not a formation question.

CPD Code Chapter 7 gives the General Superintendent authority to classify park property, with designations transmitted to the Board for approval, amendment, or rejection. Jackson Park's own administrative lineage began as a subdivision of a larger unified holding; see South Park's 1880–81 division into Jackson Park, Washington Park, and the Midway Plaisance. No published CPD procedure has yet been located describing how a community separates a facility from an existing park number and obtains a new one Pending verification — which is precisely the first written question this campaign needs LCA's help answering.

Section 3 of 9

What BBPAC Is Proposing to Represent

BBPAC's proposed jurisdiction is 63rd Street Beach as an operationally distinct complex, not a generic slice of Jackson Park. It includes: the designated swimming beach and distance swimming area; the historic 1919 Beach House and its courtyards and promenade; lifeguard operations; concessions; public restrooms and showers; a water play feature; a non-motorized boat launch; parking and ADA beach access; and approximately 12 acres of native dune and shoreline habitat supporting more than 200 bird species, including threatened and endangered species sightings. Even JPAC's own published materials acknowledge that "63rd Street Beach and Beachhouse is managed separately as a Special Event Venue."

For scale context, a far smaller and simpler beach already carries its own independent CPD park number: see Lane (George) Beach Park #1265, at 3.09 acres.

Section 5 of 9

The Three Requested Gate 0 Outcomes

The formal petition seeks one of three outcomes, in descending order of preference, and LCA's involvement matters differently to each:

Outcome A (preferred) — Distinct park status. CPD recognizes/designates 63rd Street Beach, its Beach House, and associated defined grounds as a distinct park unit for PAC purposes. LCA's role: confirm whether this is a pathway that has ever been used for a facility-specific PAC and, if so, what it requires of the community.

Outcome B — Formal exception/interpretation. CPD determines that a sufficiently distinct major facility within a very large park may have its own registered facility-specific PAC notwithstanding the existing Jackson Park PAC. LCA's role: identify whether this interpretation exists in current guidance or would need to be created.

Outcome C — Policy change. If existing policy permits neither A nor B, CPD is asked to amend the policy to accommodate unique large-park facilities (major beaches, cultural centers, other independently operated complexes). LCA's role: identify what process a policy amendment of this kind would require, and who else needs to be at the table.

For context on what a dedicated lakefront PAC can accomplish once recognized, the campaign is drawing a comparison — not a classification precedent — to the South Shore Cultural Center Advisory Council; see South Shore Beach as a facility inside South Shore Cultural Center Park.

Section 6 of 9

Where LCA Fits in the Internal CPD Pathway

The recommended internal pathway for this request runs: Park Supervisor/Area Manager → LCA → Planning & Development, Law, and South Region/Operations → Chief Operating Officer → General Superintendent → appropriate Board route, with Board action taken only if legally required. This runs alongside, not instead of, the external political lane (Alderman Yancy, community stakeholders, civic allies).

One explicit exclusion

The PAC Governance Committee is not the decision body for this request. CPD's published materials describe that committee principally in connection with PAC complaints and Code of Conduct disputes, not with whether a new PAC or PAC jurisdiction can be formed. Formation and classification questions are directed to the Park Supervisor, Area Manager, and LCA — which is why this deck is addressed to Director Cuevas rather than to that committee.

Section 7 of 9

What We Are Specifically Asking LCA to Do

Two concrete requests:

1. Identify the official pathway by which 63rd Street Beach — or a facility like it — can become eligible for its own registered PAC, whether that runs through Outcome A, B, or C above, and what CPD office or officer actually holds that authority.

2. Coordinate the internal CPD review across the departments that need to weigh in on a request of this kind — principally Planning & Development (the boundary/classification mechanism), Law (legal authority and required instrument), and Operations/South Region (operational viability) — so that BBPAC is not left to approach each department cold, and so that CPD's eventual answer reflects a coordinated institutional position rather than a series of disconnected departmental opinions.

The ask, stated plainly

Direct us to the correct procedure, and help us get a coordinated, written answer from CPD on whether — and how — 63rd Street Beach can become its own recognized PAC jurisdiction.

Section 8 of 9

Coordination with JPAC

This request is deliberately framed as "Two Strong Voices, One Historic Jackson Park Landscape," not as a conflict with or a critique of JPAC. JPAC's published mission represents all Jackson Park users, and its own records show engagement with 63rd Street Beach matters (parking changes, beach cleanups). The case for BBPAC rests on structural underrepresentation — that one general advisory council for a 551-acre regional park cannot adequately govern a major, independently operated historic beach complex with its own aquatics, cultural, environmental, historic, accessibility, and safety responsibilities — not on any claim that JPAC has failed.

A tailored presentation for JPAC is being prepared separately; see the JPAC coordination deck. That presentation will show precisely what BBPAC would represent, what JPAC continues to represent, what remains shared, and how the two councils would coordinate going forward. The outcomes being sought from JPAC, in descending order, are: support → neutrality/no objection → a documented opportunity to comment. JPAC's consent is not being treated as a requirement for CPD's decision unless CPD states that it legally is.

Next Steps & What LCA Should Expect to Receive

The next document LCA should expect from this campaign is a Formal Request for Written CPD Determination, asking CPD to answer, in writing, six questions covering eligibility, the reason for any ineligibility, the officer or body with authority to designate a distinct park unit, the procedure required, which offices can approve a facility-specific PAC, and — if current policy prohibits it — what process is required to seek a policy amendment. The full six-question text lives in the master presentation; see the Global Presentation.

Alongside that written request, a targeted FOIA/public-records campaign is being prepared to obtain the Jackson Park No. 19 boundary map, historical maps of the beach, any records of a separate park/property number ever existing for the Beach House, and CPD's own internal interpretation of the one-PAC-per-park rule, among other records. This records work is being run separately from LCA's coordination role and is noted here only for context.
