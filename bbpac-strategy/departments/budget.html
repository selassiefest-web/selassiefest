Section 1 of 6

Why Budget & Management, and Why Now

This briefing exists because the campaign's internal routing places Budget & Management deliberately downstream. Per the Stakeholder & Authority Routing table, Budget's role is described as becoming important "once an administrative structure is contemplated" — not before. Gate 0, the question actually in front of CPD right now, is a classification question: whether 63rd Street Beach can be recognized as a distinct park unit (Outcome A), a facility-specific PAC exception within Jackson Park (Outcome B), or requires a policy change (Outcome C). That determination belongs to the Park Supervisor/Area Manager, LCA, Planning & Development, and Law.

Budget & Management is being briefed early only so that this office has visibility into the case and can begin thinking about what it would need to evaluate — not because a fiscal decision is being requested today. No fiscal ask is being made at Gate 0. This deck is a heads-up and a scoping conversation, not a request for funding, a staffing request, or a budget amendment.

Section 2 of 6

What a PAC Ordinarily Costs CPD

Park Advisory Councils, including Jackson Park's existing JPAC, are volunteer community advisory bodies. They are not CPD staff positions, and PAC officers and members are not CPD employees. On that basis, the baseline working assumption in this campaign is that recognizing a new PAC is not, by itself, equivalent to creating a new paid position or a new recurring line item — it is closer to registering an additional volunteer body that CPD's existing PAC-support infrastructure (principally LCA) already interacts with for every registered PAC.

Flag for independent verification

Pending verification The source material for this campaign does not contain CPD's actual internal cost data for supporting a PAC — staff hours, materials, meeting-space costs, or any other component of what LCA and other offices actually spend per registered PAC. Budget & Management should independently confirm the "volunteer body, not new staff" characterization against CPD's real PAC-support cost structure rather than accept it as given. This deck states it as the ordinary-case pattern, not as a verified cost figure.

Section 3 of 6

Possible Cost Drivers to Evaluate

The following are candidate items for Budget to model once a classification path is chosen — they are not claimed costs, and no dollar figures are asserted anywhere in this deck or in the underlying source material.

Administrative/staffing implications of a new distinct designation. If CPD designates 63rd Street Beach as a distinct park unit or administrative park unit (Outcome A) rather than a facility-specific exception within Jackson Park (Outcome B), that could carry different administrative overhead than an exception carries. No published CPD procedure describing how a facility separates from an existing park number has been found in this campaign's research, so the actual mechanism — and therefore its administrative cost, if any — is unknown pending Planning & Development's and Law's determination.

GIS/property-record updates. Any new or amended park-number boundary, or any new administrative unit distinct from Park No. 19, would presumably require updates to CPD's property/GIS records. Budget should ask Planning & Development what that update process typically involves before assuming it is negligible or significant.

Incremental LCA liaison time. LCA already liaises with every registered PAC. Adding one additional registered body (whether structured as Outcome A, B, or a new facility-specific PAC) would add some incremental liaison time on top of LCA's existing per-PAC workload. Whether that increment is material at CPD's scale is a question for Budget to model, not a conclusion this campaign can supply.

Section 4 of 6

Possible Cost Avoidances/Benefits to Weigh

These are factors Budget may want to weigh alongside the cost drivers above — presented as considerations, not as promised outcomes or quantified savings.

Capital-planning input. A dedicated advisory body focused specifically on a major, operationally complex beach complex (aquatics, historic Beach House, dune/shoreline habitat, cultural programming) could plausibly generate more specific, better-targeted capital-planning input for that complex than one general advisory council covering an entire 551-acre park. Whether that translates into any measurable planning efficiency or cost avoidance is not established in the source material and should not be asserted as fact.

Community fundraising capacity. The source material notes that the Chicago Parks Foundation currently offers fiscal sponsorship to PACs and other park partners — a resource that becomes relevant to what BBPAC could raise and administer after recognition, not to Gate 0 itself. See the Chicago Parks Foundation partner page for the post-recognition fiscal-sponsorship angle. This is noted here only as a factor that could offset some of BBPAC's own operating needs; it does not offset or substitute for any CPD-side administrative cost Budget identifies above.

Section 5 of 6

The Specific Ask of Budget & Management

What we are asking

Once Planning & Development and Law identify which of Outcomes A, B, or C is actually being pursued for 63rd Street Beach, we are asking Budget & Management to determine whether separate administrative recognition carries material fiscal consequences for CPD — and, if so, to quantify and document them using CPD's own cost data. We are not asking Budget to select among Outcomes A/B/C, and we are not asking for a funding commitment at this stage.

This is deliberately a conditional ask: the specific fiscal question Budget would need to answer differs depending on which outcome is pursued (a new distinct park unit likely raises different questions than a facility-specific exception within the existing Jackson Park number, which in turn differs from a policy change affecting multiple large CPD parks system-wide). Until that upstream determination is made, Budget's analysis has no fixed scope to work from.

Section 6 of 6

Timing: Budget Follows, It Does Not Precede

To state this plainly: Budget & Management is not a Gate 0 blocker. The recommended internal pathway for this campaign runs Park Supervisor/Area Manager and LCA, then Planning and Law and South Region/Operations, then COO/executive review, then the General Superintendent, then the appropriate Board route. Budget's fiscal-impact analysis logically sits after the classification/governance determination, not before it or in parallel with Gate 0.

Practically, that means this briefing is informational at this stage. Budget should expect a follow-up request — with a defined scope — once Planning & Development and Law have identified which administrative path CPD intends to take. No premature budget action, cost-center creation, or line-item request is being sought today.

Does forming a new PAC require new CPD budget line items?

Not in the ordinary case, on the working assumption in this deck: PACs, including JPAC, are volunteer community advisory bodies, not paid CPD staff positions. Pending verification — this campaign's source material does not include CPD's actual internal PAC-support cost structure, so Budget should confirm this against real data rather than take it as given. Separately, if a new distinct administrative/park-unit designation is created (Outcome A), there may be secondary administrative costs — such as GIS/property-record updates — that are distinct from the PAC itself and that Budget should scope once that path is confirmed.

Who funds BBPAC's own operations?

That is BBPAC's own organizational question, not a CPD budget question. The source material notes that the Chicago Parks Foundation currently offers fiscal sponsorship to PACs and other park partners, which is relevant to how a recognized BBPAC could fund its own activities after recognition — see the Chicago Parks Foundation page. This is separate from, and not a substitute for, whatever CPD-side administrative costs Budget identifies for its own processes.

What's the fiscal difference between Outcome A and Outcome B?

Unknown at this stage. The source material does not contain a fiscal comparison between Outcome A (distinct park status), Outcome B (a formal facility-specific PAC exception within Jackson Park), or Outcome C (a policy change). No published CPD procedure describing how a facility separates from an existing park number has been found, so the underlying administrative mechanism — which would drive any fiscal difference — is itself pending Planning & Development's and Law's determination. Budget's analysis should follow once that mechanism is known.

Will CPD need to create a new cost center or park number for 63rd Street Beach?

Unknown. This is a Planning & Development and Law question, not a Budget question, and it has not yet been answered in this campaign's research. If a new park number or distinct administrative unit is ultimately designated, Budget & Management would then need to determine whether that carries cost-center implications — but that follows the classification decision, it does not precede it.

Is Budget & Management being asked to approve or oppose BBPAC recognition?

No. Budget & Management is not part of the Gate 0 decision chain and is not being asked to weigh in on whether recognition should happen. The ask is narrower and later: once the classification question is resolved by others, determine whether the resulting administrative arrangement has material fiscal consequences for CPD, using CPD's own cost data.
