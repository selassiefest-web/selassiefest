Why this deck groups Facilities and Revenue together

This presentation follows the campaign's own department architecture: Facility Management and Revenue are treated as one combined audience — "Beach House, Events, Capital & Commercial Operations" — because their two roles describe the same physical building from two sides of the same desk. Facility Management holds the Beach House's condition, maintenance, and capital-needs picture. Revenue holds the permitting, concessions, and rental record that already treats the Beach House as a special-event venue. Neither department decides whether 63rd Street Beach gets its own PAC recognition — that is a classification and governance question routed through the Park Supervisor/Area Manager, LCA, Planning & Development, Law, and ultimately the General Superintendent and Board. What Facility Management and Revenue can do is confirm, from their own records, the operational facts the campaign is relying on: that the Beach House is a distinct historic structure with unresolved capital questions, and that it already operates as a commercial/event venue separate from ordinary park recreation.

Why you're seeing this deck

You are being asked to validate facts about a building and an operation you already manage — not to weigh in on the PAC classification decision itself.

The Beach House: history and condition case

The 63rd Street Beach House is a historic bathing pavilion built in 1919. CPD's own description of the site traces its origin to South Side residents' demand for more beaches — the Beach House exists because a specific community pushed for it. That history is part of the case for why this facility deserves focused, informed capital attention rather than being one line item inside a 551.52-acre park's general facilities inventory.

What the campaign does not yet have is a completed condition assessment of the building itself.

Status check Pending verification

A full Beach House condition and capital-needs study has not yet been produced. The Gate 0 evidence plan calls this the "Conditions and Neglect Dossier" — dated photographs, unresolved maintenance problems, sanitation, lighting, restrooms, accessibility, safety and programming deficiencies, vendor problems, capital deficiencies, complaints, work orders, and prior promises. That dossier is still pending. See the evidence status page for what is and is not yet documented.

Until that dossier exists, any claim about the building's current physical condition should be treated as an open question for Facility Management to answer from its own inspection and work-order records — not as an established finding.

Revenue's role: permitting, concessions, and rentals

Revenue already treats the Beach House differently from ordinary Jackson Park recreation space. The Beach House is a CPD special-event venue, and Revenue coordinates special-event permitting for it. The Beach House also carries concessions and rental activity tied to that special-event function.

The point for this section is narrow and factual: the Beach House already functions with commercial and event activity distinct from ordinary park use, and Revenue's own permitting, concessions, and rental records are the documentation of that fact. This case does not ask Revenue to change any of that activity — it asks Revenue to help document what already exists.

What this section is not

This is not a request to shift concession revenue, permitting authority, or event-booking control to a new body. It is a request to describe, using Revenue's own current records, functions that already operate separately from general park recreation.

Capital needs and the investment case

The capital case here rests on historic preservation, not on any specific dollar figure. The Beach House is a 1919 structure that arose directly from community demand — that historic-preservation angle is the strongest available frame for why capital attention to this specific building matters, independent of whatever CPD's general facilities budget process produces for the rest of Jackson Park.

No invented figures Pending verification

No capital-investment dollar amount is asserted here because none has been established. The source record does not contain cost estimates, and none should be invented. Establishing an actual capital-needs figure depends on records that have not yet been obtained — capital expenditure and maintenance/work-order history, and facility-condition assessments — which are part of the pending FOIA/public-records requests this campaign plans to file, and part of the still-open Conditions and Neglect Dossier described in Section 2.

Until those records exist, the honest version of this case is: a historically significant, 1919 public building with a documented origin story and an unresolved condition/capital picture — not a building with a known price tag for repair.

Does dedicated PAC status change who approves event permits at the Beach House?

No. BBPAC is proposed as an advisory council, the same model as JPAC — it advises CPD; it does not take over permitting authority. CPD, through Revenue, retains permit approval for the Beach House exactly as it does today.

Would BBPAC control revenue from concessions?

No. Concession agreements, vendor relationships, and revenue collection remain CPD/Revenue functions. Nothing in this case proposes transferring commercial control to a PAC — advisory councils like JPAC do not operate concessions, and BBPAC would be modeled the same way.

What capital investment is actually being requested?

No specific dollar figure is being requested at this stage. The case is that a 1919 historic structure deserves focused capital attention, but the underlying condition assessment (the Conditions and Neglect Dossier) has not yet been completed. Any specific investment figure would need to come after that assessment and the related records requests — not before.

Is this a step toward privatizing the Beach House or its events and concessions?

No. The Beach House would remain a public CPD facility. BBPAC's proposed role is advisory — stewardship, safety, historic preservation, cultural programming, accessibility, environmental protection, and community representation — not facility operation or commercial control.

Does this deck ask Facility Management or Revenue to decide whether BBPAC is recognized?

No. That classification decision runs through the Park Supervisor/Area Manager, LCA, Planning & Development, Law, the Chief Operating Officer, the General Superintendent, and — if required — the Board of Commissioners. Facility Management and Revenue are asked to validate operational facts about the Beach House, not to approve or oppose recognition.

If BBPAC is recognized, does day-to-day Beach House management change?

Not as described in this case. CPD retains facility control, permitting, and revenue collection either way. What would change is that a dedicated advisory council — rather than one general council covering all of 551.52-acre Jackson Park — would have a formal advisory channel focused specifically on the Beach House and the beach complex around it.
