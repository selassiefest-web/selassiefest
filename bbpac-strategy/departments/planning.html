Section 1 of 10

Why Planning & Development is the most important technical audience for this request

Every other department in this campaign is downstream of one question this office owns: what is 63rd Street Beach, administratively, and can it become something else? Legislative & Community Affairs cannot register a PAC for a unit that does not exist. Law cannot write an authority memo until it knows what kind of administrative action is even on the table. The General Superintendent and the Board cannot act on a request that has not been technically defined. Planning & Development — land acquisition, park inventory, historic and boundary research, development policy — is the office that actually holds the maps, the park-number records, and the institutional memory of how CPD has classified, subdivided, and renamed park units before. This deck asks Planning to do what no other department can: determine, and document, the administrative and property mechanism by which 63rd Street Beach could become a distinct park or administrative unit.

What this means in practice

Every precedent, boundary concept, and open research question in this deck exists to support one request to this office: tell us whether this is possible, and if so, what record or action would make it real.

Section 2 of 10

The classification question, precisely stated

CPD currently lists 63rd Street Beach as located within Jackson Park (Park No. 19), which already has a registered PAC — the Jackson Park Advisory Council (JPAC). CPD's February 2025 PAC Guidelines state that the Park District "recognizes only one (1) registered PAC per park." Read narrowly, that rule alone can end a Letter of Intent for a "63rd Street Bongo Beach PAC" before it is considered.

The question for Planning is therefore not "should 63rd Street Beach have a PAC." It is: is 63rd Street Beach eligible for its own park or administrative-unit identity, distinct from Park No. 19/Jackson Park — for the specific and limited purpose of enabling dedicated PAC representation? That is a classification and property-record question first, and a governance question second. We are asking this office to answer it on the merits, using the same kind of analysis CPD would apply to any other park-boundary or park-numbering determination — not to make a policy exception for us.

Section 3 of 10

What CPD's own Code says about classification authority

CPD Code Chapter 7 assigns the General Superintendent authority to classify park property and designate classifications, with that classification transmitted to the Board of Commissioners for approval, amendment, or rejection. That establishes who can classify property in general terms.

Open question — central to this campaign

No published CPD procedure has been found describing how a community, or CPD itself, separates a facility from an existing park number and assigns it a new one. This is not a gap we are asking Planning to paper over — it is one of the campaign's central open questions for Planning and Law jointly. We are asking Planning to tell us whether such a procedure exists in practice (even if unpublished) and, if not, what the closest analogous administrative action would be.

Practically, this means Planning's answer to Section 9's request may take one of several forms: an existing but unpublished internal procedure, a novel application of the Chapter 7 classification power, or a conclusion that no such mechanism currently exists and one would need to be created. Any of those three answers is useful to the campaign; what we cannot do is guess which one applies without this office's input.

Section 4 of 10

Proposed 63rd Street Beach jurisdiction & boundary concept

BBPAC's organizing committee proposes that a distinct 63rd Street Beach unit, if CPD determines one can be established, represent the following elements as a defined jurisdiction:

  • The designated swimming beach and distance swimming area
  • The historic 1919 Beach House and its associated courtyards and promenade
  • Adjacent green space directly serving beach visitors
  • The dune/shoreline natural area (approximately 12 acres)
  • The non-motorized boat launch
  • Parking and ADA beach-access points
  • Other public facilities operating as part of the beach complex (restrooms, showers, water play feature, concessions)

Deliverable not yet built

The formal 63rd Street Beach Jurisdiction & Boundary Map — a precise, surveyable rendering of the above — does not yet exist. The list above is a proposed scope for that map, not a substitute for it.

GIS limitation to flag directly to Planning

CPD's CPD_Parks layer is a boundary polygon dataset, while CPD_Facilities is point-only. That combination can show where Jackson Park's outer boundary sits and where individual beach facilities are located as points, but it cannot by itself establish a precise internal sub-parcel boundary around the beach complex. Producing a real Jurisdiction & Boundary Map will likely require Planning's own survey, plat, or property-file records rather than the public-facing GIS layers alone — which is itself a reason this request needs to go through Planning rather than being drawn by the organizing committee from public map data.

Section 5 of 10

Precedent Bucket A — can CPD change or reorganize existing park identity?

The full analysis, ratings, and verification status for each item below live on the Precedent Library; this section names them only as an index for Planning.

  • South Park split (1880–81) — Jackson Park's own administrative lineage begins with the South Park Commission's subdivision of a single 1,055-acre holding into Jackson Park, Washington Park, and the Midway Plaisance. Confirmed
  • Maggie Daley Park (2014–15) — CPD's cleanest modern precedent for a distinctly named, operationally independent "park within a park" that remains officially inside a larger park. Confirmed
  • Seneca Park #1242 — land once part of Lake Shore Playground became an independently numbered park with its own PAC. Partially confirmed
  • Rutherford Sayre Park #127 (1999) — three separate parks administratively consolidated into one, showing CPD reorganizes park units, though in the direction opposite BBPAC's ask. Partially confirmed
  • Boundary transfers — CPD has repeatedly added and removed parcels from parks (Dooley Park, Washington Park, 2024 Board actions), establishing that park boundaries are not fixed. Confirmed

Taken together, Bucket A supports the proposition that CPD's park taxonomy has changed before through more than one mechanism — subdivision, consolidation, boundary transfer, and the "park within a park" model. It does not by itself establish which mechanism, if any, applies to separating a facility from an existing park number, which is precisely the question in Section 7.

Section 6 of 10

Precedent Bucket B — can a beach itself be an independent numbered CPD park?

  • Lane (George) Beach Park #1265 — the lead comparator: only 3.09 acres, yet maintained as its own numbered park rather than folded into neighboring Lincoln Park. Confirmed
  • Chase Avenue Beach, Park #1250 (voided) — the strongest candidate resembling a "larger beach area splitting into a separately numbered beach property," but the separation year is unconfirmed and requires a Board or property-file record. Pending verification
  • Other beach parks — more than a dozen additional CPD beaches (Hartigan, Doria, Fargo, Howard, Mahony Griffin, Juneway, North Shore, Prinz, Rogers, Leone, and others) exist as independently numbered parks. Confirmed

Bucket B's function in this deck is narrow and specific: if a 3.09-acre beach (Lane Beach) can carry its own park number, size and complexity are not disqualifying reasons to deny 63rd Street Beach a distinct identity. It does not establish that Lane Beach was ever split off from a larger parent park — see the FAQ for why that distinction matters and does not undermine the comparison.

Section 7 of 10

The Park-Number Administrative Paper Trail — the decisive open research question

Every precedent in Sections 5 and 6 is circumstantial until CPD's own records show, in writing, how park numbers are created, split, merged, or retired. The Precedent Library's Park-Number Administrative Paper Trail section frames this as a seven-question research program.

Not yet found

Question 7 — a confirmed, post-1934 CPD action that split one existing park number into two — has not been located. It is the single highest-priority open research item in the entire campaign. We are not asserting it exists; we are asking Planning whether its own property and board-action records contain it, and if not, whether an analogous action (boundary transfer, reclassification, or the "park within a park" model in Section 8) is the closer institutional fit.

This is the single most consequential thing Planning could confirm or rule out for this campaign. A confirmed post-1934 split would make Outcome A (full distinct park status) a documented, repeatable administrative action rather than a novel request. Its absence does not defeat the campaign, but it does mean the request should be framed, at least provisionally, around the mechanisms CPD is known to use — boundary adjustment, administrative sub-unit designation, or the "park within a park" model — rather than around a park-splitting precedent that has not yet been confirmed to exist.

Section 8 of 10

The "park within a park" alternative — a lower-friction fallback

If Planning and Law determine that a full separation from Park No. 19 — a new, independent park number — is not achievable or not the right instrument, Maggie Daley Park demonstrates a documented middle path: a distinctly named, operationally independent park with its own programming, fieldhouse operations, and supervisor identity, while remaining officially "within Grant Park." That model gives CPD a way to recognize 63rd Street Beach's operational distinctiveness — and, potentially, to support a facility-specific PAC — without necessarily requiring a new park number or a change to Jackson Park's outer boundary.

Why this is offered as a fallback, not the primary ask

The organizing committee's preferred outcome remains Outcome A (distinct park status) as described in the Global Presentation. The "park within a park" model is presented here as a documented, lower-friction alternative that this office should have on the table if full separation proves administratively difficult — not as a concession made in advance of Planning's own determination.

Section 9 of 10

What we are specifically asking Planning & Development to do

This request is deliberately narrow and technical:

  • Determine and document the administrative and/or property mechanism by which 63rd Street Beach could be established as a distinct park unit or a recognized administrative sub-unit, with a boundary sufficient to support dedicated PAC jurisdiction.
  • Confirm or correct the Jackson Park No. 19 property/boundary record as it currently applies to the 63rd Street Beach complex.
  • Advise whether CPD's own property or park-number records contain any precedent for the kind of action described in Section 7, and if not, which existing CPD action type (boundary transfer, reclassification, "park within a park" designation) is the closest available instrument.
  • Assist in producing the 63rd Street Beach Jurisdiction & Boundary Map described in Section 4, using Planning's own survey/property records rather than the public GIS layers alone.

We are not asking Planning to make the final governance decision — that runs through Law, the General Superintendent, and, if required, the Board. We are asking Planning to tell the rest of the process what is administratively and technically possible.

Verification discipline

Every precedent-status marker in this deck matches the corresponding entry on the Precedent Library. Before this deck leaves the organizing committee's hands, check it against the Evidence & Verification Status page.

Doesn't Maggie Daley Park remaining "within Grant Park" undercut your ask?

No — it demonstrates the opposite of what it might first appear to. Maggie Daley Park shows that CPD already operates a third category between "ordinary facility" and "independent numbered park": a distinctly named, operationally independent unit that can support its own programming, fieldhouse operations, and supervisor identity while remaining administratively inside a larger park. We cite it in Section 8 specifically as a fallback path if full separation from Park No. 19 is not achievable — not as proof that partial separation is our preferred or only outcome.

Why should Lane Beach's classification matter if it wasn't split from Lincoln Park?

Lane Beach Park #1265 is cited in Bucket B for a narrower purpose than the Bucket A precedents: it shows that a small, simple beach can carry its own independent park number rather than being folded into an adjacent large park, rebutting any argument that a beach is inherently too small or too simple to be its own park. It is not offered as evidence that CPD has split a beach off from a larger parent park — that is a separate question, addressed by Chase Avenue Beach (unconfirmed separation year) and by the Park-Number Administrative Paper Trail in Section 7. We are not conflating the two arguments and would flag it if any future draft of this deck did.

What exactly are you asking Planning to produce?

Two things: (1) a written determination of the administrative/property mechanism, if any, by which 63rd Street Beach could become a distinct park unit or administrative sub-unit, and (2) technical assistance producing the 63rd Street Beach Jurisdiction & Boundary Map described in Section 4. We are not asking Planning to decide governance questions that belong to Law, the General Superintendent, or the Board.

Has CPD ever actually split one park number into two since it took over from the South Park Commission in 1934?

Not confirmed. That is Question 7 of the Park-Number Administrative Paper Trail, described in Section 7, and it is the single highest-priority open research item in the campaign. We are asking Planning directly whether its own records contain such an action rather than asserting one exists.

Can't you just pull the boundary from CPD's public GIS data yourselves?

Not to the precision this requires. CPD_Parks is a boundary polygon layer that can show Jackson Park's outer edge; CPD_Facilities is point-only and can show where beach facilities are located as points, but neither layer is built to define a precise internal sub-parcel boundary around a facility complex inside a larger park. That is exactly why this request goes to Planning's own survey and property-file records rather than being drawn independently by the organizing committee.

Is this request a backdoor way to rename or take over part of Jackson Park?

No. Naming and jurisdiction are separate CPD processes; this deck concerns jurisdiction and classification only, for the limited purpose of enabling dedicated PAC representation. A future naming effort (e.g., an official "Bongo Beach" name) would be a separate, later project and would not by itself create a distinct park unit even if it succeeded.

If Planning cannot identify any mechanism at all, is the campaign dead?

No. That finding would be genuinely useful: it would mean Outcome C (a policy amendment permitting unique large-park facilities to obtain dedicated administrative status) becomes the primary path, per the Global Presentation's three-outcome framework. We would rather have that answer in writing early than build a Board presentation on a mechanism that does not exist.
