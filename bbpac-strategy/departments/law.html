Section 1 of 8

Purpose of This Deck

This is a request for a legal opinion, not an argument for a predetermined outcome. The organizing committee for a proposed 63rd Street Bongo Beach Park Advisory Council ("BBPAC") has assembled a factual and precedent record and now needs the Law Department to tell us what the Chicago Park District Code actually permits, what instrument (if any) is required, and which body holds the authority to act. Nothing in this deck should be read as a legal conclusion. Where the underlying research poses a question, we have preserved it as a question — addressed to Law, not answered on Law's behalf.

What we are asking for

A written determination — or a referral to outside counsel if the Department prefers — addressing the three questions in Section 2 and the six written questions in Section 5, before any petition is filed with CPD leadership or the Board.

Section 2 of 8

Three Questions This Campaign Needs Law to Help Resolve

Classification. 63rd Street Beach is currently listed within Jackson Park (Park No. 19). Can a facility be separated from an existing park number and assigned distinct status — as its own park unit, or as a defined administrative sub-unit — and if so, under what Code authority and procedure? No published CPD procedure describing this separation has been located by the committee.

Governance. CPD's February 2025 PAC Guidelines state that the District "recognizes only one (1) registered PAC per park." Is that a fixed rule, an interpretable policy, or a policy capable of formal exception or amendment for a large park containing an operationally distinct major facility? This is a policy-interpretation question, not one the committee is positioned to resolve on its own.

PAC recognition. Assuming Classification and/or Governance can be resolved in the committee's favor, what is the correct procedural sequence — administrative determination first, then Board action if required, then the ordinary CPD PAC-formation process (Letter of Intent, community meeting, elections, registration)? We want to avoid filing a Letter of Intent under a framing CPD can reject in one sentence.

Note on scope

These three questions are presented as open questions for Law's analysis. The committee has not concluded that any particular answer is correct, and this deck does not ask Law to ratify a position already taken.

Section 3 of 8

What CPD Code Chapter 7 Says on Its Face

On its face, CPD Code Chapter 7 gives the General Superintendent authority to classify park property and designate classifications, which are then transmitted to the Board of Commissioners for approval, amendment, or rejection. That much is stated directly in the Code.

What the Code does not appear to say — at least in any published procedure the committee has located — is how a community, or CPD itself, separates a facility from an existing park number and obtains a new one. Pending verification

This is a question for Law, not an assumed answer

The absence of a published procedure is not, by itself, evidence that no such procedure exists, that the General Superintendent lacks the authority, or that Board action would be required. It may mean any of those things, or none of them. We are asking Law to tell us which — including whether this has ever been done administratively without a formal Board vote, and whether any internal (non-published) CPD procedure governs it.

Section 4 of 8

The Board's Role and Vote Threshold

CPD's Code states that "the management and control of business and property of the Park District shall be vested in the Board of Commissioners." Four commissioners constitute a quorum, and four affirmative votes are required to carry a motion. The current seven-member Board is Dr. Marlon Everett (President), Modesto Valle (Vice President), Sharon Bush, Robert Castaneda, Sean Garrett, Philip Jackson, and Coya Paz Brownrigg.

Three standing committees plausibly map onto this matter: Programs & Recreation (relations with citizens, community groups, and advisory councils; park and facility naming; recreational and cultural matters); Capital Improvements (long-term facility plans; sale, lease, transfer, or acquisition of District land); and Administration (legal matters, government relations, general policy).

Procedural wrinkle: the Board President can suspend the use of standing committees, and matters originating from the General Superintendent can sometimes proceed directly to the full Board. The committee does not know, and cannot confirm without Law's or the Board Secretary's input, whether this matter would in fact route through one, some, or none of these committees.

Request

We ask Law (or the Board Secretary, at Law's direction) to confirm the actual procedural route before we prepare committee-specific materials, rather than assume a three-committee hearing sequence that may not apply.

Section 5 of 8

Six Questions the Campaign Is Requesting CPD Answer in Writing

The committee intends to submit a Formal Request for Written CPD Determination. Framed plainly, a favorable written determination from Law and/or CPD leadership would need to resolve all six of the following:

(a) Does CPD consider 63rd Street Beach eligible for independent PAC representation?

(b) If not, is the reason solely its current inclusion within Jackson Park No. 19?

(c) What officer or body has the authority to designate 63rd Street Beach as a distinct park or administrative park unit?

(d) What procedure is required to obtain such a designation?

(e) Can the General Superintendent, Legislative & Community Affairs, the PAC Governance Committee, or the Board approve a facility-specific PAC?

(f) If current policy prohibits it, what process is required to seek a policy amendment?

Why we are bringing this to Law before filing

We would rather have Law tell us these questions are unanswerable without a records review, or that the premise is wrong, than file a petition built on an assumption Law would not endorse.

Section 6 of 8

Precedent Material Law May Want to Review

The committee has compiled a Precedent Library at ../precedents.html. The following three entries relate specifically to whether CPD has reorganized existing park identity in the past. These are offered as administrative-authority analogies for Law to evaluate — the committee is not asserting that any of them establishes a legal right to the outcome BBPAC is seeking.

South Park subdivision (1880–81) — Jackson Park's own administrative lineage begins with CPD's predecessor subdividing a single 1,055-acre park into Jackson Park, Washington Park, and Midway Plaisance, which at minimum shows park boundaries have not always been fixed.

Grant Park to Maggie Daley Park (2014–15) — a distinctly named, operationally independent "park within a park" that remains officially within Grant Park; described in the committee's own materials as the cleanest modern reclassification precedent identified so far, though its legal mechanics have not been independently verified by counsel.

Rutherford Sayre Park consolidation (1999) — three separate parks administratively consolidated by CPD into a single park (#127); offered only as evidence that CPD has reorganized park units before, running in the opposite direction from what this campaign is requesting.

Caution on precedent weight

Each entry in the Precedent Library carries its own confirmed/partially confirmed/pending status there. None of the three above has been reviewed by an attorney for its actual legal mechanism (e.g., whether it proceeded by ordinance, Board resolution, administrative reclassification, or some combination). We are asking Law to make that assessment, not asserting it here.

Section 7 of 8

What We Are Specifically Asking Law to Do

We are asking the Law Department to address four discrete questions, in whatever form the Department considers appropriate — a memo, a meeting, or a referral for further factual development:

1. What authority, if any, permits CPD to grant 63rd Street Beach distinct status for PAC purposes — administratively, by policy interpretation, or only by a formal action?

2. What action is required to grant it — an administrative determination, a General Superintendent classification action transmitted to the Board, a policy amendment, or something else?

3. Is Board approval necessary, and if so, at what stage and through what procedural route?

4. Does creating a separate park number, or a separate administrative park unit short of a new number, require a land action, a classification action, or merely an administrative record change?

Format

We are not asking Law to draft advocacy materials. A short written response — even one that says "this requires a records review before we can answer" — is more useful to us at this stage than a fully reasoned opinion delivered later.

Section 8 of 8

Caveats

No attorney has yet reviewed this campaign's legal theories. Nothing in this deck, the master presentation, or the Precedent Library has been vetted by counsel for accuracy, completeness, or legal sufficiency. Every reference to Code Chapter 7, the Board's vote threshold, committee jurisdiction, or precedent should be treated as the committee's own reading of published material, not as a legal conclusion.

This deck exists to request that review — it is not a substitute for it, and the committee does not intend to proceed with a formal petition to CPD leadership or the Board until Law has had the opportunity to respond.

Standing invitation

If any statement in this deck, the master presentation at ../global/master-presentation.html, or the Precedent Library mischaracterizes the Code, District policy, or any precedent, we would rather be corrected now than after a petition is filed.

What specific statutory or Code authority do you believe supports this?

We do not believe we have identified one. CPD Code Chapter 7 gives the General Superintendent classification authority, subject to Board approval, amendment, or rejection — but no published procedure describing separation of a facility from an existing park number has been found. We are asking Law to tell us whether such authority exists, where it sits, and what procedure applies.

Have you retained outside counsel?

No. The committee has not retained counsel and is not asking the Law Department to act as counsel for the committee. This request is to CPD's Law Department, in its role advising CPD, to determine what CPD legally can and cannot do.

What is your fallback if the Board determines this requires a vote, and the vote is unlikely to pass?

The committee has not developed a fallback position and considers that premature until Law has addressed whether Board action is required at all. If Law determines a Board vote is necessary, the committee would want to revisit strategy at that point rather than assume an outcome.

Are you asking CPD to create a new park number, or something short of that?

We do not know which is required, and that is one of the four questions in Section 7. The committee's preferred outcome (distinct park status) may or may not require a new park number as opposed to a defined administrative sub-unit; we are asking Law to tell us the difference in legal effect between those two paths.

Does the existing Jackson Park Advisory Council need to consent to this?

The committee has not assumed JPAC's consent is legally required and is not treating it as a precondition unless CPD or Law says it is. The committee's stated preference is to seek JPAC's support or, failing that, neutrality — but that is a relationship strategy, not a legal position.

What happens if Law concludes current policy prohibits this outright?

Then the committee's next question — question (f) in Section 5 — is what process exists to seek a policy amendment, and we would want Law's guidance on that process before deciding whether or how to pursue it.
