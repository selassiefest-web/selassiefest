1 Project summary

In two or three sentences, describe your proposed program.

Rainbow Wednesdays is a free summer series at Rainbow Beach Park where neighborhood DJs, from middle-schoolers who taught themselves last winter to veteran selectors, perform live sets that blend, flip and remix music into something new on the spot, while young instrumentalists from South Shore play over the decks. Fourteen Wednesday evenings, June 2 to September 1, 2027, give at least 50 young people a paid stage on the lakefront and give the neighborhood a weekly reason to come to the beach together. Each Wednesday is one color of the rainbow and one sound; the season goes around the spectrum twice and ends with every color at once.

2 Program description

Describe the program in detail. What will the audience experience?

DJing has become the most accessible instrument in the neighborhood. A beginner controller costs about $100 to $300, mixing software is free, and young people in South Shore are learning from videos in their bedrooms. So are their parents and grandparents, many of whom grew up on Chicago house and on the sound systems of the South Side. What none of them have is a place to play for a real crowd. Rainbow Wednesdays builds that place, and makes it a performance worth coming to watch.

The series is built around live creation. A Rainbow Wednesdays DJ is not playing a playlist. They are blending two songs into a third, cutting a classic into a new rhythm, answering what the crowd asks for and, in the Live Lab, building a track from scratch while young drummers, steel pan players, horn players, keyboardists and violinists improvise on top of it. Every night, the audience hears music that did not exist before that evening.

Why “Rainbow”

A rainbow is ordinary light split open to show every color it was carrying. The series does the same with the music already in the neighborhood. Every genre is a color: each Wednesday is assigned one of the seven colors and one sound, and fourteen Wednesdays take the season around the spectrum exactly twice. Blends make new colors: the Theme Clash and the Live Lab are where two sounds mix into a third. The crowd is the rainbow: the format is built so that a twelve-year-old and a seventy-year-old, a first-timer and a veteran, share the same decks. Each week's color sets that night's flyer and the lights on the DJ booth, and the audience is invited to wear it. The Finale ends in full spectrum, with every color at once. And it happens at Rainbow Beach.

The hook: Theme Clash

Every Wednesday has one segment where wordplay and genre-mixing take center stage. A DJ takes a theme the whole crowd recognizes in two notes, such as a movie theme, a TV sitcom theme, video-game music or a TV commercial jingle, and blends it live with a killer, well-known song, so that both turn into something new. Other sources include cartoon themes, game-day stadium anthems, ringtones and viral video sounds, and neighborhood sounds like the ice-cream truck and the school bell. The MC rides the blend with wordplay, and the audience shouts out the next pairing.

The Theme Clash is cut to fit each week's sound (sitcom themes over Chicago house, game music over footwork, movie scores over a reggae riddim). It builds to semifinals on August 25 and a final at the September 1 Finale. Every pairing is cleared for a family audience, and themes are quoted briefly the way a DJ quotes a hook, not played in full.

Why this belongs in a park

  • It is participatory. Open Decks and the Theme Clash are driven by whoever walks up, and the Sing-Along night turns the whole beach into the choir. The line between audience and performer is meant to blur.
  • It is a ladder, not a single show. Free spring workshops lead to Open Decks, to a paid Showcase set, to the Finale. A young person can start the summer as a spectator and end it on stage.
  • It is intergenerational. The format pairs young DJs with veterans every week, and the music itself, from house to footwork to roots reggae, is the neighborhood's own history.
  • It is regular. A fixed weekly night builds a habit of using the beach on a weekday evening. That is something a one-day event cannot do.

3 Artistic quality and artists

Who are the artists, and how will you ensure quality?

Every night is anchored by a featured DJ, an experienced Chicago DJ paid a professional fee to lead the Live Lab and mentor the young performers. Featured DJs are chosen for live skill (blending, live editing, looping, playing with musicians), not for following, and the roster draws on the SelassieFest network of Chicago sound-system, reggae, house and hip-hop DJs. A paid MC hosts every night and leads the wordplay in the Theme Clash.

Youth DJs (ages 12–24) and young instrumentalists are chosen through an open call in April 2027. They apply by sending a short mix or a phone video, with priority for residents of South Shore and neighboring community areas. A panel of a featured DJ, a council member and a music educator selects each month's Showcase lineup. Every selected young artist attends at least one workshop before their first set and rehearses with the featured DJ before a Live Lab night.

Quality is built in through the structure: short sets (10 to 15 minutes) keep energy high, a mentor stands beside first-time performers, and every set is family-appropriate under a clean-lyrics policy (radio edits only).

Spring workshops (April–May 2027)

Six free two-hour workshops, taught by featured DJs: beatmatching and blending; building a set; live looping and editing; playing with live instruments; the Theme Clash (wordplay and genre-mixing); and a dress rehearsal on the beach. A small lending library of three starter DJ controllers and headphones lets young people without equipment practice and perform.

4 Community engagement

Who is your audience, and how will you engage the community around the park?

Primary audience. Families and young people in South Shore and the neighborhoods around Rainbow Beach, including the people already on the beach on a summer weeknight. Secondary audience. Young DJs and musicians from across the South Side, their families and friends, and Chicago's house, footwork, hip-hop and Caribbean music communities.

Marketing

A rainbow season poster and a weekly flyer printed in that night's color, with its theme and performers, using Night Out's printed materials; banners at the beach; flyers at schools, libraries, churches and businesses near the park; and weekly posts by the performers themselves. Young performers bring their own crowds, and that is the series' strongest marketing.

5 Production and technical needs

Describe your space, power, sound and staffing needs.

SpaceA grass or paved area of about 60 ft × 60 ft for the DJ position, a small live-music area and the audience. Exact location to be chosen with park staff, away from the swimming area and the fieldhouse entrance.
StagingGround level. One 10 ft × 10 ft canopy over the DJ position. No riser, no tent over 400 sq ft.
PowerPartner supplies a quiet inverter generator. No park electrical service or fieldhouse access is assumed.
SoundPartner supplies sound reinforcement from its own inventory. A sound meter at the edge of the audience keeps levels within standard park limits. No amplified-sound exception is sought.
HoursLoad-in 5:00 pm, program 6:00–8:30 pm (7:00–8:30 pm on the first Wednesday of each month), off site by 9:00 pm. No overnight equipment.
StaffingProducer, sound technician, two paid youth crew apprentices (teens learning live sound), the MC and at least two volunteers each night.
RestroomsExisting park facilities, during their normal hours.
WeatherLight rain: the canopy covers the DJ and equipment. Lightning or heavy rain: the night is cancelled and announced by 3:00 pm, and the lineup moves to a later week.
AccessibilityAn accessible viewing area on a firm surface, seating for elders and people with disabilities, and ASL interpretation at the Finale.

Safety and young people

  • Signed parent or guardian consent for every performer under 18, with an adult they choose on site.
  • Background checks for every adult who works with the youth performers.
  • Clean-lyrics policy for every set and the MC.
  • No alcohol, no tickets and no sales by the series.
  • $1,000,000 general liability insurance naming the Chicago Park District as additional insured.

8 Applicant and producing partner

Tell us about your organization and its capacity to deliver.

Applicant: Rainbow Beach Park Advisory Council

The Rainbow Beach Park Advisory Council is the volunteer community council for Rainbow Beach Park, 3111 E. 77th St., Chicago 60649. It meets at the park and represents the neighbors the series is built for. As applicant, it holds the Night Out agreement and leads the series locally. The council will name the officer who serves as main applicant when it votes to apply.

Consultant and producing partner: Ras Tafari Inc.

Working as Ras Tafari Inc. Consultants, Ras Tafari Inc. prepared this proposal and would produce the series for the council. Ras Tafari Inc. is a Chicago cultural and educational nonprofit dedicated to Caribbean and African Diaspora heritage, and the producer of SelassieFest, a roots reggae and cultural festival in Jackson Park. It produces events from end to end: artist contracting, staging, sound, permits, insurance and marketing. It has its own sound inventory and production crew. Its year-round work includes youth programming, and it is a member of several South Side park advisory councils, including Rainbow Beach's.

9 The same plan, other doors

This proposal is written for Night Out in the Parks, but the series is designed as regular, ongoing programming, which also fits the Chicago Park District's ARCS Partner Program (Resident track). If the park prefers that route, the same program, schedule and budget become an ARCS partnership proposal, submitted 60 to 90 days before opening night. The Illinois Arts Council Creative Projects application will use the same program description and budget.
