Rainbow Wednesdays
Every color of sound, live on the beach

A free summer series where neighborhood DJs, from first-timers in middle school to veteran selectors, blend, flip and build new music on the spot. Young musicians from the neighborhood play live alongside the decks, and every night peaks with the Theme Clash, where a movie, TV or game theme collides with a hit everyone knows. Every Wednesday evening, June 2 to September 1, 2027.

The idea

The DJ booth is the new garage band

A beginner DJ controller now costs about the same as a pair of sneakers, and free software runs on a laptop or a phone. Kids in South Shore are teaching themselves to mix, and so are their parents and grandparents. Almost none of them have a place to play for a real crowd. Rainbow Wednesdays gives them one, on the lakefront, every week of the summer.

Not a playlist. A performance.

Our DJs don't just press play. They blend two songs into a third, flip a classic into something new and answer the crowd in real time. The audience hears music that didn't exist until that minute.

Live instruments meet the decks

Young drummers, steel pan players, horn players, keyboardists and violinists from neighborhood schools play over the DJ's loops. It's a band and a booth at the same time.

Young and old on the same decks

A twelve-year-old who started last winter can play back-to-back with a selector who has rocked parties since the 1980s. Each one teaches the other something.

Why it's called Rainbow

A rainbow is every color at once. So is this music.

A rainbow is ordinary light split open to show every color it was carrying. Rainbow Wednesdays does that with sound. House, reggae, R&B, footwork, gospel, Afrobeats and hip-hop are all already in this neighborhood. Each Wednesday turns one of them into a color for the night, and then the DJs start mixing.

Every genre is a color

One color and one sound each week. The season works through the whole spectrum, so nobody's music is left out.

Blends make new colors

Red and yellow make orange. A sitcom theme and a reggae hit make a song nobody has heard. That's the Theme Clash. A DJ and a young drummer make a third thing too. That's the Live Lab.

The crowd is the rainbow

Twelve-year-olds and seventy-year-olds, first-timers and veterans, every block of South Shore. The more kinds of people on the beach, the better it sounds.

Your grandmother’s music, like she’s never heard it

A thirteen-year-old cues up the record her grandmother danced to in 1979, lays it over a footwork beat, and her grandmother hears her own song brand new. The music isn’t replaced; it’s carried forward. That’s a blend, and blends make new colors.

Music belongs to whoever loves it

A kid in Tokyo spins Chicago house. A kid from Bronzeville plays Afrobeats, and another learns roots reggae from a sound system. On Rainbow Wednesdays any young person can take on any sound, from any part of the world, and make it their own. The crowd is the rainbow.

What a Wednesday looks like

Five segments, two and a half hours, one big hook, done by dusk

Every night follows the same structure, so performers know what to prepare and families know what to expect. The music changes every week.

6:00 pm

Open Decks

Three 10-minute slots for first-timers of any age. Sign up at the table; a mentor DJ stands beside you.

6:30 pm

Youth Showcase

Four young DJs, 15 minutes each, chosen through the season's open call. The heart of the night.

7:30 pm

Live Lab

The featured DJ builds a track live while young instrumentalists improvise over it, starting from a beat and a loop.

8:00 pm · center stage

Theme Clash

The signature segment. A theme everyone knows meets a killer, well-known song, blended live, with the MC on wordplay. See how it works.

8:20 pm

Generations Back-to-Back

A youth DJ and a veteran trade songs one for one to close the night.

8:30 pm

Lights up, beach clean

Music ends. The crew and volunteers load out and do a litter sweep. Off site by 9:00 pm.

On the first Wednesday of each month (June 2, July 7, August 4, September 1), the Rainbow Beach Park Advisory Council holds its regular 6 pm meeting. On those nights the music starts at 7:00 pm, so council members can attend both, and the night opens with the Youth Showcase.
The hook, every Wednesday

Theme Clash: the song you know meets the theme you'd never expect

Every Wednesday has one segment where wordplay and genre-mixing take center stage. A DJ takes a theme the whole crowd knows instantly and collides it with a killer, well-known song, live, so that both become something new. The MC rides the blend with wordplay, and the audience calls out the next pairing. Each week's Theme Clash is cut to fit that night's sound: sitcom themes over Chicago house, game music over footwork, movie scores over a reggae riddim.

Movie themes

The score everyone hums, dropped into a dance-floor hit.

Sitcom themes

The intro to the show you grew up on, flipped into a summer anthem.

Gamer themes

Classic and current video-game music, from 8-bit to orchestral, blended with a banger.

TV commercial jingles

The ad song stuck in your head, made into something you actually want stuck there.

Cartoon themes

Saturday-morning songs for the kids, with a beat their parents remember.

Game-day anthems

Stadium chants and arena organ riffs meet a crowd favorite.

Ringtones & viral sounds

Phone chimes, notification sounds and the clips everyone has seen online.

Neighborhood sounds

The ice-cream truck, the school bell and the radio station intro, mixed into the groove.

Every Theme Clash pairing is cleared for a family audience. Themes are used briefly, the way a DJ quotes a hook, not played in full.

For DJs & musicians

From your bedroom to the beach in four steps

You don't need expensive gear or a following. You need a set you're proud of and the nerve to play it.

  1. Learn

    Free spring workshops (April–May 2027) on beatmatching, blending, live looping and playing with musicians. Starter controllers are available to borrow.

  2. Try it

    Play a 10-minute Open Decks slot on any Wednesday, with a mentor DJ beside you.

  3. Showcase

    Send a short mix or phone video to the open call. Selected youth DJs get a paid 15-minute Showcase slot.

  4. Take the crown

    Play your way into the Theme Clash semifinals and the September 1 Finale.

Youth DJs

  • Ages 12–24, with priority for South Shore and nearby neighborhoods
  • Paid a stipend for every Showcase set
  • Any genre, any gear: controller, turntables or a laptop
  • Mentored by an experienced DJ all season

Young instrumentalists

  • Drums, steel pan, horns, keys, strings, voice and more
  • School band, church and self-taught players welcome
  • Rehearse with the featured DJ before your night
  • Paid a stipend for every Live Lab set

Veteran DJs & mentors

  • Play a featured set and lead the Live Lab
  • Mentor a young DJ through the season
  • Paid for each featured night
  • Background check required for anyone working with youth

How we run it

A family night on a public beach, run like one

Safety and young people

  • A parent or guardian signs a consent form for every performer under 18, and an adult they choose is on site.
  • Every adult who works with the youth performers passes a background check.
  • Clean-lyrics policy for every set and the MC. DJs use radio edits.
  • No alcohol, no tickets, nothing sold by the series.

A good neighbor to the park

  • Our own power and sound; no fieldhouse or park electrical service assumed.
  • Volume checked with a sound meter at the edge of the audience and held within park limits.
  • Music ends at 8:30 pm; off site by 9:00 pm.
  • Litter sweep after every night. We leave the beach cleaner than we found it.
  • $1 million general liability insurance naming the Chicago Park District as additional insured.

Partner wish list

Seven colors, seven kinds of partners

A rainbow needs every color, and so does this series. Below are 28 kinds of partners we'd love to bring in, grouped by color. Most aren't the usual arts partners. Some could take one Wednesday; others could shape the whole season. None of them are signed on yet. This is the list we're bringing to the park and the neighborhood, and introductions are welcome.

Red

Family & generations

  • Parents & grandparents. “Show Me How You Moved”: during Generations Back-to-Back, elders teach the dance that went with a song, and the kids answer with theirs. Plus a Family Requests board, where grandparents name the songs of their era for the DJs to blend.
  • Senior centers & senior buildings. Bring residents along South Shore Drive down for an early-evening seat in the shade, with an elders' row near the booth.
  • Block clubs. Host a Wednesday: welcome the crowd from the mic, spread the word on their block and nominate a neighborhood DJ.
Orange

Movement

  • Fitness instructors. A 15-minute Rainbow Warm-Up led to the Open Decks set, so a young DJ's first gig is a whole beach working out. ReggaeRobics on Riddim & Steel night, footwork cardio on Footwork night.
  • Chicago steppers & line-dance groups. Teach a step to a youth DJ's blend; steppin' sets on Old School / New School night.
  • Dance crews, step teams & double-dutch. Footwork crews, school step teams and double-dutch squads perform in front of the booth during the Theme Clash.
Yellow

Music & schools

  • Middle and high schools. Recruit youth DJs and players, and give students credit for service hours on the youth crew.
  • Music teachers & band directors. Recommend young players for the Live Lab, coach them in rehearsal and get a featured shout-out on their students' night.
  • Marching bands & drumlines. A drumline entrance to open a night, or a full section on Band Meets Booth night.
  • Church music ministers & choirs. Lead Gospel at Sunset and lend voices to the Soul & R&B Sing-Along.
Green

Sound & music careers

  • Library teen media labs. Spring workshops in their studios, and open practice time on the lending-library controllers.
  • Recording studios. Studio time as the prize for the Theme Clash winner and the Rainbow Crown.
  • Community & college radio. Broadcast a set, interview the young DJs and invite one to guest on air.
  • DJ and music shops. Lend or discount starter gear, and teach a workshop on setting up a controller.
  • Audio and media programs. Mentor the youth crew apprentices on live sound and video.
Blue

Neighborhood economy

  • Sponsor a color. Seven local businesses, one per color. “Blue Wednesdays, presented by…” twice a season, on the flyer and from the mic.
  • Barbershops & salons. The neighborhood's real music hubs: flyers on the mirror, DJ nominations from the chair and a sponsored youth DJ.
  • Food trucks & the ice-cream truck. Permitted vendors nearby, and an ice-cream truck jingle in the Theme Clash on Neighborhood Sounds night.
  • Banks & credit unions. Open savings accounts for paid youth performers, with a five-minute money talk at the spring workshops.
Indigo

Care & community

  • Youth jobs programs. Make the youth crew and stipends official summer jobs through citywide teen employment programs.
  • Peacekeeping & youth-outreach groups. Be part of the welcome on summer nights, and bring young people who need a stage and a reason to be there.
  • Health clinics & audiologists. A blood-pressure table for elders, and free earplugs plus a hearing-safety talk for every young DJ, because a DJ's ears are their career.
  • Disability and ASL partners. ASL at the Finale, an accessible viewing area, and a sensory-friendly first half hour.
Violet

Earth, art & neighbors

  • Great Lakes beach-cleanup groups. Turn the after-show litter sweep into an official beach cleanup, with gloves, bags and data.
  • Visual artists & muralists. Paint live to the set, and a sidewalk-chalk rainbow by the entrance every week in that week's color.
  • Face painters, tie-dye & local designers. Help the crowd wear the color: face paint and a tie-dye table in the week's color, and a youth-designed season T-shirt.
  • Photographers & film students. Document the season and build each young DJ a portfolio photo.
  • Neighboring park councils. Cross-promotion with the South Shore Cultural Center, Jackson Park and Washington Park councils, and their young DJs on the bill.
Full spectrum

How it comes full circle

The grandparent who teaches a dance on Old School night brings the family back for the Finale. The music teacher who sends one drummer in June sends a section in August. The barbershop that sponsors Blue week nominates next year's youth DJ. Every partner feeds the next Wednesday, and the young people who start in the audience end the summer on the decks.
