## The four review criteria

### 1. Enriching and engaging programming
Reggae is Jamaican music the whole world claims, and UNESCO named it part of humanity's intangible cultural heritage in 2018. JayRebl brings it to the park as a night neighbors take part in, not just watch.

JayRebl is from Kingston, Jamaica, and has been singing all his life. He plays guitar and is a featured singer with Ifficial Reggae Movement, which backs him in this show. His sets are built on the songs reggae crowds love best: the lovers rock of Gregory Isaacs, John Holt, Freddie McGregor and Tarrus Riley, the roots of Dennis Brown, Lucky Dube and Bob Marley, and newer love songs by Jah Cure and Gyptian. He adds a few surprises, such as Peter Tosh's "Johnny B. Goode" and Sam Smith's "Stay with Me" in reggae style, and closes his second set with a guitar solo on "No Woman, No Cry" before the whole lawn sings the last refrain.

The show runs on twelve live moments between JayRebl and the DJ: a scratched door-knock that JayRebl answers, a custom dubplate voiced with his name, a live countdown the crowd shouts, and a sound-clash face-off the audience judges. Phone lights and call-and-response keep the audience in it. Set 1 moves from romance into reggae. Set 2 opens with roots, moves into singalongs and closes on familiar favorites. The repertoire is 24 songs and medleys across the two sets.

### 2. Production viability
Self-contained, and run entirely by JayRebl. He provides the stage, the sound system, the lighting, his own power source and the rest of the equipment, and he and his band set up, run and strike the show. The park supplies the space and a permit fee waiver, and nothing else is asked of it.

- **Show length:** two 50-minute sets with a short changeover, about 2 hours on stage.

### 3. Commitment to community connectivity
At each park we start with the park supervisor and the park's partners, and ask what the neighborhood wants, who should open the night, and where flyers will actually be seen. We recruit a local opener through those partners, such as a church choir, a school band or a young DJ, so the stage is shared with the neighborhood. Outreach runs through block clubs, churches, libraries and Caribbean and African diaspora organizations near each park. JayRebl's songs are the ones families already know, so the night works for grandparents, parents and children together.

**Outreach and marketing plan**
- Six weeks out: meet the park supervisor and partners and set the local opener.
- Four weeks out: CPD's printed postcards and posters (up to 1,000 pieces) at the park, libraries, barbershops, groceries and churches nearby, in English and Spanish.
- Two weeks out: e-blast to the SelassieFest list, social posts tagging the Chicago Park District, and notices to Caribbean and community groups.
- Week of: reminder posts and a street team at the park.

**Accessibility**
- ASL interpretation at each show. [confirm: the cost is in the optional budget line below]
- An accessible viewing area on a firm surface, with seating for elders.
- Free earplugs for children, speakers kept away from the seating, and a quieter zone at the back.
- Bilingual English and Spanish flyers.
- Routes kept clear for wheelchairs and strollers.

### 4. Budget feasibility
JayRebl's fee is a flat **$1,500 a show**, and it covers everything: his backing band, his DJ, his stage, sound and lights, his power and his travel. There is no separate equipment, staging or sound rental. The park provides the space and a permit fee waiver. Ras Tafari Inc. covers insurance and project coordination.
