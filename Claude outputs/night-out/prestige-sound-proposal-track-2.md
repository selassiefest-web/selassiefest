## Artist bio (about 450 words; trim to the form's limit, the flyer can use the first two paragraphs)

Prestige Sound brings Jamaican sound system culture to Chicago through reggae, dancehall, exclusive dubplates, and a live exchange between selector, microphone and crowd. Its sets connect the pleasure of a great dance with the stories, expressions and movement traditions that give Jamaican music its cultural character.

At the heart of Prestige Sound is an expansive collection of exclusive dubplates, built through extensive relationships with artists from dancehall's defining 1990s era. These custom recordings feature artists reworking their songs, calling Prestige by name, and creating versions audiences cannot hear on the ordinary commercial release. Each carries a personal connection between artist and sound.

Prestige brings that collection into the dance as a living musical archive. A familiar voice delivers an unexpected salute; a beloved chorus returns with lyrics made specifically for Prestige; an exclusive introduction builds anticipation before the riddim drops. For longtime listeners, these moments awaken memories. For newcomers, they reveal the relationships and creative exchange behind Jamaican sound system culture.

The artistry lies in knowing which dubplate to draw, when to introduce it, and how to connect it with the tune before and after. Prestige's deep reserves allow the selector to build conversations across artists, eras, and riddims, answering the audience's energy with another musical surprise. When recognition earns a forward and the crowd calls "Pull up!", the exclusive becomes a shared experience: Prestige's name in the recording, the artist's voice through the speakers, and the audience completing the moment.

In this tradition every selection begins a conversation. The selector builds the musical journey, the microphone voice introduces tunes and engages the gathering, and the audience answers through singing, dancing and spontaneous expressions of appreciation. A powerful response, a "forward," can bring the session to a peak. A call of "Pull up!" or "Wheel and come again!" sends a favorite tune back to the beginning so everyone can experience its entrance together.

Prestige Sound welcomes newcomers into the language of the dance. Riddim juggling shows how different artists interpret the same instrumental backing. A musical dropout gives the crowd room to carry a familiar chorus before the sound returns. Dedications, shout-outs and call-and-response acknowledge the people present.

That participation extends into **Reggae Aerobics**, an interactive movement segment woven into the performance. Prestige Sound teaches named Jamaican dancehall steps, breaking movements into approachable sequences before bringing them together with the music. Steps such as Bogle, Willie Bounce and Row di Boat become ways to explore rhythm, coordination and shared enjoyment. Participants learn the names as well as the movements, recognizing the Jamaican dancers and communities whose creativity travels with the music.

**Prestige Sound: Feel the riddim. Learn the movement. Become part of the session.**

## The four review criteria

### 1. Enriching and engaging programming
A sound system session is a Jamaican art form in its own right: the selector chooses, the voice on the microphone hosts, and the crowd answers. Prestige Sound opens that tradition to first-timers. Terms like "forward," "pull up" and "dropout" are explained as they happen, so no one is left out. The center of the night is Prestige Sound's collection of **exclusive dubplates** from 1990s dancehall artists, recordings made for Prestige by name that cannot be heard anywhere else. They give every show its own sound and make the night a live archive, not a playlist. [confirm with Stephen which artists he can name publicly, and that each artist is fine with being named]

Reggae Aerobics turns the lawn into a dance class. Prestige Sound teaches named steps (Bogle, Willie Bounce, Row di Boat) in short sequences, with demonstration, repetition and encouragement, then plays them out to the music at different speeds. Beginners get a way in, and experienced dancers add their own style. The names matter: each step is tied to Jamaican dancers and crews, and teaching the names keeps that credit attached. ### 3. Commitment to community connectivity
At each park we start with the park supervisor and the park's partners, and ask what the neighborhood wants, who should open the night, and where flyers will be seen. The Reggae Aerobics segment is built for families: children, parents and elders learn the same steps together. Outreach runs through block clubs, churches, libraries, dance and fitness groups, and Caribbean and African diaspora organizations near each park. A local dancer, school group or young DJ can open the night.

**Outreach and marketing plan**
- Six weeks out: meet the park supervisor and partners and set the local opener.
- Four weeks out: CPD's printed postcards and posters (up to 1,000 pieces) at the park, libraries, barbershops, groceries and churches nearby, in English and Spanish.
- Two weeks out: e-blast, social posts tagging the Chicago Park District, and notices to dance, fitness and Caribbean groups.
- Week of: reminder posts and a street team at the park.

**Accessibility**
- Reggae Aerobics steps taught at any pace, standing or seated.
- ASL interpretation if funded (optional line below).
- An accessible viewing area on a firm surface, with seating for elders.
- Free earplugs for children, speakers kept away from the seating.
- Bilingual English and Spanish flyers, and routes kept clear for wheelchairs and strollers.

### 4. Budget feasibility
Prestige Sound's fee is a flat amount per show and covers everything: the selector and microphone, dubplates, the Reggae Aerobics segment, sound system, stage, lights, power and travel. There is no separate equipment, staging or sound rental. The park provides the space and a permit fee waiver. Insurance is carried in the applicant's name.
