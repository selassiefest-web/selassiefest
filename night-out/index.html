The proposal, in short

Anatomy of a Shoreline treats a Chicago beach the way a biographer treats a life — as a body with a history, a rhythm, and a set of people who made it what it is.

The series is anchored by the premiere of an original documentary about 63rd Street Beach and its 1919 Classical Revival pavilion. The film follows the Olmsted and Vaux ground the beach sits on, the generations of South Side families who claimed it, the drummers whose circles have kept time on that shore for decades, and the people still there on an ordinary Tuesday afternoon. The film is in production now, with a rough cut complete.

Around the film, each edition builds a day of live work made in response to the shoreline: a visual arts showcase, spoken word written for the site, an open drum circle, original musical performances, and a community open mic where residents and elders say what the park has meant to them.

The flagship is a weekend at 63rd Street Beach in Jackson Park, where the film premieres. Two single-day companion editions carry it north to Rogers (Phillip) Park and west to Humboldt Park, with the live program re-curated by artists from those communities around their own park’s relationship to water and gathering ground.

Built to the program’s parameters

Free and unticketedNo gate, no admission, nothing to buy on site.
No alcohol serviceNo special-event liquor permit sought or needed.
No street closureEntirely within park boundaries.
Within park sound limits and hoursNo amplified-sound permit sought. Live program ends at dusk; the screening runs from dusk to about 10:30 PM, well inside park hours.
Partner-supplied power and soundOwn generator, sound and projection at all three sites. No field house assumed.
Same-day load-out at companion sitesOne overnight at the flagship only, with contracted security budgeted.
All three park regionsSouth, North and Central — a stated program priority.
Family-first, all agesDaytime programming built for children and families.
Crews hired in the park’s own zip codeTwo crews, each staffed from the community the park serves.
