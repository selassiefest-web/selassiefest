Bongo Beach PAC › Board & Meetings › October 21, 2026
Working Session · The GIS Map

Draw the Line

Ninety minutes on one job: the map that shows exactly what Bongo Beach Park is. We'll walk the live map together, settle what we can, and hand out what we can't. Then we head to the Washington Park Advisory Council meeting at 7:00.

Date & Time
Wednesday, Oct. 21, 2026 · 5:30–7:00 PM
Place
Washington Park Fieldhouse, 5531 S. King Dr.
Right after
Washington Park Advisory Council, 7:00 PM
What are we doing?
#PARK#

A park number needs a boundary. Tonight's job is the map that shows where Bongo Beach Park begins and ends.

Agenda

About 90 minutes. Bring a laptop or phone with the live map open. We end on time so everyone can walk over to the 7:00 meeting.

  1. Welcome & what's changed

    10 min
    • Beach inventory list (FOIA 6925), park-number list (FOIA 6927) and Jackson Park #19 boundary (FOIA 6929) are all due the day after this meeting, October 22. The inventory list may answer several of tonight's safety-asset questions, and the boundary record is the Park District's own line to test Candidate 1 v6 against.
    • Maggie Daley Park (FOIA 6838): the Park District asked to extend to October 23 and needs our written agreement. Its boundary record for #1303 is our model for how a carved-out park is mapped.
    • Full status: Since the last meeting.

  2. Walk the live map together

    15 min

    What's on the map today

    The GIS working record plots every verified layer together: Jackson Park #19's official Park District boundary, the confirmed facilities (Beach House, both dune sections, the parking lot, the beach), paths and underpasses, the boat launch, entrances, concessions, public art, ISGS shorelines from 2021–2024, and the draft boundary, Candidate 1 v6.

    Solid shapes are Park District-confirmed. Dashed or faded shapes are candidate or generalized positions that still need checking. An empty layer means “not yet proven,” never “not there.”

    • Toggle the layers, switch between street and satellite, and agree on what the map already shows for certain.
    • Note anything that looks wrong or missing; each note becomes a field check.

  3. Safety assets still to verify (step 1)

    15 min
    • The one category open data can't settle. Still needed: the exact Life Ring #025 cabinet location, lifeguard chairs and guard stations, the first and third distance-swimming buoys, swim-zone boundary markers, rescue equipment, the AED and emergency-vehicle access, and the beach wheelchair route and storage point.
    • Split the list: who walks the beach, with what (phone GPS pin plus a dated photo), and by when.
    • Cross-check against FOIA 6925 when it arrives October 22. A lifeguard-station count also closes one of the two open gaps in the Statement of Need.

  4. What today's beach operation covers (step 2)

    20 min
    • For each item, decide “in,” “out” or “needs a check”: the whole Beach House; both dune sections; the whole parking lot; the boat launch and its access route; the Lakefront Trail stretch; the swimming area out into the lake; the southern buoy and distance-swimming area.
    • The underpasses: the working recommendation is to include only their lakeward entrances, not the full tunnels. A tunnel under DuSable Lake Shore Drive is very likely a city or state structure, and pulling it in invites a fight we don't need.
    • This is the operational footprint: what the Park District already runs as the beach today. It is a record of fact, separate from our ask.

  5. The proposed boundary: Candidate 1 v6 (steps 3 & 4)

    15 min
    • Western line: the lakeward edge of DuSable Lake Shore Drive, stepping west only where needed for the Beach House approaches, parking, underpass entrances and access. That keeps us out of the interior of Jackson Park and likely avoids any state or federal road approval.
    • North and south anchors (pinned September 3): N-01 at the 59th Street Harbor pier and S-01 at the S. Coast Guard Drive bridge by the Jackson Park Outer Harbor. The south stretch stays road-to-waterline and leaves the yacht club and docks outside.
    • Known weak spot: the south stretch is built from four pinned cross-sections, not a continuous survey. A GIS analyst or surveyor would firm this up.
    • Housekeeping: Exhibit B still shows the older v4 acreage. Update it to v6.
    • Decide: adopt Candidate 1 v6 as the group's working draft, or name what has to change first.

  6. Transfer matrix & the boundary records (FOIA 6929)

    10 min
    • Every row of the transfer matrix is still pending: Beach House, dunes, parking lot, underpasses, boat launch, swimming area, life rings, paths and trail. Fill in whatever tonight's decisions settle; leave the rest honestly pending.
    • Acreage, the coordinate schedule and the metes-and-bounds description (step 5) wait until steps 1–4 are done.
    • Jackson Park #19 boundary (FOIA 6929): filed, due October 22. Agree now who compares the Park District's boundary and GIS record against Candidate 1 v6 when it arrives, and flags every place the lines differ.
    • Expert: who reaches out to find a volunteer GIS analyst or surveyor (pro bono planning groups, Catchafire, Taproot, university GIS programs).

  7. Decisions, owners & walk over

    5 min
    • Read back each decision and each owner with a date.
    • Head to the Washington Park Advisory Council meeting at 7:00. Listen and introduce yourself; no presentation tonight.

Before Wednesday

Twenty minutes of reading makes the ninety minutes count.

  • Read Operational Footprint & the Western Boundary, especially the five-step sequence and the boundary decisions.
  • Open the live map on the device you'll bring and try the layer switcher.
  • If you've been to the beach recently, bring dated photos of any lifeguard chairs, life rings, buoys or markers you saw.
