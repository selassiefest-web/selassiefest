Initial Organizing Meeting

What Are We Doing?

The first meeting of the Bongo Beach organizing group: one question, one answer, and a plan for who does what next.

Date & Time
Friday, Sept. 25, 2026 · 3:00 PM
Place
Jackson Park Field House
Attending
Stephen, Denise, Stanley, Riley, Brandon
What are we doing?
#PARK#

We're getting Bongo Beach Park recognized as its own park, with its own Chicago Park District park number. Every one of us gives this same answer.

Agenda

About 90 minutes. Times are guides, not limits.

  1. Welcome & introductions

    10 min
    • Each person says who they are and shares a memory of the beach. What connects all of us: we grew up enjoying this beach.

  2. The core question: “What are we doing?”

    10 min

    Bongo Beach Park: the name of the new park

    The Park District's official name for this beach is 63rd Street Beach. Chicagoans have called it Bongo Beach for generations, because drummers have gathered in a circle here almost every warm day since roughly the 1970s. The name came from the community, not from the city. Even the ward's alderman, Desmon Yancy, has noted that many locals call it “Bongo Beach”.

    So the new park's official name is Bongo Beach Park, built on the name people already use, kept from a tradition that is more than fifty years old.

    Beaches that already have their own park number

    Of the Park District's 27 swimming beaches, 12 are their own separately numbered park. Bongo Beach Park would join them:

    • Everyone answers in their own words, one at a time and without discussion.
    • Then agree on the one answer everyone gives from now on: #PARK#.
    • Agree that everyone gives this same answer to press, officials, neighbors and the Park District.

  3. Where things stand

    10 min
    • The beach is currently a facility inside Jackson Park (Park No. 19), not a park of its own.
    • In July 2025, gated parking and higher rates pushed the drum circle out of its longtime spot.
    • Allies so far: the Jackson Park Advisory Council has publicly called the circle a cultural landmark, and Alderman Desmon Yancy (5th Ward) has said he'd like to see an installation honoring the drummers, and perhaps an annual ceremony opening the beach season or a program to train new drummers (Block Club Chicago, Aug. 28, 2025).
    • The recognition process has started, but nothing has been submitted to the Park District yet.

  4. The two tracks, and why #PARK# comes first

    15 min
    • Track A, the park: the formal request to the Park District for separate park recognition, plus the evidence file behind it.
    • Track B, the council: the Park District allows only one registered advisory council per park, and Jackson Park already has one. The Bongo Beach council depends on getting the park number first.
    • Anything that doesn't move #PARK# forward waits.

  5. Who does what

    20 min
    • Park District contact and the written request: who drafts it and who signs it.
    • Elected officials: Alderman Yancy's office.
    • Jackson Park Advisory Council: keeping them on board as partners.
    • Evidence and history: photos, oral histories, community support letters, proof of the circle's 50+ years.
    • Community and drummers: keeping the circle informed and involved.
    • Each person can then claim matching sections on Join a Section.

  6. Decisions & next steps

    10 min
    • Confirm who owns what and the first deadline for each person.
    • Decide how the group will communicate.
    • Set the next meeting date and a regular schedule.

  7. Close: say #PARK# again

    5 min
    • Everyone answers “What are we doing?” once more, with the agreed answer.
