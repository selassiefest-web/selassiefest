#PARK#
The headline
The Park District has no record of the beach as its own place

Asked in writing for 63rd Street Beach's own facility record, the Park District could only produce Jackson Park's building list. That gap is exactly what #PARK# fixes.

What's new

Four developments since September 25.

  1. The Park District answered our first records request (FOIA 6835)

    Answered Sept. 16

    A correction to the September 25 agenda

    The September 25 agenda listed our records requests as “not filed.” In fact Ras Tafari Inc. had already filed one. FOIA 6835 asked how the Park District records 63rd Street Beach in its systems: the beach's facility ID, its location ID, and where it sits under Jackson Park. It was filed September 1, and the Park District answered on September 16.

    What the Park District sent

    No facility ID, no location ID, and no parent/child hierarchy for the beach. The Park District said “there is no document that covers every point” of the request. The only record it produced was its list of the buildings in Jackson Park, Park No. 19:

    Source: Chicago Park District FOIA 6835 response and its attachment “R – 6835,” September 16, 2026. All six buildings are listed under Park No. 19, JACKSON (ANDREW), South Region, owner CPD.

    What it tells us

    In the Park District's own records, the Beach House is a building of Jackson Park, and the beach itself does not appear at all. It is the largest of the six, twenty times the size of the 57th Street Beach House. The Park District also confirmed in writing that Jackson Park is in Area 3, South Region, and that its address for the building is 6245 S. DuSable Lake Shore Drive, not the 6300 address used publicly.

    One caution: the Park District cited the exemption for computer-system information, so an internal-only code could still exist. We describe the finding as “no record produced,” never as “no record exists.”

  2. The Maggie Daley Park request (FOIA 6838) is narrowed and accepted

    Answer due Oct. 8
    • Filed September 2 to learn how Maggie Daley Park, carved out of Grant Park, got its own number (#1303). This is our closest modern precedent.
    • September 10: the Park District sent Part 1 (the 2012–2013 Board notices and naming letters) and said the rest was too broad. It asked us to narrow the request, or it will be denied.
    • October 1: we sent the narrowed request. It asks for four specific things: the record that created cost center 1303, the first staff position assigned to it, its boundary record, and any written procedure for assigning park numbers. It also keeps one piece of the Board request: written public comments on the 2012 naming. The procedure record would tell us exactly how Bongo Beach Park gets its number.
    • The Park District accepted the narrowed request as written and said it will respond by close of business October 8, 2026.

  3. The website now shows the Park District's answer

    Done Oct. 1
    • Path to Formation: the records card now reads “2 filed · 1 answered,” with the status of each request.
    • Statement of Need, Pillar 2: the “no facility ID, no child record” point is now backed by the Park District's own written answer, not only by public datasets.
    • Matrix #748 (“How does the Park District recognize it?”), plus items #75, #115, #116, #119 and #162, carry the new findings.
    • Formation Process: the open question about the internal hierarchy now has a partial answer.

  4. Next records requests are drafted

    Ready for review
    • Beach inventory list (request 2 of 5): a plain list of what is at the beach and Beach House, such as benches, tables, grills, fountains, showers, signs, life rings, lifeguard chairs and buoys, with quantity and general location. It deliberately leaves out system records, building plans and security equipment, the three things the Park District withheld last time, so there is less to refuse.
    • Park-number list: one existing list of every Park District park number, showing which numbers are taken and whether one like #1304 is open.
    • Jackson Park #19 boundary: the official boundary map, any record of the beach as a sub-parcel, and whether the beach ever had its own number.
    • Seneca Park #1242: how a parcel once treated as part of another park got its own number.
    • Still to draft: GIS boundary records (request 3) and work orders (request 4). Both will use the Park District's own name and address for the building, “63rd Street Bathing Pavilion, 6245 S.”

Decisions for the next meeting

Each needs a name and a date.

  1. Review the Maggie Daley answer

    Due Oct. 8
    • The narrowed reply went out October 1 and was accepted. Go over what the Park District sends on October 8, especially the record that created cost center 1303 and any written procedure for assigning park numbers.

  2. Approve the next requests and their order

    Decide
    • Suggested order: the beach inventory list, then the park-number list, then the Jackson Park boundary, then Seneca. The beach inventory list is ready to send.
    • Send about one a week. Seven requests in seven days, or fifteen in thirty, can label us a “recurrent requester” and slow every answer down.
    • Put the full request in the body of the email. The Park District does not open attachments.
    • Decided October 7: Stephen Henry signs off on each request before it is sent. Requests will not go to a lawyer for review first.

  3. Set the meeting schedule

    Decide
    • Fix the next meeting date and a regular schedule, and bring the two people each of us agreed to invite.
