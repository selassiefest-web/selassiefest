Flags — fix or confirm before wider use

Nothing below looks like fabrication. These are the specific gaps a hostile reader — a Park District staffer, an alderman's office — would find first.

1. The 29-license cap is staleNeeds correction

The compact page states the Lakefront Venue liquor license is capped at "no more than 29" citywide. Chicago's own legislative record (Councilmatic ordinances O2026-0025029 / O2026-0024897) shows a recent amendment moving that citywide cap from 31 to 30 — not 29, either before or after the change. Re-read Municipal Code §4-60-073 directly and cite whatever number is currently in force.

2. Sanctuary of Unified Light has no public corroborationPending — in progress

Source [10] on the circle brief — the 2 September 2025 nine-request letter — is attributed to Allison "Mahma Zah" Young, executive director of Sanctuary of Unified Light. Their website, sanctuaryofunifiedlight.org, is currently a bare "Coming Soon" placeholder with no mention of Young, the drummers, or Bongo Beach anywhere on it.

Status: Sister Imani is meeting with the team this weekend (19–20 Sept 2026) and is expected to speak directly to this. Treat as pending, not unresolved — update this entry after that conversation rather than acting on the placeholder site alone.

3. Two sources that still need a human to open themConfirm manually

The Illinois Dram Shop caps (nolo.com — returned HTTP 402) and the ILCC Special Event Retailer's License PDF (returned unreadable binary twice) both need someone to open them directly before the figures inside are quoted externally. Dram Shop Act text: 235 ILCS 5/6-21, checkable at ilga.gov. ILCC PDF: ilcc.illinois.gov.

4. All September 2026 "status" claims are community-reported onlyTime-sensitive

That the fencing is still up and the 2026-season lawn commitment went unmet rests on community report, not a published Park District statement — both pages already say so themselves. Put it to the District in writing before this is used in testimony, and attach the answer, or the silence.

Resolved — the dead-outlet citation is replacedUpgraded

Source [7] on the compact page traced Chicago's beach-alcohol prohibition to dnainfo.com, which shut down in 2018. That's now replaced with the actual governing law: Municipal Code §10-36-100, "Intoxicating liquors" — live, current, and it names the rule directly: no bringing in, selling, giving away, or drinking intoxicating liquor in any public park, playground, or bathing beach, with limited exceptions for a picnic permit or a licensed on-site concessionaire (the mechanism Reggie's operates under). See the updated source [7] entry below.

Resolved — the JPAC resolution and Park District letter are now publicUpgraded

JPAC posted its own page on the drummers, which links directly to the adopted minutes containing the resolution and to the Park District's reply. See sources [7] and [8] below — both are now live links, not "in hand only." One small thing to square away: the Park District letter's filename dates it 9-23-2025; both BBPAC pages cite it as 25 September 2025. Check the letterhead date before citing it externally.

Historians, researchers & contacts worth adding

Matched to the specific arguments each page makes. Sister Imani is already engaged; the rest are researched leads with links to check before reaching out.

The Bongo Beach Drummers (63rd Street Drummers)
Meeting scheduled Sunday, 20 Sept 2026 · at Bongo Beach · direct stakeholders, not a researched lead

The community itself — the primary source both pages are written for. Named individuals already documented in the existing sourcing (Block Club, 28 Aug 2025 — source [3] above) include Debra Lawson of the 63rd Street Drummers Affiliation, Ric Currington (quoted in the brief), and elder drummer Will Crosby, whose 1969/1985 recollections are the brief's only oral-history-tier claims.

This meeting is the highest-value verification opportunity on this whole page: it can directly confirm or correct the "still up as of Sept 2026" fencing status (Flag #4), and put faces to the nine requests instead of only a letter.

Sister Imani
Already in contact · community / oral-history knowledge of the 63rd Street Drummers

No independent public bio turned up in search — she's an existing relationship, not a researched lead. Capture her account at the same evidentiary tier the brief already uses for Will Crosby's recollections: credible and specific, labeled as oral history rather than blended into the sourced record.

Adam Green
History & Race, Diaspora and Indigeneity · University of Chicago

Author of Selling the Race: Culture, Community, and Black Chicago, 1940–1955 — the closest existing scholarship to "the drum is the deed" and free-enterprise-as-culture.

Eve L. Ewing
Race, Diaspora and Indigeneity / Sociology · University of Chicago

Public-facing scholar on race, space and South Side communities; faculty affiliate, Center for the Study of Race, Politics, and Culture. Comfortable in public testimony.

Julia Bachrach
Independent historian · former Chicago Park District official historian

The actual Olmsted/Jackson Park landscape historian, with prior park-landmark designation experience — directly useful for the historic-landmark ask.

Geoff Smith & Sarah Duda
Institute for Housing Studies · DePaul University

Executive Director and Deputy Director of the institute that already produced the Woodlawn housing figures both pages cite. Formalizing this turns "we read their report" into "our research partner."

William M. Tuttle Jr.
Emeritus (retired 2008) · University of Kansas

Author of the standard reference on the 1919 riot, Race Riot: Chicago in the Red Summer of 1919. Cite the book; confirm his current status before treating him as recruitable — not a UChicago contact.

Travis A. Jackson
Ethnomusicology / Jazz & Black music · Music Dept., University of Chicago

Best lead for the "living transmission line" argument (djembe, Latin patterns, the Black Arts Movement's anti-commercial ethic). Confirmed current: Associate Professor, Director of Graduate Studies, Music.

Robert Vargas
Professor of Sociology & Director, Justice Project · University of Chicago

Studies the political economy of policing and how privatized safety infrastructure — vendor-produced policy, contracted enforcement — reshapes city responses to social problems. A near-exact match for the mechanism both pages describe: SP+'s private gate, the BACP enforcement partnership, and "who runs the gate" as a governance question. Author of Wounded City, on how ward redistricting reshaped conflict in Little Village.
