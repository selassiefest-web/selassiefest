Track A · Precedent Deep-Dive · Case File #1303

The Maggie Daley Blueprint

A single question, chased to the primary source: does the Chicago Park District ever give a defined slice of an existing park its own separate identity, while the parent park keeps operating around it? For Grant Park → Maggie Daley Park, the answer is now confirmed in CPD's own budget ledgers — and it is the closest modern blueprint we have found for Jackson Park → Bongo Beach.

This page does one thing only: it takes the single strongest modern precedent we've found — Grant Park's Daley Bicentennial Plaza becoming Maggie Daley Park — and holds it up against Bongo Beach's own 19-step formation path, field by field, with every claim marked as verified against a primary source, partially verified, or an open research target.

The verified timeline

Every entry below is sourced to a primary document — a government press release, an official CPD budget PDF, or a CPD Board legislative record — not to secondary reporting alone.

Pre-2012

Daley Bicentennial Plaza & Peanut Park, inside Grant Park

A 1970s-era plaza and adjoining green space occupy the northeastern portion of Grant Park — aging, underused, and administratively just part of Grant Park. Built in the mid-1970s on top of a new underground parking garage, the complex included a fieldhouse, tennis courts, an ice-skating rink, and Grant Park's first playground.

widely reported  Maggie Daley Park's own History page; CBS Chicago

Aug 25–26, 2012

The public announcement

Mayor Rahm Emanuel and former Mayor Richard M. Daley announce that a defined ~20-acre area — bounded by Randolph, Monroe, Columbus, and DuSable Lake Shore Drive — will become Maggie Daley Park.

verified  City of Chicago press release

Sept 12, 2012

The Board formally starts the naming clock — and awards Phase 1 construction

Two clean, unambiguous items, adopted 7–0: “Request to Initiate 45 Day Notice Period to Name North Grant Park in Honor of Maggie Daley” (item 10) authorizes the General Superintendent to open the required public-notice period; immediately after it, “Authorization to Enter Into a Contract for North Grant Park Renovation” (item 11) awards Phase 1 construction to James McHugh Construction Co., not-to-exceed $7,447,000 (bid opened July 11, 2012; Spec. #P-12017-01).

verified — primary source  Chicago Park District, Journal of Proceedings, Vol. 79, Regular Meeting of September 12, 2012, items 10–11. Searchable (not yet directly linked) via CPD's Legistar portal, which hosts digitized Journals of Proceedings back to 1869.

Dec 5, 2012

General Superintendent uses the name “Maggie Daley Park” at the Board's own public budget hearing

Presenting CPD's 2013 budget at the Board's public hearing, General Superintendent and CEO Michael Kelly names “the Bloomingdale Trail and Maggie Daley Park” among the district's signature capital projects — corroborating, on the record and on video, that the name was already in official use more than two months before the February 13, 2013 naming item.

verified — primary source (video)  CPD Board, 2013 Budget Public Hearing, Dec. 5, 2012

Dec 12, 2012

“Maggie Daley Park” is already the name CPD uses internally

The Board authorizes a contract with MDE Thermal Technologies Inc. for chilled-water service “at Maggie Daley Park” (non-competitively procured, estimated $98,440/year) — nearly two years before the park opens, the Board's own paperwork is already calling it by its new name and provisioning it with dedicated utility service.

verified — primary source  Journal of Proceedings, Vol. 79, Regular Meeting of December 12, 2012, item 7. Searchable (not yet directly linked) via CPD's Legistar portal.

Feb 13, 2013

“Request to Officially Name North Grant Park in Honor of Maggie Daley”

This exact item title appears as item 8 on the agenda for the Board's regular meeting of February 13, 2013 — confirmed directly from the Journal of Proceedings, not secondary reporting. The specific resolution language and roll-call vote could not be reliably read from this section of the source document: the text extraction shows a page-layout artifact here — item titles and their adopted resolution text appear to be drawn from misaligned columns, confirmed by checking an adjacent item, #7, whose title is about Soldier Field but whose resolution text is plainly about an unrelated audit contract. A direct look at the original page image, rather than automated text extraction, is needed before quoting exact wording or a vote count for this item. Two other CPD Board meeting recordings on YouTube have been checked (Dec. 5, 2012 and Aug. 14, 2013 — see below) and neither is this meeting; a recording of the February 13, 2013 meeting itself, if one exists, is still an open research target.

confirmed to exist — exact text pending  Journal of Proceedings, Vol. 79, Regular Meeting of February 13, 2013, item 8 (pp. 171–172). Searchable (not yet directly linked) via CPD's Legistar portal.

During 2013

Operating identity 1303 goes live — before the park even opens

CPD's financial system posts real actual payroll spending — $152,662 — against a cost center literally labeled “Maggie Daley – 1303,” a full year before construction finished. A Park Supervisor of Recreation position already exists on the books. Notably, no Board item anywhere in Volume 79 states a park number for Maggie Daley the way other CPD naming/creation items do: a contemporaneous item elsewhere in Volume 79 reads “...For a New Park (Park No. 572)”; the December 5, 2012 budget hearing names the new “Park 553” then under design for South Lawndale; and the Board's own Committee on Programs and Recreation adopted a “Request to Officially Name Park 499” at the very same August 14, 2013 meeting that authorized Maggie Daley's Phase Two renovation contract (see below). All three of those are brand-new parks created from scratch — unlike Maggie Daley, which was carved out of an already-existing, already-numbered park (Grant Park). That may be exactly why Maggie Daley's own items never had to state a number of their own — consistent with 1303 having been assigned administratively rather than through a named Board vote.

verified  CPD 2015 Adopted Budget, p. 84

verified — primary source (video)  Park 553: 2013 Budget Public Hearing, Dec. 5, 2012; Park 499: Regular Meeting of Aug. 14, 2013, Committee on Programs and Recreation report, item 1

Aug 14, 2013

Board authorizes Maggie Daley Park renovation, Phase 2

The Committee on Capital Improvements' report, adopted by the full Board at its regular meeting, lists “Authorization to Enter Into a Contract for Maggie Daley Park Renovation Phase Two” as item 1 — confirming construction continued in phases well into 2013, more than a year after cost center 1303 began posting real payroll spending. The contractor and contract amount were not read into the record at this meeting; that detail is still open, and CPD's own Legistar system is the next place to look for the underlying file. The same meeting's Committee on Programs and Recreation report, by contrast, adopted an unrelated item titled “Request to Officially Name Park 499” — a useful side-by-side reminder that when CPD names a park through the ordinary process, the item typically states a park number, something no Maggie Daley item in Volume 79 ever does (see the entry above).

item confirmed — contractor/amount pending  CPD Board, Regular Meeting of Aug. 14, 2013, Committee on Capital Improvements report, item 1

Dec 2014

Maggie Daley Park opens

The park opens to the public with its signature ice-skating ribbon, climbing sculptures, and play garden. Mayor Rahm Emanuel cuts the ribbon on December 13, 2014.

verified  CBS Chicago; DNAinfo Chicago; Wikipedia; CPD facility page

Oct 8, 2014

CPD Board authorizes the management contract, by name

The Board authorizes the General Superintendent to contract with MB Real Estate Services, Inc. for management and operation of “Maggie Daley Park, Cancer Survivors Garden, and Peanut Park” — a five-year deal with five one-year extension options, awarded through a public RFP that drew four submissions.

verified  CPD Legistar File #14-2125-1008

2015–2016

1303 becomes a permanent, growing budget line

“Maggie Daley – 1303” carries a dedicated Park Supervisor and Physical Instructor, and account 626060 (“Maggie Daley Park Management”) is appropriated $3,579,000 in 2015 and $3,861,966 in 2016 — Grant Park continuing to exist, unaffected, around it.

verified  CPD 2015 Adopted Budget & 2016 Budget Appropriations PDF

Not yet found

The single instrument that created 1303

With the Journal of Proceedings now in hand for the entire naming period (Sept. 2012 – Feb. 2013), it's confirmed that none of the public Board items say “1303” or otherwise assign a park number — only the naming action itself. That narrows the remaining gap to one specific question: what internal, non-Board record (a property/GIS master entry, a Budget Office location code, an administrative order signed by the General Superintendent) activated cost center 1303 sometime between the February 2013 naming and 2013's first actual payroll postings. That's this case file's single sharpest FOIA target now.

That absence is itself useful: it shows how much of the OPC comparison was never really about park classification at all.

Where this ranks among our precedents

Now that Maggie Daley's own budget identity is directly confirmed rather than inferred, it moves to the top of the list.

1

Grant Park → Maggie Daley Park (1303)

A modern (2012–2013), CPD-era creation of a separately numbered operating identity from a defined portion of a continuing parent park — verified directly against CPD's own budgets, not inferred from a park page's wording.

2

Chase Avenue Beach #1250 (voided)

A beach-specific carve-out from the larger Rogers Park/Beach system — the closest analog to a beach getting its own number, though the separation year and instrument are still unconfirmed.

3

Rutherford Sayre (1999 consolidation)

Modern CPD-era park-identity restructuring, running in reverse (merging, not splitting) — still proof the machinery exists and CPD has used it since 1934.

4

Seneca Park #1242

A separately numbered park identity with a well-documented property/lease history, though the original 1915 split predates modern CPD.

5

Lane Beach Park #1265

A small, standalone beach park — strong as a classification comparator, but not evidence of an actual CPD-initiated split.

Full detail on all nine precedents, including South Park's 1869 division and Maggie Daley's original writeup, lives on the History page and in the Formation Process Benchmarking comparator, both now updated with the 1303 correction.

The 1300-series family

Chasing the number series itself — not just the one precedent — turned up more members than expected, pulled directly from CPD's own 2015/2016 budget documents. Each of these is confirmed to exist as its own CPD budget cost center; which is not yet confirmed is which larger parent park each one was drawn from, so treat this as a lead, not a finished argument.

If even one more of these turns out to be a genuine parent-park carve-out like Maggie Daley, the “CPD has never done this” objection gets harder to make with every one we confirm. Next FOIA/research pass: pull the parent-park record for each of these before presenting them as precedents.

The honest limit of this precedent

A separate park number did not produce a separate PAC. This is the single most important caution this case file surfaces. Maggie Daley Park has operated under its own CPD budget/location identity since 2013 — over a decade — and no “Maggie Daley Park Advisory Council” has ever been formed. The Grant Park Advisory Council simply extended its own scope to cover it, meeting at the Maggie Daley Fieldhouse. That means winning Track A (park classification) for Bongo Beach would not automatically win Track B (a separate, registered BBPAC) — the two remain genuinely separate asks, exactly as the Formation hub's own “why both, why now” framing already assumes. This precedent should be presented to CPD as strong evidence for Outcome A's classification question, not oversold as proof that PAC recognition follows automatically.
