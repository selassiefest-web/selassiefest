Operational Footprint & the Western Boundary

Converting the verified facility, dune, parking, and access layers into the existing 63rd Street Beach operational footprint — and the first real boundary-control decision for the proposed Bongo Beach polygon, laid against Jackson Park #19 on a live map.

This page is where NP-02's GIS working layers become a decision record. Nothing here is a finished boundary. The formal decision layers stay empty until the sequence below is actually worked through — the same discipline the underlying GIS package itself insists on: “No empty layer in this package should be mistaken for an absent feature. It means the geometry has not yet been defensibly established.” A first Candidate 1 draft of the proposed boundary is sketched on the live map below (off by default) purely so the working boundary controls can be tested against what's on the ground — it is not an adopted decision.

The sequence

Five steps between the mapped assets sitting in the GIS package today and a boundary CPD could actually be asked to act on.

1

Verify remaining safety assets

The one category that can't be resolved from open data alone. Still needed, via CPD asset export or field verification:

  • Exact Life Ring #025 cabinet location
  • Lifeguard chairs and guard stations
  • First and third distance-swimming buoys
  • Swim-zone boundary markers
  • Rescue equipment, AED, and emergency vehicle access
  • Beach wheelchair route and storage point
Pending — blocks a defensible safety-geography boundary
2

Set the operational-footprint limits

Determine whether the present beach operation includes the entire Beach House, both dune sections, the entire parking lot, both underpasses or only their lakeward entrances, the boat launch and access route, Lakefront Trail portions, the swimming area extending into Lake Michigan, and the southern buoy/distance-swimming area.

In progress — see the western-boundary decision below
3

Establish stable boundary controls

The Bongo boundary should follow durable features — the DuSable Lake Shore Drive right-of-way, Hayes Drive or another western access line, underpass centerlines or portals, parking-lot edges, path centerlines, harbor or pier structures, fixed coordinate lines across the water — not the moving wet/dry shoreline, which remains evidence, not the permanent boundary.

In progress — western line proposed below
4

Draw two separate polygons

The existing 63rd Street Beach operational footprint, and the proposed Bongo Beach administrative boundary. These may overlap heavily, but they are not automatically identical — the operational footprint records what CPD already runs today; the proposed boundary is the ask.

In progress — Candidate 1 drafted for both (Exhibit B; live-map boundary overlay), neither adopted
5

Create the transfer matrix, then calculate the result

Once the polygons are settled: Bongo Beach land acreage, water-area acreage (calculated separately), total administrative acreage, boundary perimeter, acres removed from Park #19, remaining Park #19 acreage, a coordinate schedule, and a legal-style metes-and-bounds description.

Pending — depends on steps 1–4

The western-boundary decision

The one boundary-control choice that can be made now, ahead of the safety-asset verification.

  Recommended starting position

Use the lakeward edge of DuSable Lake Shore Drive as the principal western boundary

Expanding west only where necessary to capture the Beach House approaches, parking, underpass portals, and operational access. That gives Bongo Beach a coherent lakefront jurisdiction without carving deeply into the interior of Jackson Park.

This isn't just administratively tidy — it's already supported by this project's own research. Step 10 of the Formation Process Benchmarking tool states: “If the new park boundary simply uses DuSable Lake Shore Drive or Hayes Drive as a line and does not alter transportation facilities, there may be no IDOT/FHWA approval. If the proposal changes roadway ROW, intersections, lanes, underpasses, access, signals or federally aided facilities, transportation review becomes a separate track.” Choosing this line isn't only about drawing a clean map — it's the choice most likely to keep Bongo Beach out of the federal transportation-review track (Steps 8–15) entirely.

One refinement worth deciding now, not later: the underpasses. Recommend including only the lakeward portals of both pedestrian underpasses in the operational footprint and proposed boundary, not the full tunnel structures. A pedestrian underpass running beneath a state route like DuSable Lake Shore Drive is very likely a CDOT/IDOT-maintained structure. Pulling the entire tunnel into Bongo Beach's boundary risks pulling CDOT/IDOT jurisdiction into what should stay a purely CPD administrative action — the same reasoning that favors the DuSable Drive line itself, applied one level deeper. This is a proposed working position, not yet confirmed with CDOT/IDOT — flagged here as a decision to make deliberately rather than default into.

Not a legal opinion, and not a finished boundary. The western-boundary position above is a recommended starting point for the committee's own decision-making, grounded in this project's existing research — it is not a surveyed line, not confirmed with CPD, CDOT, or IDOT. Exhibit B's operational envelope and the Candidate 1 proposed-boundary sketch on the live map above are both first drafts built from the mapped assets and working boundary controls, not adopted decisions — the formal decision layers in the underlying GIS package (beach_operational_footprint, bongo_beach_proposed) stay empty by the package's own convention until steps 1–4 above are actually completed and a candidate is formally adopted.
