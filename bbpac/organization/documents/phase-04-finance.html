Financial Management & Controls

Bylaws Article 17 sets the framework: bank/accounting controls, financial reports at meetings, public access to required records, budget approval, and fundraising compliance. Phase 4 is the operational layer underneath it — the actual policies, forms, logs, and registers that make those requirements real rather than aspirational.

Working document, not legal advice. This page is part of a governance/compliance planning library for the proposed 63rd Street Beach Park Advisory Council. It is not legal, tax, or accounting advice, and nothing here should be read as confirming that the Chicago Park District (CPD) has recognized a separate 63rd Street Beach PAC. That threshold question — Gate 0 — is still open, and no fiscal structure decision below has actually been made yet either. Every document is labeled Proposed for that reason. Confirm current CPD, Illinois, and federal requirements with CPD and qualified counsel/a tax professional before relying on anything here.

4-01

Fiscal Structure Decision Memo — Own 501(c)(3) vs Fiscal Sponsor

CPD-RCPD-CProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Board / Finance Committee

No decision has been made yet — this document exists to force one before any money moves. CPD requires a PAC that solicits or receives funds to hold either its own qualifying federal tax-exempt status or use an approved third-party fiscal sponsor. Two real paths, with real tradeoffs:

Path A — Own 501(c)(3). File IRS Form 1023-EZ (if eligible; roughly $275 filing fee, weeks-to-months processing) or the full Form 1023 (higher fee, longer review, needed if projected revenue or complexity exceeds 1023-EZ thresholds) after Illinois incorporation and EIN (Phase 1). Gives the Council full control over its own funds, its own bank account, and its own annual Form 990 filing obligation — but requires sustained compliance capacity (a Treasurer willing to own annual filings) and a delay between incorporation and actually being able to accept tax-deductible gifts.

Path B — Fiscal Sponsorship. A qualified 501(c)(3) holds funds on the Council's behalf under a written agreement (4-02), typically charging a fee (often 5–10% of funds processed) in exchange for immediate tax-deductible giving capacity, its own accounting/990 filing, and reduced Council-side administrative burden — at the cost of the sponsor retaining legal control over the funds and some approval authority over spending. CPD's own current PAC Guidelines name a concrete example of what it considers acceptable here: "the Chicago Parks Foundation, or another organization offering fiscal sponsorship services."

This memo is where the Board records which path it selects, why, and when — and until it's completed, the Council should not represent to any donor, sponsor, or grantor that either path is settled.

4-02

Fiscal Sponsorship Agreement

CPD-CProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Board / Treasurer / Counsel

Triggered only if 4-01 selects Path B. A real fiscal sponsorship agreement — not a handshake — needs to specify: who legally owns and controls the funds while held (ordinarily the sponsor, until disbursed); the sponsor's fee structure and how/when it's deducted; how restricted gifts are tracked and honored; what expenditures the sponsor will and won't approve, and how fast; the sponsor's own reporting to the Council (statements, at minimum monthly); and a clear termination clause describing what happens to any remaining funds if the relationship ends. Executed by an authorized Council officer and the sponsor's authorized representative before any solicitation begins under this structure.

FED-RCPD-CProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Chart of Accounts

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

A starter structure, refined once real transaction volume exists:

4-10

Financial Policies & Internal Controls Manual

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Finance Committee

The master reference that everything else in this phase implements piece by piece. Core principle: segregation of duties — no single person should be able to both authorize and record a transaction unsupervised. Concretely: the Treasurer records transactions, but disbursements above the lowest threshold need a second approver (4-21); bank reconciliations are performed or reviewed by someone other than the person who made the deposits (4-16); and cash is always counted by two people (4-19). One firm, real-precedent rule stated plainly: no Council funds may be loaned to any person, officer or otherwise. Sections: receipts, disbursements, reconciliation, reporting cadence, records retention cross-reference (Phase 5), and an explicit fraud-red-flags list drawn from the Fraud Prevention & Response Policy (4-51).

4-11

Annual Budget Policy & Calendar

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Treasurer / Finance Committee

Sets the annual rhythm: draft budget prepared by the Treasurer two months before the new fiscal year begins; circulated to the full membership (not just the Board) for comment, consistent with the public-access spirit of Bylaws Article 17; adopted by the threshold the Bylaws set for budget approval; amendments during the year require the same approval level as the original line item under the Expense Approval Threshold Matrix (4-21); and monitored monthly via the Budget-to-Actual Report (4-13).

4-12

Annual Operating Budget

CPD-RBPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

The actual approved dollar plan for the year, structured by the Chart of Accounts (4-09) and tied line-by-line to the Annual Work Plan (Phase 10) — a stewardship-day budget with no matching Chart of Accounts line, or a work-plan goal with no funding behind it, is exactly the mismatch this document is meant to surface before the year starts, not after.

4-13

Budget-to-Actual Report

CPD-RBPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Treasurer

A simple three-column report (Budgeted / Actual / Variance) by Chart of Accounts line, prepared monthly at minimum and presented at every regular meeting alongside the Treasurer's Report (4-14). A variance beyond a threshold the Finance Committee sets (e.g. 15% over on any expense line) triggers a one-line written explanation, not just a number — the point is early warning, not a retrospective audit finding.

4-14

Treasurer's Meeting Report

CPD-RProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Treasurer

Current CPD guidance expects a financial report at PAC meetings and expects the Council's financial records to be available for public review — this is that report, in standing form: current bank balance, month's receipts and disbursements in summary, budget-to-actual highlights (4-13), and any restricted-fund or grant-compliance notes. Attached to the minutes and retained in the PAC Binder (Phase 6) as the primary evidence CPD's requirement is being met continuously, not just at year-end.

4-15

General Ledger / Transaction Register

IL-RBPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Treasurer / Bookkeeper

The complete, continuous accounting record — every receipt and disbursement, dated, coded to the Chart of Accounts (4-09), with a running balance. Whether kept in dedicated nonprofit accounting software or a well-controlled spreadsheet, this is the single source of truth every other financial report in this phase (4-13, 4-14, 4-16) is drawn from, and the record Illinois corporate law and CPD's public-access expectation both ultimately point back to.

4-16

Monthly Bank Reconciliation

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Treasurer + Independent Reviewer

Every month, without exception, the bank statement is reconciled against the General Ledger (4-15) — and a second person (an officer other than the Treasurer, or, once one exists, the Finance Committee chair) reviews and signs off on the reconciliation. This second-set-of-eyes step is the single cheapest fraud/error control available and should never be skipped even in a "quiet" month with few transactions.

4-18

Cash Handling & Event Cash-Control Policy

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Finance Committee

Adopted before the first cash-handling event (a bake sale table, a beach cleanup donation jar, a merchandise table). Requires: two-person counts at every stage (collection, transfer, deposit); deposits within 2 business days, never held at an officer's home longer than necessary; sequentially numbered receipts for any in-person sale over a set amount; and an absolute rule that Council cash is never commingled with any individual's personal funds, even temporarily "for convenience."

4-21

Expense Approval Threshold Matrix

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Finance / Governance Committees

Illustrative starting tiers, to be calibrated to real budget size once one exists:

Under $100: Treasurer approval alone, logged.
$100–$500: Treasurer + one other officer.
$500–$2,000: Full Board vote (simple majority).
Over $2,000, or any multi-year commitment: Full Board vote plus advance notice to the general membership at the prior regular meeting.

No officer may approve their own reimbursement or a payment to an entity in which they've disclosed a conflict (3-12) regardless of amount.

4-22

Expense Reimbursement Policy

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Finance Committee

Eligible expenses must be pre-approved where the Threshold Matrix requires it, and always supported by an original itemized receipt (not a card statement line alone). Reimbursement requests must be submitted within 60 days of the expense. No officer may approve their own reimbursement — a second officer signs off, every time, regardless of amount.

4-24

Purchasing / Procurement Policy

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Finance Committee

Purchases above a set threshold (aligned to the Expense Approval Threshold Matrix, 4-21) require at least two competitive quotes unless a documented sole-source justification applies (a specialty vendor, a time-sensitive need). 4-26

Debit / Credit Card Policy

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Finance Committee

If the Council issues any card, it is issued to a named individual officer (never a shared/anonymous card), with a set spending limit, a defined list of permitted categories, and a hard rule that every charge is matched to a receipt and reviewed monthly against the statement by someone other than the cardholder. Cash advances are prohibited outright.

4-27

Petty Cash Policy

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Finance Committee

Default position: no petty cash fund. Small purchases go through the Expense Reimbursement Form (4-23) instead. If the Finance Committee later determines an actual on-site cash fund is unavoidable (e.g. for a specific recurring event), any such fund is capped at an explicit dollar limit, held by a single named custodian, and reconciled after every use — never left as a standing, unreconciled float.

4-28

Gift Acceptance Policy (GAP)

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Finance / Governance Committees

Defines what the Council can accept without special review (cash, checks, standard in-kind items under a set value) versus what requires Finance Committee sign-off before acceptance: securities or real property (valuation and liquidity concerns), gifts with unusual strings attached, gifts from a source the Conflict of Interest process (3-12, Phase 5) flags, or any gift genuinely intended for the park itself rather than the Council — which routes through the Donation Acceptance Policy (4-29) and CPD's own acceptance process instead.

4-29

Donation Acceptance Policy

CPD-CBPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Finance Committee / CPD Liaison

Draws a bright line every donor-facing volunteer needs to understand: a gift to the Council (funds it operating, programming, stewardship supplies) follows the GAP (4-28) and the Council's own bank account. A gift intended for the park itself (a bench, a sign, a physical improvement) is not the Council's to accept unilaterally — it routes through the CPD Donation Acceptance Form File (4-55) and Park Supervisor/Area Manager coordination, regardless of how it was originally offered.

4-30

Restricted Gift Policy

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Finance Committee

A donor-imposed restriction (e.g. "for dune restoration only") is only accepted if the Council can realistically honor it and it's mission-consistent. Every accepted restriction is recorded in the Donor Restriction Agreement (4-32) and tracked separately in the Restricted Funds Ledger (4-46) until fully spent for its stated purpose. Modifying or releasing a restriction after acceptance requires either the donor's written consent or, absent that, a documented cy pres-style Board finding with counsel input.

4-31

In-Kind Donation Policy

CPD-CBPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Finance / Stewardship Committees

Before accepting donated goods or equipment, screens: is it actually useful to a current program; can the Council realistically store or maintain it; and — critically — is it intended for the Council's own use or for installation on CPD property? Anything destined for the park (a donated bench, signage, planting materials) requires the same CPD acceptance process as monetary park gifts (4-29, 4-55) before it's installed, regardless of who physically delivers it.

4-34

Sponsorship Policy

CPD-CBPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Finance / Communications / CPD Liaison

Sets sponsorship tiers and benefits (recognition on materials, event signage, website listing) in a way that never implies the sponsor is endorsed by, or has any special standing with, the Chicago Park District — every sponsorship benefit is a Council benefit, not a CPD one. Prohibits representations that a sponsorship buys naming rights to CPD property (that's 4-36's territory) or any preferential treatment in the Council's advisory positions.

4-35

Corporate Sponsorship Standards

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Board / Finance Committee

Screening criteria the Board applies before accepting a sponsorship above a set dollar threshold: reputational fit with a community/environmental/cultural mission; no direct promotion of alcohol, cannabis, or tobacco products given the youth and family programming this Council supports; no material environmental-harm conflicts given the dune/habitat stewardship mission; and, always, deference to whatever CPD's own sponsorship/branding rules require for anything touching park property.

4-36

Naming / Recognition Policy

CPD-CBPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Board / CPD Liaison

States plainly, for internal and external audiences alike: this Council cannot independently grant naming rights, memorial plaques, or permanent recognition on any Chicago Park District property — 63rd Street Beach, the Beach House, or any feature within it. Any naming/recognition proposal touching CPD property is routed through the Park Supervisor/Area Manager and CPD's own approval process before the Council makes any commitment to a donor or sponsor.

Fundraising Policy

4-41

Illinois Attorney General Charity Registration File (CO-1/CO-2 or successor)

LEG-CProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Treasurer / Counsel

Illinois charitable-solicitation registration applies once solicitation/receipt activity crosses the state's current threshold — CPD guidance specifically flags checking this. Per the Illinois Solicitation for Charity Act (225 ILCS 460) as cited in CPD's current PAC Guidelines: PACs that intend to solicit and receive more than $15,000 for charitable purposes during any 12-month period ending December 31 must register with the Illinois Attorney General's Office as a charitable organization prior to any solicitation of funds. This file exists to hold the applicability determination itself (with the date it was checked and the running 12-month total against this $15,000 threshold) and, if registration is required, the completed CO-1/CO-2 or current-successor filing. Re-checked annually regardless of the prior year's conclusion, since activity levels change.

4-62

Dissolution / Fund Disposition Worksheet

CPD-RIL-RProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Treasurer / Secretary / Counsel

Kept as a ready template, activated only if the Council ever disbands or is suspended. Per current CPD guidance, upon suspension or disbanding the Council's permission to raise or spend funds solicited on CPD's behalf is suspended immediately, and its fiscal agent (if any) is notified to safeguard the funds; if the Council is not reinstated or a new PAC is not formed within 12 months, individual donors whose unspent restricted contributions in the preceding 12 months total $2,000 or more may either have those donations returned or redirect them, with the remaining balance going to CPD. Walks through, in order: notifying CPD; freezing or spending down the bank account (or fiscal sponsor account) consistent with any restricted-fund obligations still outstanding (4-46) and the $2,000 donor-refund rule above; ensuring any remaining unrestricted funds are distributed consistent with Bylaws Article 30 and Illinois not-for-profit dissolution requirements (typically to another 501(c)(3) with a related mission, never to individuals); and filing Illinois Articles of Dissolution. Cross-referenced to the broader Disbanding / Dissolution Compliance Packet in Phase 5.
