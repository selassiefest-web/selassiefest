Legal & Compliance

Bylaws Articles 18-20 set the framework: ethics and conflict-of-interest disclosure, records/transparency/retention, and complaint resolution. Phase 5 is the operational layer underneath — including the two memoranda most likely to be mis-stated in any hurried nonprofit-formation effort: exactly how the Open Meetings Act and the Freedom of Information Act do and don't apply here.

Working document, not legal advice. This page is part of a governance/compliance planning library for the proposed 63rd Street Beach Park Advisory Council. Several documents below (5-15 through 5-18 especially) describe current, general Chicago Park District guidance on legal status — they are not a substitute for advice from qualified counsel on this Council's specific facts, and nothing here should be read as confirming that CPD has recognized a separate 63rd Street Beach PAC. That threshold question — Gate 0 — is still open. Every document is labeled Proposed for that reason.

CPD-RIL-RBPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

5-15

Open Meetings Act Applicability Memorandum

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Governance Committee / Counsel

Get this exactly right — it corrects a common assumption. Current Chicago Park District PAC Guidelines expressly state that Park Advisory Councils are not subject to the Illinois Open Meetings Act (OMA). That is a real, current CPD position, not an interpretation this Council is making up. This memorandum exists specifically so the Bylaws and every governance document in this library state that position accurately, rather than incorrectly asserting OMA coverage the Council does not actually have — and rather than swinging to the opposite, equally wrong conclusion that "not OMA-covered" means "no transparency obligations at all."

The correct position, and the one this Council adopts: OMA does not legally apply to this Council under current CPD guidance, so no OMA-specific notice period, closed-session rule, or minutes-publication timeline is legally mandatory here. Instead, the Council adopts a voluntary transparency standard (5-16) that in practice meets or exceeds what OMA would require of a covered public body — because good governance, not a statute, is the reason for doing it. This memorandum should be re-checked any time Illinois OMA is amended or CPD revises its PAC Guidelines on this point (see the Version-Control Notes in the source master checklist).

5-16

Voluntary Open-Meeting Transparency Standard

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Governance Committee

Because 5-15 establishes OMA doesn't legally apply, this standard is what the Council actually commits to instead — voluntarily, and describable to CPD, members, and the public as exactly that: a voluntary practice, never as a statutory OMA compliance claim. In practice: meetings are open to the public per Bylaws Article 12; the annual meeting calendar (Phase 6, 6-09) is published in advance; agendas are posted per the current CPD notice requirement (Phase 6, 6-10); minutes are drafted, approved, and posted (Phase 6, 6-12) within a set number of days after each meeting; and a public-comment period (Phase 6, 6-15) appears on every regular agenda. This standard is reviewed annually and may be strengthened, but should never be described using OMA's own statutory vocabulary ("closed session," "OMA-compliant") since that vocabulary doesn't actually apply here.

5-17

FOIA Applicability / Public-Records Status Memorandum

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Governance Committee / Counsel

Get this exactly right — the status is genuinely undetermined, not a flat yes/no. Unlike OMA (5-15), current CPD materials reviewed for this library do not affirmatively state that an independent PAC is, or is not, itself a public body subject to the Illinois Freedom of Information Act (FOIA). This memorandum should never assert "the Council is subject to FOIA" or "the Council is exempt from FOIA" as a flat legal conclusion — that determination depends on facts this document doesn't resolve (the final corporate/CPD-relationship structure, the degree of any CPD funding or delegated authority, and how Illinois courts have treated comparable volunteer advisory bodies), and can shift if the Gate 0 (Phase 0) determination or the fiscal structure decision (Phase 4, 4-01) changes the Council's relationship to CPD.

Pending that determination, this memorandum records the working position: FOIA status is unresolved, counsel should be consulted before either asserting or denying FOIA coverage in response to a specific request, and — regardless of the eventual legal answer — the Council commits to the FOIA-informed voluntary practice in 5-18 so that "we're not sure if FOIA applies" never functions as an excuse to be less transparent than a covered public body would have to be. Re-evaluate this memorandum whenever the corporate structure, CPD relationship, or applicable case law changes.

5-18

Public Information / Records Request Procedure (FOIA-informed)

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Records Officer

A simple public-facing intake (5-48 in the Templates Library, Phase 11) that borrows FOIA's structure — written request, defined response window (this Council targets 5 business days), and a short written response identifying what's being provided and what's being withheld and why — without claiming this is a statutory FOIA response. Requests touching genuinely sensitive categories (individual donor identities, minors' information, active legal-hold material) may be narrowed or declined with a written explanation; routine requests for agendas, approved minutes, or financial summaries should essentially never be declined.

5-20

Meeting Recording Consent Procedure

CPD-RProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Secretary / Meeting Chair

Implements Bylaws Article 19's recording-consent clause and current CPD guidance: any proposed audio/video recording of a meeting is announced at the start of the meeting, and if any attendee objects, the recording does not proceed (or is stopped for that portion of the meeting). 5-22

Conflict of Interest Policy

CPD-RIL-RProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Governance Committee

Implements Bylaws Article 18 in operational detail. Requires written disclosure (Phase 3, 3-12) of any financial, family, or business interest that could reasonably affect an officer's or director's judgment on a Council matter; requires recusal from discussion and voting (not just voting) on any matter where a disclosed interest exists; requires the recusal itself to be recorded in the minutes (3-13); and applies without exception regardless of how small the transaction or how well-intentioned the interested party.

5-23

Related-Party Transaction Procedure

IL-RBPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Governance / Finance Committees

Any contract or transaction involving an officer, director, or their immediate family or business (a vendor contract with a board member's company, for example) requires: advance written disclosure, comparison against at least one arm's-length alternative or quote where feasible, approval by disinterested officers/directors only, and documentation of all of the above retained in the Corporate Records Book (5-07) — not merely a conflict disclosure on file, but an actual comparative record showing the terms were fair to the Council.

5-24

Code of Ethics

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Governance Committee

A short values statement, not a rulebook (the rules live in the specific policies around it): integrity in financial and public representations; public service ahead of personal or organizational benefit; honest, sourced communication about beach conditions and CPD operations (never inventing or exaggerating either); impartial treatment of all community members regardless of how long they've been involved; and stewardship of both the beach and the Council's own resources as a public trust, not a private asset.

5-25

Supplemental Member Code of Conduct

CPD-RBPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Governance Committee

The current CPD PAC Code of Conduct (Phase 1, 1-02) is mandatory and controlling. It is not the only CPD conduct standard that applies: current CPD guidance separately requires compliance with a distinct "Facility Users Code of Conduct" — "PACs and PAC members shall comply with the Facility Users Code of Conduct and the Park Advisory Council Code of Conduct" — so members and officers are bound by both, not the PAC Code alone. This document only adds Council-specific expectations that don't conflict with either: respectful engagement with the longstanding beach drumming community and other cultural traditions (see Bylaws Article 24), no use of Council channels for personal business promotion, and a reminder that supplemental rules never override either CPD Code where they would conflict.

5-26

Whistleblower / Non-Retaliation Policy

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Governance Committee

Any officer, volunteer, or member may report a good-faith concern about financial mismanagement, governance failures, safety issues, or legal violations directly to the President, or to the Vice President if the concern involves the President, without fear of removal, exclusion, or informal retaliation. Reports are logged confidentially and referred to the full Board (or, if the concern involves a majority of the Board, escalated to CPD or Counsel) — retaliation against a good-faith reporter is itself a Code of Conduct violation subject to the Removal / Recall procedure (Phase 3, 3-27).

5-27

Anti-Harassment Policy

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Governance Committee

Prohibits harassment — including sexual harassment, intimidation, and hostile or abusive conduct — at meetings, events, volunteer activities, and in digital communications (email, social media, text). Reports route through the same Complaint Intake procedure (5-46) with confidentiality protections for the reporter, and severe or substantiated conduct triggers the Removal / Recall procedure regardless of the reported party's office or tenure.

5-28

Non-Discrimination & Equal Participation Policy

CPD-RBPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Implements Bylaws Article 27 operationally: membership, committee service, volunteer roles, and event participation are open without regard to race, ethnicity, national origin, religion, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, age, disability, or income, consistent with CPD's own inclusive-service expectations. Any participation barrier identified (physical, linguistic, financial) is referred to the Accessibility Committee (5-29) or Language Access Policy (5-30) rather than left unaddressed.

5-29

Accessibility / ADA Participation Policy

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Sets an accommodation-request contact and response process for Council-run meetings, communications, and events (distinct from CPD's own beach-facility accessibility, tracked in Phase 7); commits to accessible meeting locations and virtual-participation options (Bylaws Article 13) as a standing accommodation, not a special request; and requires digital materials to meet the Digital Accessibility Standard (Phase 9, 9-23).

5-30

Language Access Policy

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Communications / Accessibility

Identifies which materials get priority translation based on the community's actual language needs (starting with meeting notices and any safety-related public postings), sets a good-faith interpretation request process for meetings, and requires translated materials to go through a basic quality/accuracy review before publication rather than relying on unreviewed machine translation for anything safety-related.

5-31

Political Activity / Candidate Neutrality Policy

FED-RBPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Governance Committee

The Council, its funds, its name, its meeting space, and its communications channels are never used to endorse or oppose a candidate for public office, contribute to a campaign, or otherwise intervene in a partisan political campaign — a restriction that applies with particular force if the fiscal-structure decision (4-01) selects Path A (501(c)(3) status), where campaign intervention risks the exemption itself, but is adopted as Council policy either way. Officers and members remain free to engage in individual civic and political activity on their own time, using their own resources, provided they do not present it as a Council position.

5-32

Advocacy & Legislative Activity Policy

FED-CBPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Governance Committee / Counsel

Distinguishes ordinary, nonpartisan advocacy (asking an alderman's office for a capital improvement, testifying at a Park District board meeting about 63rd Street Beach) — which this Council may do freely and is arguably core to its mission — from formal lobbying, which carries specific recordkeeping and, if Path A (501(c)(3)) is selected in 4-01, statutory expenditure limits under federal tax law. Any activity that starts to resemble organized lobbying (repeated, resourced efforts to influence specific pending legislation) should be flagged to Counsel before it becomes a pattern.

5-33

Confidentiality Policy

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Governance / Records

Defines a short, specific list of what actually counts as confidential — individual membership application contact details, donor identities where requested, minors' information, active complaint files, and legal-hold material — and prohibits officers/volunteers from removing, copying, or forwarding it outside authorized Council systems, consistent with the CPD Code of Conduct's own data-handling expectations (Phase 3, 3-15). Everything not on this list is presumptively open, consistent with the Voluntary Transparency Standard (5-16).

5-34

Data Privacy Policy

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Data Steward / Governance

Governs what personal data the Council collects (membership applications, newsletter signups, donation and event records), why, how long it's kept (per 5-10), who can access it, and how it's disposed of. Data is collected only for the stated purpose it was gathered for — a membership application is not repurposed as an unrestricted mailing list, and event sign-in data is not shared with sponsors without separate consent. Paired with the public-facing Privacy Notice (Phase 9, 9-25).

5-35

Cybersecurity Policy

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Secretary / Technology Lead

Minimum controls for a volunteer-run organization with no dedicated IT staff: unique, strong passwords per account (no sharing a single login across multiple officers); multi-factor authentication enabled on email, banking, and cloud-storage accounts wherever the provider offers it; shared drives restricted to the minimum access each role actually needs; basic phishing awareness for anyone with financial or membership-data access; and regular backups of anything that would be catastrophic to lose. Any suspected compromise is reported immediately per the Incident Response Plan (5-38), not quietly worked around.

5-36

AI Use Policy

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Governance / Technology Lead

This Organization document library itself is a live example of what this policy governs — its first draft was produced with generative-AI assistance, under a named officer's direction, and is being published, reviewed, and corrected by the humans responsible for it rather than treated as self-authenticating. The policy this practice implies: no confidential or personal data (5-33, 5-34) may be entered into an AI tool that isn't specifically approved and covered by an appropriate data agreement; any AI-assisted content intended for public use (this library, social posts, official letters) is reviewed and approved by a responsible officer before publication, not published automatically; AI-assisted drafts are treated as drafts, explicitly and visibly, until that review happens (see the "Proposed" status pill on every document in this library); and material factual claims — CPD rules, legal requirements, dollar figures — are checked against a real source before being relied upon, exactly as the disclaimer on every page of this library already asks the reader to do.

5-37

Digital Access / Credential Management Policy

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Technology Lead

Every institutional account (email, website hosting, social media, cloud storage, banking portal) is owned by the Council, not by whichever individual happened to create it — registered under an institutional email address where the provider allows it, with access granted on a least-privilege basis (an Event Lead doesn't need banking-portal access), MFA required, and a documented recovery contact who isn't the sole person with access to any given account. Access is revoked, not just "forgotten about," within 48 hours of an officer transition per the Digital Account Handover Record (Phase 3, 3-29).

5-38

Data Breach / Cyber Incident Response Plan

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Technology Lead / President

Drafted before the Council holds significant donor or personal data, not after an incident. Covers: immediate containment (revoke compromised credentials, isolate affected accounts); preservation of evidence before remediation destroys it; a decision process for whether/who to notify (affected individuals, CPD, law enforcement, Illinois's own data-breach notification requirements if triggered) with Counsel's input; vendor/platform coordination (bank, email provider, website host); and a post-incident review feeding back into the Enterprise Risk Register (5-43).

5-39

Contract Review & Signature Authority Policy

CPD-RIL-RProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

5-40

Standard "PAC Is Independent of CPD" Contract Clause

CPD-RBPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Governance Committee / Counsel

Standard language for insertion into any Council-signed agreement:

"[Council name] is an independent, volunteer community organization and is not an agency, department, or authorized representative of the Chicago Park District ('CPD'). Used verbatim (or as adapted by Counsel for a specific agreement) in every sponsorship, vendor, partnership, and MOU template across this library (Phase 4, Phase 8, Phase 9).

5-41

Insurance Review Policy

CPD-CBPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Risk Lead / Treasurer

Requires a coverage review — not just a renewal-date check — before any material event, before onboarding volunteers into higher-risk roles (stewardship workdays, youth programming), and before signing any contract with an indemnification clause. Confirms explicitly what current coverage does and doesn't include (general liability, event-specific riders, D&O, cyber), and identifies any CPD-provided benefit the Council may already be covered under versus what it must obtain independently (Insurance Register, Phase 4, 4-48).

5-42

Risk Management Policy

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Board / Risk Lead

Assigns risk ownership across the Council's actual risk categories — beach/safety, event, financial, environmental, cyber, and reputational — to specific committees or officers, requires each owner to feed identified risks into the Enterprise Risk Register (5-43), and sets an escalation path (owner → Risk Lead → full Board → CPD/Counsel as needed) so a risk doesn't sit unaddressed simply because it doesn't cleanly belong to one committee.

5-43

Enterprise / PAC Risk Register

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Risk Lead

Fields: Risk Description · Category · Likelihood (Low/Med/High) · Impact (Low/Med/High) · Current Mitigation · Owner · Next Review Date

A living document, reviewed quarterly at minimum, that should read as slightly uncomfortable — a risk register with nothing "High" on it after a real review is more likely incomplete than genuinely risk-free.

5-44

Volunteer Protection & Safety Policy

CPD-RBPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Volunteer Coordinator / Risk Lead

Requires role-appropriate training before a volunteer takes on a task, direct supervision for anything beyond routine cleanup/hospitality work, a hard list of tasks volunteers may never perform regardless of enthusiasm (any lifeguard/rescue function, unauthorized natural-area work, electrical/structural repairs), a simple incident-reporting channel (Phase 6, 6-30), and confirmation that CPD's current volunteer waiver/application process (Phase 1, 1-27; Phase 11, 11-59) is followed for every volunteer, one-day or recurring, before they begin.

5-45

Youth Protection Policy

CPD-RBPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Youth / Volunteer Leads

Applies to any Council-run activity involving minors. Requires CPD-approved background checks for anyone in an unsupervised or near-unsupervised role with youth (Phase 1, 1-29); a minimum adult-to-youth supervision ratio set before the activity, not improvised on the day; written parent/guardian consent and emergency contact information (Phase 8, 8-24) for every participating minor; a no-lone-adult-with-a-single-minor rule for any Council-run activity; and an incident-escalation path that treats any youth-safety concern as immediately reportable to the Youth Lead and, where warranted, to CPD and appropriate authorities — never handled quietly at the volunteer level.

5-46

Complaint Intake, Response & Escalation Procedure

CPD-RProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: President / Governance Committee

Implements Bylaws Article 20. Any complaint — from a member, volunteer, or member of the public, about governance, conduct, safety, or finances — receives a written acknowledgment within 5 business days and a substantive written response within 30 days, with a copy filed at the park per current CPD guidance; complaints involving an officer are reviewed by disinterested officers only. If the complainant is dissatisfied with the Council's response, current CPD guidance gives them the right to appear before the general membership to be heard. If the matter is still unresolved after that, the real escalation path outside the Council runs: written appeal to the Park Supervisor or Area Manager → shared with CPD's Department of Legislative and Community Affairs (LCA) → shared with the CPD-side body holding final authority over PAC complaints. That body is named the "PAC Governance Committee" in CPD's current (Feb. 2025) PAC Guidelines and the "Complaint Review Committee (CRC)" in the Jackson Park Advisory Council's own current bylaws — the two real sources reviewed while drafting this library disagree on the name, so confirm the current correct name directly with LCA before citing it externally. Under either name, its decision (in partnership with LCA) is final and binding, and it is not the same body as this Council's own internal Governance Committee (Phase 2, 2-34/2-37; Phase 3; Phase 6) — that is the Council's own committee for its own internal governance work, with no role in CPD's complaint process.

5-47

Complaint Case File / Resolution Record

CPD-RBPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Secretary / Governance

One file per complaint: the original complaint, the acknowledgment, any hearing or review notes, the final written resolution, and any resulting corrective action (5-49) — and, for any complaint escalated per 5-46, the CPD-side committee's final decision as well. Held to a stricter confidentiality standard than most records (5-33) and retained for 7 years after resolution per the Retention Schedule (5-10), given the potential future relevance to a removal proceeding (Phase 3, 3-27) or external inquiry.

A short, signed statement each of the three officers completes annually confirming (or flagging as incomplete) the core recurring obligations from the Compliance Matrix (5-01): CPD Annual Report filed, Illinois annual report filed, NFP status evidence current, background checks current for all officers, and the Records Retention Schedule followed for the year. 5-49

Compliance Exception / Corrective Action Log

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Governance / Compliance Lead

Fields: Issue · Source (missed deadline / CPD finding / audit / self-identified) · Owner · Corrective Action · Target Date · Closed Date

A missed filing or a CPD finding that's logged and fixed is a normal part of running a volunteer organization; one that's quietly ignored is how a small gap becomes a real problem. This log exists so nothing falls into the second category.

5-50

Disbanding / Dissolution Compliance Packet

CPD-RIL-RProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: President / Secretary / Treasurer

A maintained-but-inactive template implementing Bylaws Article 30, activated only if the Council actually disbands: written notice to CPD, notice to and settlement with the bank or fiscal sponsor (Phase 4, 4-01/4-02), a final financial report and disposition of any remaining or restricted funds consistent with donor intent, and the formal Illinois corporate dissolution filing (cross-referenced with the Fund Disposition Worksheet, Phase 4, 4-62). Having this ready in advance, even though nobody expects to need it, is itself a marker of a well-governed organization.
