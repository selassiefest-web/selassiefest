Operational & Administrative Manuals

Phase 6 is where those promises actually become procedures — plus the Council's records infrastructure, volunteer handbook, and emergency/continuity planning.

Working document, not legal advice. This page is part of a governance/compliance planning library for the proposed 63rd Street Beach Park Advisory Council. Nothing here should be read as confirming that CPD has recognized a separate 63rd Street Beach PAC — that threshold question, Gate 0, is still open. Every document is labeled Proposed for that reason.

6-01

PAC Binder — Physical In-Park Official File

CPD-RProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Secretary

The in-park compliance file CPD's current PAC Guidelines expect to exist at the recognized meeting location — distinct from, and narrower than, the permanent Corporate Records Book (Phase 5, 5-07). Contains, matching CPD's own required-contents list verbatim ("bylaws, financial statements, meetings agendas, minutes, sign in sheets, and evidence of Not-For-Profit status with the State of Illinois"): the current Bylaws (2-02) and NFP status evidence (5-04), the current CPD Code of Conduct (1-02), the last twelve months of agendas and approved minutes (6-12), recent Treasurer's Reports (4-14), and sign-in sheets/attendance records (6-13). Established the moment CPD recognition is confirmed (Phase 0/1), and self-audited quarterly against the CPD PAC Binder Compliance Checklist (Phase 5, 5-14).

6-02

Digital Governance Repository / Electronic Binder

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Secretary / Technology Lead

A cloud mirror of the PAC Binder plus everything the in-park binder can't hold: full corporate history, every policy, every event and grant file, and this Organization document library's source material. Role-based access — officers and committee chairs get what their role needs, not blanket access to everything (donor records, background-check details, and active legal-hold material stay restricted regardless of officer rank). This public library at /bbpac/organization/ is deliberately the public-facing front door onto this repository: what's published here is what the Council is comfortable with anyone reading; what's not published (personal data, in-progress drafts, sensitive logs) stays in the private repository even after this page-by-page rollout is complete.

6-03

Master Document Register

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Secretary / PMO Lead

The index of all 510 documents in this checklist — which, as it happens, is not an abstraction here: it's literally the data.js file powering the search catalog at /bbpac/organization/documents/, where every document's ID, title, classification, owner, and drafting status already lives as a maintained, machine-readable record. Updating that file each time a document is drafted, adopted, or revised is keeping this register current — there is no separate spreadsheet to reconcile against it.

6-04

Document Control & Versioning Procedure

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Records Officer

Every governing document carries a visible status (Proposed, Adopted, Superseded), a version marker, and a single authoritative-copy location — for public documents, that location is this library; for internal-only records, the Digital Governance Repository (6-02). This matters most at the exact moment it's easiest to get wrong: when an officer is working from a printed or downloaded copy that quietly became outdated after a later revision. The rule is simple and absolute — the version in the authoritative location always controls, and any copy elsewhere (a printout, an email attachment, a personal drive) is treated as potentially stale until checked. When a document is superseded, the prior version is archived, not deleted, so the Council can always answer "what did this policy say when that decision was made," and the new version's adoption date is recorded per the Policy Adoption / Amendment Procedure (Phase 2, 2-38).

6-05

File Naming & Folder Taxonomy Standard

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Records Officer

A folder structure mirroring this checklist's own 12 phases (Governance, Board, Finance, Legal, Operations, Stewardship, Programming, Communications, Strategy) plus year-based subfolders for meetings, events, and grants, adopted before the Digital Governance Repository accumulates enough files that retrofitting a structure becomes painful — restructuring a repository with hundreds of files already in it is far more disruptive than adopting a sensible structure on day one. File names follow a consistent pattern: YYYY-MM-DD_DocumentID_ShortTitle_vN (e.g. 2027-03-14_4-12_Annual-Operating-Budget_v2), so a file's date, source document, and version are legible from the name alone without opening it. The Records Officer audits the taxonomy annually and folds in any ad hoc folder that's sprung up outside the standard structure.

6-08

Meeting Operations Manual

CPD-RBPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Secretary / Parliamentarian

The end-to-end procedure implementing Bylaws Articles 12-13 and the Voluntary Open-Meeting Transparency Standard (Phase 5, 5-16). Every regular meeting follows the same sequence so no step depends on any one officer's memory:

  1. Calendar published. The Annual Meeting Calendar (6-09) is posted every January, or immediately upon CPD recognition if that happens mid-year.
  2. Notice issued. At least 14 days before a regular meeting (48 hours for an emergency or special meeting), notice goes out through every channel on the Meeting Notice Checklist (6-10) — the posted location, the website, and the mailing list — and proof of posting is saved before the meeting, not reconstructed after.
  3. Agenda finalized. Committee chairs and officers submit items to the Secretary by the deadline in the Agenda Development Procedure (6-11); the President and Secretary lock the final order, always including the Public Comment slot (6-15) and any pending CPD item.
  4. Quorum confirmed. Before the meeting is gaveled into business (not after a vote is already underway), the Secretary counts attendance against the quorum threshold in Bylaws Article 14 and records the count in the minutes.
  5. Recording consent, if applicable. If any recording is planned, the Chair announces it at the start per the Recording Consent Procedure (Phase 5, 5-20); if any participant present objects, no recording happens, full stop — this is checked before the meeting starts, not treated as a formality to skip.
  6. Business conducted. Motions, seconds, and votes follow Robert's Rules per Bylaws Article 28; conflicts of interest are disclosed and recused per Article 18 as they come up, not retroactively.
  7. Minutes drafted and approved. The Secretary drafts minutes per the Minutes Preparation Standard (6-12) and circulates them for correction before they're formally approved at the following meeting.
  8. Follow-through. New action items go into the Action Item Tracker (6-16) that same week; any formal decision is logged in the Decision Register (6-17); approved minutes are posted per the Public Document Publication Schedule (Phase 9, 9-26).

This Manual is adopted by the Board and reviewed annually alongside the Annual Governance Review Checklist (Phase 2, 2-40) — if a step in practice consistently gets skipped or reordered, that's a signal to amend this Manual to match reality, not a reason to quietly let the gap persist.

6-10

Meeting Notice Checklist & Proof-of-Posting File

CPD-RProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Secretary

Checklist, completed per meeting: Notice posted at the meeting location at least 14 days before a regular meeting or election · Notice posted on the website/social channels · Notice emailed to the mailing list · For an emergency or special meeting, a minimum 48-hour notice used instead, per current CPD guidance · Screenshot or photo retained as proof-of-posting, dated

If a meeting is cancelled, notice of the cancellation is posted immediately and the Park Supervisor or Area Manager is informed.

6-11

Agenda Development Procedure

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: President / Secretary

A predictable rhythm so agenda-building isn't reinvented each meeting: committee chairs and officers submit items to the Secretary by a set deadline (five days before the meeting is the working default); the President and Secretary finalize the order, grouping routine, low-discussion items (financial report, standing committee updates, approval of prior minutes) into a consent section that can be approved together, and reserving real discussion time for substantive business rather than letting it get squeezed at the end. Late items may still be raised as "new business" at the meeting itself but cannot be voted on that same night unless the Board waives notice by unanimous consent — this protects members who planned around the posted agenda from being surprised by a vote they didn't know was coming. The Public Comment slot (6-15) and any pending CPD staff item always appear on the agenda when applicable, and the final agenda is posted per the Notice Checklist (6-10) before the meeting, not handed out at the door.

6-12

Minutes Preparation & Approval Standard

CPD-RBPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Secretary

Minutes are a record of decisions and actions, not a transcript — they record, at minimum: date/time/location, attendance and quorum confirmation, every motion with its exact wording, maker, second, and vote count (including any abstention), any conflict-of-interest recusal (Phase 5, 5-22) with the reason stated, officer/committee reports (by reference to the written report, not restated in full), and action items assigned. What minutes deliberately do not record: verbatim debate, individual members' side comments, or characterizations of tone — the goal is an accurate, defensible record of what the body decided, not a narrative of how the conversation felt.

The Secretary circulates draft minutes to the Board within ten days of the meeting for correction; corrections are proposed as amendments at the next meeting, not negotiated privately beforehand; once approved, minutes are posted publicly per the Public Document Publication Schedule (Phase 9, 9-26) — this posting step is the specific fulfillment of the Voluntary Open-Meeting Transparency Standard's (Phase 5, 5-16) "minutes are posted within a set number of days" commitment, and a copy goes in the PAC Binder (6-01).

6-13

Sign-In / Attendance Data Handling Procedure

CPD-RBPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Secretary

Sign-in sheets exist for one purpose — proving attendance and quorum for CPD compliance and the Council's own records — and are designed to collect the minimum needed for that purpose: name and, as an optional separate line, an email opt-in for the mailing list. They never collect phone numbers, home addresses, or other personal data with no compliance purpose, consistent with the Data Privacy Policy's minimization principle (Phase 5, 5-34). Original sign-in sheets go into the PAC Binder (6-01) after each meeting; a scanned copy goes into the Digital Governance Repository (6-02). Access to the underlying names/emails (as opposed to a public headcount reported in the minutes) is limited to the Secretary and Membership Lead, and the list is never shared with a sponsor, partner, or third party without the consent procedure in the Mailing List Consent / Subscription Record (Phase 9, 9-14).

6-14

Virtual / Hybrid Meeting SOP

CPD-RBPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Secretary / Technology Lead

Implements Bylaws Article 13. When a meeting is hybrid or virtual: the platform and dial-in number are included in the meeting notice (6-10); per current CPD guidance, the agenda and the prior meeting's minutes are made available at least three days before the virtual meeting takes place, and CPD staff are notified that the meeting will be held virtually; remote attendees count toward quorum and may vote using the platform's polling/hand-raise feature, logged in the minutes the same as an in-person vote; a technical failure affecting a material portion of the meeting is noted in the minutes and may trigger a re-vote at the next meeting; and any recording follows the same consent procedure (Phase 5, 5-20) regardless of format.

6-15

Public Comment / Community Input Procedure

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Governance Committee

A standing agenda item at every regular meeting, open to any member of the public (not just voting members) — this is the document that fulfills the Voluntary Open-Meeting Transparency Standard's (Phase 5, 5-16) "public-comment period appears on every regular agenda" commitment. Speakers sign up on arrival or are recognized in the order they raise a hand; each gets 2-3 minutes, with a total time cap for the whole comment period that the Chair may extend at their discretion if turnout warrants it. Comments are heard respectfully and logged in the minutes by topic (not transcribed verbatim); the Board is not required to respond substantively in the moment, since public comment is for listening, not for immediate debate, but any comment raising something needing a real answer is followed up per the Public Contact & Response Service Standard (Phase 9, 9-30) within that policy's target response window. The Chair may limit repetitive comments on the same point but may not exclude a viewpoint because the Board disagrees with it.

6-22

Officer / Committee Handbook

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Governance Committee

A practical, plain-language companion to the Bylaws and Officer Position Descriptions (Phase 3, 3-01), written for someone who has never served on a nonprofit board before — the Bylaws state the rule, this Handbook explains what actually doing the job looks like month to month. Covers, at minimum:

  • What a Board report actually looks like. A committee report to the full Board is one page or less: what happened since the last meeting, any decision the Board needs to make (with a specific ask, not just an update), and any budget impact — not a narrative of every conversation the committee had.
  • How an approval actually moves. Any spending, contract, or public commitment above an officer's individual authority follows the Delegation of Authority Matrix (Phase 2, 2-36) — the Handbook walks through two or three realistic examples (a $400 stewardship-supply purchase; a $3,000 event sponsorship) so a new officer can see which tier applies before making a costly assumption.
  • Meeting cadence and preparation. What to actually do in the week before a regular meeting: submit agenda items by the Agenda Development Procedure's (6-11) deadline, review the prior draft minutes, check the Action Item Tracker (6-16) for anything assigned to you.
  • Transitions. What "handing off the role" concretely means — see the Officer Transition Checklist (Phase 3, 3-28) and Digital Account & Credential Handover Record (Phase 3, 3-29) — walked through here in narrative form rather than just as a checklist to fill in blind.
  • Where to go when something isn't covered. When a situation doesn't map cleanly onto any existing policy, the answer is to raise it with the President or the Governance Committee before acting unilaterally, not to guess.

Reviewed and updated whenever the Bylaws or Delegation of Authority Matrix change, so this Handbook never quietly drifts out of sync with the documents it's supposed to translate.

6-23

Committee Handbook

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Governance Committee

Companion to the Standing Committee Charter Set (Phase 2, 2-37) — the operating rules that apply across every committee regardless of subject, so each new chair isn't reinventing basic process:

  • Requesting budget. A committee submits a written request to the Treasurer at least two weeks before the meeting where budget approval is needed, stating amount, purpose, and which line of the Annual Operating Budget (Phase 4, 4-12) it falls under.
  • Reporting to the Board. Every standing committee reports at every regular meeting, even if the report is "no update this month" — silence from a committee for multiple consecutive meetings is itself a signal the Governance Committee should follow up on.
  • Bringing in outside volunteers. A committee may recruit volunteers for its own work but cannot commit the Council to an ongoing partnership, sponsorship, or contract without Board approval under the Delegation of Authority Matrix (Phase 2, 2-36).
  • Membership and quorum. Each committee's charter states its own membership size and quorum for internal votes; absent a charter provision, a committee acts by majority of its current members.
  • Sunset review. Every committee's charter and continued existence is revisited on the schedule set in the Policy Sunset / Periodic Review Schedule (6-36) — a committee doesn't survive by default just because no one raised the question.
6-24

Volunteer Handbook

CPD-RBPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Volunteer Coordinator

The single document every volunteer actually reads before a workday or event shift. Organized around the questions a first-time volunteer actually has:

  • "Which form do I need?" There is no single generic "CPD Volunteer Application" — the right one depends on the role. Officers, anyone handling Council finances, and anyone working with youth complete the Long Term Adult Volunteer Application and a CPD background check before starting (Phase 1, 1-27 through 1-29). Volunteers 16 and younger complete the Long Term Minor Volunteer Application. A single-day helper at one event or workday, with no finance/youth responsibility, completes only the shorter one-day Volunteer Waiver and Release before that shift.
  • "What am I actually allowed to do?" Volunteer tasks are always support tasks: setup, check-in, cleanup, handing out information, staffing a table, walking a stewardship crew through an authorized task. Volunteers never direct CPD staff or lifeguards, never perform a rescue or medical response unless separately certified and acting under an authorized program, and never do habitat work (planting, removal, signage) without the specific written CPD authorization referenced in Phase 7. When in doubt, the answer is to ask the shift lead, not to guess and proceed.
  • "How do I check in and out?" Sign in with the shift lead at arrival (name, time, role) and sign out at departure — the same sign-in principle as Council meetings (6-13): only what's needed to confirm who was present and for how long.
  • "What if something goes wrong?" Any injury, hazard, or safety concern is reported immediately using the Incident Reporting & Escalation SOP (6-30) — call 911 or CPD/311 as the situation requires, then tell the shift lead; volunteers never handle the fix themselves.
  • "What's expected of my conduct?" The Supplemental Member Code of Conduct (Phase 5, 5-25) and CPD's own Park Advisory Council Code of Conduct and Facility Users Code of Conduct all apply to volunteer shifts, not just to officers at meetings.
  • "Does this count for anything?" Volunteer hours are tracked (6-26) and recognized under the Volunteer Recognition Policy (Phase 8, 8-36) — service is noted, not assumed unrecorded.

New volunteers complete the orientation in 6-25 before their first shift; this Handbook is the reference they keep afterward.

Volunteer Orientation / Training Curriculum

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Volunteer Coordinator

Short (20-30 minute) modules delivered before a volunteer's first shift, in this order: (1) who the Council is and isn't — the CPD/PAC independence boundary, so a new volunteer never accidentally represents themselves as CPD staff; (2) basic beach safety awareness — where lifeguards are stationed, what the flag colors mean, who to call for what; (3) natural-area rules, for any role touching the dunes (Phase 7); (4) the specific tasks for that day's role; (5) the Code of Conduct expectations (Phase 5, 5-25); and (6) how to escalate an incident (6-30). Delivered in person for event-day crews, by short video for recurring roles, or as a written packet when neither is practical — the content covered matters more than the delivery format, and completion is logged in 6-26 either way.

6-27

Emergency Operations Plan

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Risk / Emergency Committee

Defines what Council volunteers actually do during severe weather, an injury, a missing person, or a major event disruption — and, just as importantly, what they don't. Every scenario below follows the same controlling principle: this plan is explicitly subordinate to CPD staff and 911/public-safety authority at every point; Council volunteers support communication, they never substitute for a lifeguard, medical responder, or CPD employee.

  • Severe weather. When CPD/NWS conditions trigger a warning (Phase 7, 7-26's Severe Weather Beach Activity Protocol), the Event Lead or on-duty officer directs attendees toward posted CPD shelter guidance and pauses or ends the activity — volunteers do not make an independent call to continue an event against posted conditions.
  • Injury. A volunteer who witnesses an injury calls 911 for anything beyond minor first aid, notifies the nearest lifeguard or CPD staff if the injury is water- or beach-related, and stays with the person only to provide comfort and information to responders — not to render medical care beyond basic first aid unless separately certified.
  • Missing person. Handled per the Lost Person / Lost Child Escalation Protocol (6-31) — immediate notification to lifeguards/CPD/911 first, volunteer search support only as directed by responders.
  • Major event disruption (crowd issue, equipment failure, unauthorized disturbance). The Event Lead assesses whether the event can safely continue, escalates to CPD staff or 911 if there's any safety concern, and communicates any pause or cancellation to attendees using the Emergency / Safety Post Approval Protocol (Phase 9, 9-19) for any public-facing message.

This plan is reviewed and walked through with volunteer leads before every event of meaningful scale (Phase 8), not filed away and assumed remembered from the prior season.

6-28

Crisis Management Plan

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: President / Risk Lead

Governance-level response to a serious crisis that isn't a physical-safety emergency: a financial irregularity, a leadership scandal, a data breach (Phase 5, 5-38), or a reputational incident. Unlike the Emergency Operations Plan (6-27), which handles what happens at the beach in the moment, this plan handles what the Board does in the hours and days after.

  1. Convene. The President (or, if the President is implicated or unavailable, the Vice President) convenes an emergency Board meeting within 48 hours of the crisis becoming known, using the Virtual/Hybrid Meeting SOP (6-14) if an in-person meeting can't be arranged that fast.
  2. Contain and preserve. Before any public statement, the Board directs preservation of relevant records (Legal Hold Procedure, Phase 5, 5-12) and, for a suspected financial irregularity, immediate suspension of the individual's signing/spending authority pending review (Phase 4's internal controls).
  3. Decide who speaks. Only the President or a Board-designated spokesperson communicates publicly during the crisis, per the Media Relations / Spokesperson Policy (Phase 9, 9-16) — no officer freelances a public statement, even a well-intentioned one.
  4. Decide when to bring in Counsel or notify CPD. Any crisis involving suspected fraud, a legal claim, or a matter CPD's Code of Conduct requires reporting is escalated to qualified counsel and to CPD's Department of Legislative and Community Affairs promptly — not held internally in hopes it resolves quietly.
  5. Debrief. Once resolved, the Board conducts a written after-action review: what happened, what the response got right or wrong, and what changes to policy or practice follow — filed with the Compliance Exception / Corrective Action Log (Phase 5, 5-49).
6-29

Business Continuity / Disaster Recovery Plan

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Secretary / Treasurer / Technology Lead

If the Council loses access to its primary records, bank, or leadership (illness, sudden resignation, a cyber incident) all at once, this plan says exactly who has emergency access to what, so recovery doesn't depend on improvising under pressure:

  • Records access. At least two officers (never one) hold recovery access to the Digital Governance Repository (6-02), per the Digital Access / Credential Management Policy (Phase 5, 5-37) — no single point of failure on institutional records.
  • Financial access. The bank account signatory chain (Phase 4, 4-08) always names at least two authorized signers, so a single officer's unavailability never freezes the Council's ability to pay an obligation or access funds.
  • Minimum viable operations. If a majority of officers become unavailable simultaneously, the surviving officer(s) and the Governance Committee convene within two weeks to appoint interim replacements per the Vacancy Appointment / Special Election Packet (Phase 3, 3-25), rather than letting meetings and filings lapse indefinitely.
  • Communications continuity. Institutional social media and email accounts remain accessible via the credential-recovery contacts named in the Digital Account & Credential Handover Record (Phase 3, 3-29), not tied to any one departed officer's personal recovery email or phone.

Tested annually via a tabletop walkthrough ("what would we actually do if the Treasurer and Secretary both left tomorrow") rather than written once and shelved.

6-30

Incident Reporting & Escalation SOP

CPD-RBPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Risk Lead

Any volunteer or officer who observes a safety hazard, injury, or incident follows the same sequence, every time:

  1. Call first, document second. 911 for any emergency (injury, water rescue, missing person, fire); CPD or 311 for a non-emergency facility issue (damaged equipment, a safety hazard that isn't actively harming someone); the on-duty lifeguard directly for a water-safety concern. The call happens before anything else — do not stop to write notes while something urgent is unfolding.
  2. Stay within your role. Never attempt a fix, rescue, or repair yourself unless you are specifically trained and currently authorized for that exact task (a certified lifeguard volunteering through an authorized CPD program, not through the Council, is the only case where a "volunteer" might render aid, and even then under that program's authority, not the Council's).
  3. Document after the fact. Once the immediate situation is handled by the appropriate authority, complete the Incident Report Form (Phase 11, 11-55): what was observed, who was called, when, and the outcome.
  4. Notify the Risk Lead within 24 hours for any incident involving injury, a near-miss, or property damage, even if it was fully resolved on-site — pattern-spotting across many small incidents is often how a real hazard gets caught before it causes a serious one.

Reported incidents are logged and reviewed at least quarterly by the Risk Lead against the Enterprise / PAC Risk Register (Phase 5, 5-43).

6-31

Lost Person / Lost Child Escalation Protocol

CPD-RBPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Risk / Volunteer Leads

For a missing child specifically, the sequence is non-negotiable and does not wait to see if the child turns up on their own:

  1. Call 911 immediately. Not after a few minutes of looking around — immediately, every time, without exception for a reported missing child.
  2. Notify the nearest lifeguard and CPD staff in the same breath, since a beach setting means water is always a possibility.
  3. Support the search only as directed by responders — spreading an accurate description, watching a specific assigned area, helping keep the family calm and informed — never launching an independent, undirected search that could waste responder time cross-checking who's already covered what area, or that pulls a volunteer's attention away from other event responsibilities.
  4. Stay with the family if one volunteer can be spared for it, as the point of continuous contact and reassurance while official responders work.

For a missing adult (no immediate danger indicators), the same escalation order applies but with slightly more room for judgment — lifeguards/CPD/911 are still notified promptly, especially if water involvement is possible. This protocol is walked through out loud with all event-day volunteers before any event with meaningful attendance, not just handed out as a page to read.

6-32

Lost Property Referral Protocol

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Operations Lead

Found property is turned over to CPD staff or the Beach House front desk the same day it's found, not held informally by a volunteer "just in case someone comes back for it." A brief note is made in the Issue & Escalation Log (6-20) only if the item is high-value or the finder needs a record of the handoff — routine items (a towel, sunglasses, a toy) don't need individual documentation. This simple rule avoids both liability (the Council is not equipped to secure or verify ownership of found property) and the appearance of the Council operating an unofficial, unregulated lost-and-found alongside CPD's own.

6-33

Vendor / Partner File Standard

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Operations / Treasurer

Required per vendor/partner file: Approval record · Signed contract/MOU (6-34) · Certificate of insurance (6-35) · Applicable permits · W-9/tax form · Invoices · Conflict-of-interest screen (Phase 5, 5-22/5-23) · Closeout note

Opened the moment a vendor or partner relationship is approved, not backfilled after invoices start arriving — a missing document in this list (most commonly, an insurance certificate no one asked for until an event was already booked) is one of the most preventable gaps in nonprofit vendor management, and this standard exists specifically to catch it before the relationship starts, not after.

6-37

Knowledge Transfer / Institutional Memory Manual

BPProposed — Pending Board Adoption & CPD Determination

Owner: Secretary / Past Leadership

The living answer to "why do we do it this way, and who do we actually call at CPD" — the institutional memory that otherwise walks out the door with whichever officer happens to be leaving. Built gradually over the Council's first year and updated at every officer transition, covering:

  • Recurring relationships. Who the current Park Supervisor and Area Manager are, which CPD department handles which kind of issue (LCA for registration/complaints, the Planning Department for capital projects, the Aquatics Unit for beach/water questions), and how those relationships have actually behaved in practice, not just their formal roles.
  • The reasoning behind past decisions. Not just what the Council decided, but why — a decision that looks arbitrary to a new officer two years later often made complete sense given a constraint (a CPD preference, a past incident, a scheduling conflict) that isn't otherwise written down anywhere.
  • Open initiatives. Anything a past officer started that outlived their term — a partially-negotiated partnership, a stewardship idea CPD hadn't yet approved, a grant application in progress — logged clearly enough that the next person can pick it up instead of it quietly dying.

This is, in a real sense, what the entire Organization document library exists to prevent: the Council's actual working knowledge living only in one departing officer's head.
