Phase 0 — Pre-Formation Gate

Jurisdiction, eligibility & scope. Nothing else in this library proceeds as a CPD-recognized 63rd Street Beach PAC until the question on this page is answered in writing by the Chicago Park District.

Resolve the threshold issue first

63rd Street Beach sits within Jackson (Andrew) Park, which already has a Chicago Park District-recognized Jackson Park Advisory Council (JPAC). Current CPD Advisory Council Guidelines recognize only one registered PAC per park. Before any formation step below is treated as more than a draft, the Founding Committee needs a written CPD determination on whether a separate 63rd Street Beach PAC is possible at all — or whether this becomes a sub-council/committee under JPAC, an adopted-site arrangement, or an independent "Friends of 63rd Street Beach" organization instead. This page is the living record of that process: ten documents, each status-tracked as it's drafted, sent, and answered — and its live, public, version-by-version backing record is the Version Log.

G0-01

Written CPD PAC Eligibility / Jurisdiction Determination

GATEProposed — Pending CPD Response

This is the single most important letter in the entire formation effort. It asks CPD, in writing, to tell the Founding Committee whether a separately registered 63rd Street Beach Park Advisory Council can exist at all, given that the beach sits inside Jackson (Andrew) Park and CPD's current Advisory Council Guidelines recognize only one registered PAC per park — a park that already has the Jackson Park Advisory Council (JPAC). Nothing downstream (Letter of Intent, membership drive, bylaws, elections, incorporation) should be represented publicly as CPD-recognized PAC formation until this comes back in writing.

Coordinate with Track A before sending. This letter asks a PAC-jurisdiction question (can there be a separate PAC, a JPAC sub-council, or an adopted-site arrangement). Track A's NP-01 and NP-16 ask a related but distinct park-classification question (can 63rd Street Beach become its own distinct park). Both come from the same organizing effort and both may land on overlapping CPD desks. Send them together, or reference each other explicitly, so CPD sees one coordinated ask rather than two separate requests about the same site.

Draft letter

To: [Park Supervisor / Area Manager — Jackson (Andrew) Park], Chicago Park District Cc: [CPD Department of Legislative and Community Affairs (LCA) contact, 312-742-2797] From: 63rd Street Beach Founding Committee Re: Request for Written Jurisdiction Determination — Proposed 63rd Street Beach Park Advisory Council We are a group of residents, longtime beachgoers, and community members organizing to formally support 63rd Street Beach through a registered Park Advisory Council. Before we take any further formation steps, we want to resolve a threshold question directly with the Park District rather than assume an answer. 63rd Street Beach is located within Jackson (Andrew) Park, which is currently represented by the Jackson Park Advisory Council. We understand that current CPD Advisory Council Guidelines generally recognize only one registered PAC per park. Given that, we are requesting a written determination on which of the following paths — or another CPD-recognized model we haven't identified — applies to 63rd Street Beach: 1. A separately registered 63rd Street Beach Park Advisory Council, distinct from JPAC; 2. A 63rd Street Beach committee or sub-council operating under the recognized Jackson Park Advisory Council; or 3. Another adopted-site or coordination model CPD already uses elsewhere in a similar situation. We have prepared a draft boundary and asset scope map (attached) identifying what we believe is 63rd Street Beach's distinct advisory scope — the beach and swim zone, the Beach House, the accessible beach walk and beach wheelchair access point, the non-motorized launch, the distance-swim area, and the dunes/natural area — and we would welcome the chance to review it with you or revise it based on your guidance. We are not seeking to duplicate or work around the Jackson Park Advisory Council's existing role, and we have prepared a parallel overlap analysis (attached) documenting JPAC's current recognition so any path forward coordinates with, rather than competes against, that council. For full transparency: our organizing committee is separately preparing a related but distinct request asking CPD to consider recognizing 63rd Street Beach as its own distinct park unit (not just a separately advised facility). We want CPD to have both questions in view together rather than receive them piecemeal, and we're glad to coordinate on whichever question CPD believes should be addressed first, or to combine them into a single review if that's more useful to your office. Could you let us know the appropriate next step, and whether a meeting with your office and/or JPAC leadership would be useful before we proceed further? We are glad to work on whatever timeline and process CPD prefers. Respectfully, [Founding Committee Chair name, contact information]

Once a response is received, its content and date go into G0-04 (CPD-Approved Representation Structure Memorandum) and this row's status updates from "Draft ready to send" to "Sent [date]" to "Answered [date] — see G0-04." Do not send this letter in isolation — see the coordination note above and confirm with the Track A lead that NP-01/NP-16 are either attached, already sent, or deliberately being held until after this response.

G0-02

63rd Street Beach PAC Boundary & Asset Scope Map

GATEProposed — Pending CPD Concurrence

Defines exactly what this council proposes to advise on — deliberately narrow, so it reads as a genuine site-specific complement to JPAC's park-wide role rather than a competing claim over Jackson Park as a whole.

Reconcile with Track A's NP-02 before either map goes to CPD. Track A is separately drafting a Jurisdiction & Boundary Map (NP-02) defining the proposed park boundary — a related but distinct purpose from this map's advisory scope. If the two are drawn independently they risk showing CPD two different lines around the same beach from the same organizing effort. Draft one shared base map and layer the PAC-scope and park-boundary distinctions on top of it, rather than producing two unrelated maps.

Proposed scope — included

  • The 63rd Street Beach sand and designated swim zone
  • The 63rd Street Beach House (historic structure, event/rental space, restrooms, showers)
  • The lakefront promenade directly fronting the beach
  • The accessible beach walk and beach wheelchair access point
  • The non-motorized boat launch
  • The designated distance-swim area
  • The dunes / natural area supporting migratory birds, immediately adjacent to the beach
  • Adjacent paths and parking directly serving beach visitors
  • The established cultural-programming footprint (including the longstanding beach drumming gathering area)

Explicitly excluded — remains Jackson Park / JPAC scope

  • The Jackson Park golf course and driving range
  • The Music Court, Perennial Garden, and Wooded Island / Osaka Garden
  • Jackson Park's broader lagoon, harbor, and inland pathway systems not directly serving the beach
  • Any other Jackson Park facility not listed as "included" above

The final version of this map is drawn and reviewed jointly with CPD once G0-01 gets a response, and the confirmed boundary becomes part of the CPD-Approved Representation Structure Memorandum (G0-04). A simple annotated aerial/plan-view graphic should accompany the text version published here once available.

G0-03

Jackson Park PAC Overlap / Conflict Analysis Memo

GATEProposed — Under Review

This is the crux of Gate 0. It exists so the Founding Committee — and CPD — can see the actual jurisdictional tension in writing rather than discover it mid-process.

The existing council

The Jackson Park Advisory Council (JPAC) is the currently recognized Park Advisory Council for Jackson (Andrew) Park, the park within which 63rd Street Beach is located. JPAC publishes its mission and governance information and its meeting minutes and records publicly, and, per current publicly available material, has an amended set of bylaws (most recently amended December 2, 2024). JPAC's recognized scope is Jackson Park as a whole, which on its face includes 63rd Street Beach.

The rule, verbatim — from both sides

This is not a paraphrase or an inference. CPD's own current (Feb. 14, 2025) Park Advisory Council Guidelines state: "The Park District recognizes only one (1) registered PAC per park." JPAC's own current bylaws state the identical rule from the other side: "JPAC acknowledges that each park shall have only one registered Park Advisory Council, and that there shall be no joint Park Advisory Councils or combined representation." Both the regulator and the incumbent council agree on the rule in writing; there is no reading of current source material under which a second, fully independent, separately-registered PAC for 63rd Street Beach is assumed to already exist or to be a foregone conclusion.

The tension

Current CPD Advisory Council Guidelines describe PACs as the community voice for parks, playgrounds, beaches, and natural areas — language that, read alone, could support a beach-specific council. The same guidelines state that CPD recognizes only one registered PAC per park. Both statements are simultaneously true in CPD's current materials, and they point in different directions for a site like 63rd Street Beach: distinctive enough (historic Beach House, accessible shoreline infrastructure, non-motorized launch, distance-swim area, dune/migratory-bird natural area, and a fifty-plus-year cultural drumming tradition) to arguably justify its own dedicated advisory body, while sitting inside a park that already has one.

Why this matters before any other formation step

If the Founding Committee proceeds with a Letter of Intent, membership drive, election, and Illinois incorporation as though a separate 63rd Street Beach PAC is already CPD-recognized, and CPD's eventual determination is "no, this remains under JPAC" or "this becomes a sub-council," the group risks having built governance infrastructure (bylaws, elected officers, a bank account) around a legal status it doesn't actually hold — and risks appearing to CPD, JPAC, and any outside audience (including elected officials) as having presumed an outcome that wasn't theirs to presume.

Three realistic outcomes

  • Separate recognition. CPD determines 63rd Street Beach's distinct facilities and programming justify a second, separately registered PAC coexisting with JPAC, likely with a coordination protocol (see G0-05).
  • Sub-council / committee under JPAC. This organizing effort continues as a 63rd Street Beach committee operating under JPAC's existing recognition, with its own working structure but not its own CPD registration.
  • Independent community organization. If CPD declines any PAC-affiliated structure, this effort continues as an independent "Friends of 63rd Street Beach" organization — still able to fundraise, volunteer, and advocate, but not as a CPD-recognized PAC, and without using PAC terminology in a way that implies that status.

Coordination with Track A (park recognition)

This memo resolves the tension between BBPAC and JPAC. It does not, by itself, resolve a second tension: Track B's G0-01 asks CPD a PAC-jurisdiction question while Track A's NP-01 and NP-16 ask a broader park-classification question. Both originate from the same Founding Committee and are addressed to overlapping parts of CPD (Park Supervisor/Area Manager/LCA for G0-01; Planning & Development and Law for NP-01/NP-16). Treat this as a fourth possible source of jurisdictional confusion, alongside the JPAC question above, and resolve it the same way: in writing, before either request goes out, not after CPD notices two related asks arriving separately.

Recommendation

Send G0-01 now, attach the boundary map (G0-02) and this memo, and request a direct conversation with CPD and, ideally, JPAC leadership before proceeding to Phase 1. Before sending, confirm with the Track A lead whether NP-01/NP-16 are going out at the same time, already sent, or intentionally held — and reference that status in the letter (see the disclosure paragraph now included in G0-01) so CPD receives one coordinated picture of this effort rather than two unlinked requests. Every public-facing description of this effort in the meantime — including this website — should reflect that recognition status is pending, not assumed.

Sections this MOU should contain once activated

G0-09

Official Name & Naming Consistency Memo

BPDraft Complete

Two names are in use right now, deliberately: "63rd Street Beach Park Advisory Council" is the formal working name used in CPD-facing correspondence and this governance library, matching the naming convention CPD's Advisory Council Guidelines expect. "Bongo Beach PAC" is this website's public-facing, community-friendly name, referencing the beach's longstanding drumming tradition — used on selassiefest.com's /bbpac/ section, its social presence, and community materials.

Naming rules going forward

  • Any CPD filing, letter, or registration document uses the formal working name.
  • Public materials may use "Bongo Beach PAC" but should make the formal name discoverable (e.g., in a footer or "about" line), so the two are never presented as different organizations.
  • Nothing in either name may state or imply that this organization is the Chicago Park District, an office of the Park District, or an already-CPD-recognized PAC while Gate 0 is unresolved.
  • Before Illinois incorporation, the Governance Lead must confirm the formal name's availability with the Illinois Secretary of State's business name search.
