How We're Organizing

Building a 63rd Street Beach Park Advisory Council, in the open

Every governance document, policy, and compliance step in this formation effort is published here as it's drafted — not after the fact. This is a living record, meant to be checked, questioned, and held accountable to.

This is a working document set, not legal advice, and not a claim of Chicago Park District recognition. Every document in this library is drafted for the proposed 63rd Street Beach Park Advisory Council and is labeled "Proposed" or "Registered" until the Chicago Park District (CPD) and this organization's own membership formally act on it. Nothing here should be read as evidence that CPD has recognized a separate 63rd Street Beach PAC — that question is open (see Gate 0 below) and current requirements should always be confirmed directly with CPD and qualified counsel before anything is filed, submitted, or formally adopted.

The threshold question: Gate 0

63rd Street Beach sits within Jackson (Andrew) Park, which already has a Chicago Park District-recognized Jackson Park Advisory Council (JPAC). Current CPD Advisory Council Guidelines generally recognize only one registered PAC per park. Before anything below is treated as more than a draft, we've asked CPD in writing whether a separate 63rd Street Beach PAC is possible — or whether this becomes a sub-council under JPAC, or an independent community organization instead. Until that comes back, every document here reflects a proposed path, not a settled one.

What this Council will not do

Repeated throughout the governing documents, on purpose, so no volunteer accidentally assumes authority this organization doesn't have:

  • Direct CPD employees, lifeguards, maintenance staff, or vendors — requests route through the Park Supervisor/Area Manager or the designated CPD channel.
  • Represent that the Council speaks for or can bind CPD in any contract, sponsorship, or public statement.
  • Issue official swim or water-quality advisories — only link to or accurately restate current CPD-authoritative information, with source and date.
  • Conduct natural-area restoration, plant removal, collection, or scientific sampling without CPD authorization where required.
  • Install, move, repair, or control public-safety equipment, signage, barriers, or CPD property without authorization.
  • Hold a Council event without the required CPD Activity Request approval, or without permits/insurance when triggered.
  • Solicit or receive funds without the required fiscal structure, bank account, CPD coordination, and records.
  • Use member applications or sign-in sheets as an unrestricted mailing list, or share personal data.
  • Record meetings unless the current CPD consent rule is satisfied.
  • Claim Open Meetings Act or FOIA status without a current legal basis — transparency is practiced voluntarily, on top of whatever actually applies.
