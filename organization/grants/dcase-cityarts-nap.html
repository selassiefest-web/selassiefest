DCASE: CityArts & the Neighborhood Access Program

Two separate CyberGrants applications, prepared ahead of time so Ras Tafari Inc can submit the moment each portal opens.

The Department of Cultural Affairs and Special Events (DCASE) administers both the CityArts Program and the Neighborhood Access Program (NAP) through its CyberGrants online portal. While both support Chicago-based cultural production, they serve distinct organizational needs and operate on separate annual timelines.

This page is Ras Tafari Inc's working instrument for both programs: one shared organization profile (Section 04) feeds every application below, so a fact only needs to be entered once. Sections 05–07 are the actual application drafts — question by question, pulling in organization-profile facts automatically and leaving narrative answers open to draft directly on this page. Section 08 compiles everything into a clean view for copying into CyberGrants once each portal opens.

Every fact below is sourced from DCASE's published 2026 program guidelines (see Sources, Section 09) — the most recent guidelines published as of this writing. DCASE has not yet published 2027 guidelines; where the 2027 cycle's exact dates/amounts may differ, that is called out explicitly rather than assumed.

01 Before Drafting

Eligibility Watch

Two open questions could affect whether Ras Tafari Inc qualifies for either program as currently structured. Both are flagged here deliberately rather than assumed away — resolve them before treating either draft below as submission-ready.

!

Registered address is outside Chicago city limits. Both programs require the lead applicant's primary office address to be inside the city of Chicago. Ras Tafari Inc's address on file is 765 E 154th St, South Holland, IL 60473 — South Holland is a separate south-suburban municipality, not Chicago. CityArts guidelines explicitly disqualify "organizations with primary office addresses located outside of the city of Chicago"; NAP requires the lead applicant to "reside/be located in the city of Chicago" as well as in the specific neighborhood where the project happens.

This is confirmed as the org's real registered address, not an error to fix on this page — it needs to be resolved organizationally (a Chicago address for the org, or a structural workaround DCASE accepts) before either application can be submitted as currently eligible.

!

Incorporation-date timing is tight for CityArts. CityArts requires the applicant to be incorporated in Illinois at least 12 months before applying (the 2026 cycle's cutoff was incorporation by April 2025, for a June 2026 deadline). Ras Tafari Inc incorporated June 8, 2026. If the 2027 cycle follows the same roughly-14-month lookback pattern, the earliest cycle Ras Tafari Inc could clear that bar is uncertain and should be checked against the 2027 guidelines the moment they're published, rather than assumed to be the 2027 cycle by default.

NAP's individual-artist/collective pathway does not carry the same incorporation-history requirement in the same way, but does require the lead applicant to show at least one year of relevant community/creative work — SelassieFest's first year ran in 2026, so this clears by mid-to-late 2027.

02 Choosing a Program

Program Comparison

DCASE's own framing: CityArts is ongoing institutional support for organizations that are themselves Chicago-based and already an established 501(c)(3). NAP explicitly widens eligibility beyond that — it accepts individual artists, collectives, and fiscally-sponsored applicants, and is place-based rather than citywide, prioritizing neighborhoods with a history of disinvestment. A current NAP grantee with an active project is ineligible for CityArts Project funding in the same cycle; a CityArts general operating grantee remains eligible for NAP.
