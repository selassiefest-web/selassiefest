Executive summary

Ras Tafari Inc., a 501(c)(3) nonprofit, respectfully requests a site-use and special event permit to hold SelassieFest 2027 at Seven Hills in Washington Park on Saturday, July 24, 2027. SelassieFest is Chicago's roots reggae and Rastafari cultural festival, tracing its lineage to the city's original Caribbean cultural gatherings held between 1981 and 1997 — a run of festivals that, for many who attended, has never been matched since.

This is not a cold request. Over the past several months a cross-agency working group has already come together around this proposal: two DCASE contacts (Camille and Jackie), a Chicago Park District contact (Denise), and two community members — Denise and Diedra — who lived the original festival era first-hand and have joined the planning committee specifically to help bring it back. This document lays out the case for the Seven Hills site, the team already assembled, and what we're asking the City and the 3rd Ward to help us secure next.

Turning 2026 into 2027 goals

2026 proved the concept at small scale: a real crowd, a full day of programming, a diverse vendor mix, and a team that can execute fast. 2027's goals build directly from that baseline — a fixed year-long runway instead of 47 days, an expected attendance of 500 at a civic-scaled site instead of 273 at a constrained one, and an organizing body now including Denise, Diedra, and Brother JahSyll in addition to the core team that delivered 2026.

Why Seven Hills, Washington Park

A civic-scaled outdoor home for a festival that has outgrown a room

Chicago's reggae and dancehall community has real venues it loves — but nothing built at the scale the original SelassieFest reached in its 1981–1997 run. Seven Hills' natural bowl topography inside Washington Park gives us a purpose-built outdoor amphitheater setting: a sloped green that works as a built-in gallery for a main stage, with the surrounding lawn and path network able to host a marketplace, heritage village, healing grove, and a dedicated family/kids zone side by side — without needing to shut down city streets.

The site sits within the 3rd Ward, is served by CTA Green Line and bus routes, and is a short walk from the DuSable Black History Museum — a natural cultural anchor for a festival rooted in Pan-African and Rastafari heritage. Choosing a Chicago Park District green space also puts this proposal squarely inside the Park District's own mission of hosting civic cultural programming, rather than asking the City to accommodate an ill-fitting private venue.

Community planning committee — lived experience

Denise & Diedra

Both attended the original SelassieFest during its 1981–1997 run and describe it as a profound cultural experience Chicago hasn't seen replicated since. When asked directly whether they wanted to be part of bringing it back, the answer from both was immediate and unprompted: "I needed this." They've since joined the planning committee for the 2027 revival — not as honorary guests, but as working members helping shape site logistics, programming, and community outreach — driven by wanting their grandchildren to have the same experience they did.

Community & cultural impact

SelassieFest 2027 continues Ras Tafari Inc.'s mission of preserving and reviving Rastafari and Caribbean cultural heritage in Chicago, and directly supports the organization's ongoing Historical Reconstruction Project documenting the festival's 1981–1997 era. Hosting in Washington Park roots this revival in the 3rd Ward, bringing foot traffic and vendor opportunity to local businesses and giving the community that lived the original festival — and the generation that never got to — a shared, intergenerational gathering.

Closing statement

SelassieFest 2027 is a chance to give Chicago back something it lost — not as a recreation, but as a living continuation, led in part by the very people who experienced its original run. With DCASE, the Chicago Park District, and the 3rd Ward's office already engaged and a community planning committee in place, we're asking the City to help us bring this home to Washington Park.
