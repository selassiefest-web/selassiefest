06 The Program

Groundation — The Opening Program

The day opens at 6:00 AM, with the park, in a Groundation: a Rastafari gathering of Nyabinghi drumming, chanting and reasoning, held acoustically at the north end of the footprint. It runs two hours and hands over to the amplified program at 8:00 AM.

What a Groundation is

"Grounation" — also written Groundation — comes from Iyaric, the Rastafari register, built on "foundation" with "ground" carrying the sense of making contact with the soil. The word dates to April 21, 1966, when Emperor Haile Selassie I landed at Palisadoes Airport in Kingston before a crowd of roughly one hundred thousand Rastafari, and stepped off the aircraft onto the ground rather than the red carpet laid for him. April 21 has been observed as Grounation Day ever since — the most significant date in the Rastafari calendar.

As a gathering rather than a date, a groundation is the Nyabinghi Order's form of worship: drumming, chanting and reasoning held together. The Nyabinghi mansion takes its name from Queen Nyabinghi, an East African warrior empress of resistance, and emerged in Jamaica in the 1930s. Sessions traditionally run for hours, and the tradition anchors the rhythmic line that later fed ska, rocksteady and reggae.

The three drums

Bass (Thunder)
The heartbeat
The largest drum, double-headed with goat skin, played with a mallet across the lap. Strikes loudly on the first beat, softly on the third of four.
Funde
The steady line
Middle-pitched, held between the knees, struck with the palms. Holds the rhythm on two and four without variation.
Repeater
The carrier of spirit
Smallest, highest-pitched, played with the fingertips. Improvises across the top of the other two, trading phrases like a conversation.

The music is in 4/4. Chanting draws on the Psalms and Rastafari settings of well-known hymns, carrying themes of redemption and repatriation. Reasoning — open, unhurried discussion — sits between the chants rather than after them.
