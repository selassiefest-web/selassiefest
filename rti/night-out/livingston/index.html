Growth playbook: more parks and repeat bookings (proposed work plan)

Background · tap to open
Proposed planning target: a six-event pilot across three parks, with two distinct visits per park, subject to funding and staff approval. This is a planning target, not an established NOITP entitlement. We will use it to structure the conversation, the park conversations, and the next application, and compare it against the other growth options before selecting a target.
ActionPractical approach
1. Diagnose the actual constraintDocument requested, awarded, scheduled, and completed events. Establish whether Livingston asked for two events or asked for more and received two. Identify the real constraint and build the next proposal around the reason.
2. Reconnect and secure return interestAsk Tilton and Ada staff (previous hosts) what worked, what needs improvement, and whether they would support two different visits next season. Request a short statement of interest, without treating it as funding approval. Seek permission for introductions.
3. Give each return visit a purposeOffer a connected sequence: Visit 1, Jamaican rhythms, instruments, and musical history with audience participation; Visit 2, a distinct interactive reggae concert that builds on the first. Livingston shapes the content around what his band delivers well.
4. Package and offer scalable budgetsPrepare distinct program options and transparent 2-, 4-, and 6-event budgets showing musician pay, sound, transport, outreach, administration, and any amenities the park cannot supply. Claim only genuine shared savings and keep artist pay fair.
5. Reduce hosting workPackage a concise technical rider, setup timeline, staffing responsibilities, promotion package, and weather alternative. State exactly what the park must provide.
6. Build local, neighborhood demandName a local outreach partner for each proposed park, such as a school, library, community organization, or neighborhood business, with its specific contribution and a specific resident-engagement plan showing how residents will take part.
7. Broaden availabilityOffer realistic weekday and geographic flexibility across the North, Central, and South regions, plus an indoor version where feasible. Do not promise dates or production capacity the band cannot sustain.
8. Document, then renewAfter each event, track attendance estimates, participation, outreach activity, staff and host observations and feedback, and return interest. Collect consented photos (images) and short testimonials. Turn results into evidence for the following application and park conversations.
9. Use park relationshipsWhere we already have a relationship with a particular park, submit our program to that individual park so its staff can request our presence at their venue for their own events, planned together with that park. Confirm with NOITP how park-requested events are approved and funded.
10. Confirm authorityAsk the central NOITP team how park interest, repeat visits, additional dates, and independently funded performances are approved. Individual park enthusiasm supports a proposal; it is not an award or permission to add funded events.

This approach fits the current guidelines’ emphasis on local relationships, returning partners’ outreach records, flexibility across regions, feasible production, and sustainable budgets.

2027 guidelines and calendar

Background · tap to open

Scored heavily

  • Enriching and engaging programming
  • Production viability in park spaces
  • Commitment to community connectivity, with a thorough outreach and marketing plan
  • Budget feasibility: sustainable, fair, accurate to scope

Requirements that shape the plan

  • Chicago-based applicant, verified at application, contracting, and payment; main applicant 21+
  • Program free to the public; other funding encouraged
  • Returning applicants must show intentional outreach and community engagement
  • Priority for willingness to present in all three regions; partners transport their own gear and people
  • Events any day, mostly evenings; no NOITP dates on Sundays or federal holidays
  • Budget for any amenity the program depends on

Contracting

  • $1 million general liability insurance strongly recommended, naming the Park District as additional insured
  • Fingerprint clearance or background check possible where partners interact with the public
  • Accessibility plan encouraged: mobile access, bilingual narratives, ASL, audio description
  • Performance schedule must be confirmed by mid-March 2027

What partners can receive

  • Permit fee waivers
  • Full or partial budget support; no two awards are the same
  • Up to 1,000 printed marketing pieces, plus digital promotion
  • A NOITP liaison for park connections, site selection, and training
  • Some applicants are offered space and marketing only
