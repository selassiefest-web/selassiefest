Here's the research. It explains how Illinois pays for reentry housing, through IDOC, the R3 grant program (ICJIA), IDHS and IHDA, and names the nonprofits that deliver it. The Copy button copies all of it.

The Illinois Department of Corrections (IDOC), which oversees parole and Mandatory Supervised Release (MSR), does not directly cut rent checks to private landlords or run an ongoing cash housing voucher program directly out of parole field offices.

Instead, the state funds and coordinates rental assistance, transitional placement, and housing stability through legislative appropriations, inter-agency partnerships (IDOC, IDHS, and IHDA), and state-contracted community reentry providers.

PRIMARY FUNDING SOURCES AND STATE INITIATIVES

1. IDOC Reentry Housing & Halfway House / Placement Contracts
Funding Stream: State General Revenue Fund (GRF) appropriations allocated to IDOC's Division of Reentry and Specialized Services.
Mechanism: IDOC contracts directly with licensed adult transitional centers (halfway houses), contracted recovery residences, and non-profit residential reentry centers. This pays for 30- to 90-day transitional bed space to ensure a viable host site for individuals who otherwise lack an approved home site.

2. Restore, Reinvest, and Renew (R3) Grant Program
Administering Body: Illinois Criminal Justice Information Authority (ICJIA).
Funding Stream: 25% of all Illinois adult-use cannabis tax revenues.
Mechanism: R3 awards multimillion-dollar grant cycles directly to community-based non-profits in communities disproportionately impacted by incarceration and disinvestment (especially in Chicago and Cook County). Reentry providers use these funds for emergency housing, short-term rental subsidies, and move-in deposits.

3. Illinois Department of Human Services (IDHS) Supportive Housing Line Items
Programs: Crisis/Emergency Housing Assistance and the Permanent Supportive Housing (PSH) line.
Funding Stream: IDHS State Budget appropriations paired with federal Emergency Solutions Grants (ESG) and HUD Continuum of Care (CoC) dollars.
Mechanism: IDHS funds specialized reentry housing programs that offer scattered-site rapid re-housing (subsidizing private market rent for 6–24 months) while providing case management to help the individual maintain lease compliance.

4. Illinois Housing Development Authority (IHDA) Reentry Housing Allocations
Programs: Long-Term Operating Support (LTOS) through the Illinois Affordable Housing Trust Fund and the Home Illinois plan (Illinois Interagency Task Force on Homelessness).
Mechanism: Subsidizes physical rental units reserved specifically for justice-impacted individuals or offers project-based rental assistance to keep rent capped at roughly 30% of adjusted gross income.

KEY COMMUNITY CONTRACTORS & HOUSING PROVIDERS

Because funds are passed through to certified providers, these are the primary networks delivering rental assistance and placement in the region:

- St. Leonard's Ministries (West Side of Chicago): Comprehensive supportive housing, interim housing, and permanent supportive housing for formerly incarcerated men and women.
- Safer Foundation: Manages transitional housing placements, rapid re-housing funds, and workforce/retention stipends.
- Inner-Voice & Christian Community Health Center (CCHC): Administer HUD/IDHS rapid re-housing vouchers and scattered-site rental assistance for returning residents experiencing homelessness.
- A Safe Haven Foundation: Behavioral health, transitional housing, and permanent supportive housing options with direct IDOC linkage.
- The Renaissance Collaborative (TRC) & Featherfist: Supportive housing and emergency stabilization across the South Side of Chicago.

PROCEDURE TO ACCESS AND MAINTAIN RENT FUNDING

IDOC Facility / Parole Field Office
  → 1. Pre-Release Screening / Parole Agent Referral (Host Site Verification via Field Services / Parole Officer)
  → 2. Referral to Contracted Reentry Navigator / Housing Provider (IDHS-funded agency, Safer Foundation, or local CoC intake)
  → 3. Coordinated Entry System (CES) Assessment (VI-SPDAT / Standardized Housing Vulnerability Indexing)
  → 4. Housing Placement / Subsidy Execution, either:
       • Short-Term Rapid Re-Housing (direct landlord subsidy: 3–12 months), or
       • Transitional / Supportive Facility (on-site housing + wraparound services)
  → 5. Lease Maintenance & Ongoing Compliance (case management, income verification, parole host-site re-approval)

1. Pre-Release Identification or Parole Officer Referral:
- Before Exit: Clinical Services or Field Services staff submit an electronic referral if an individual is designated as a "no-host-site" case.
- On Active Parole/MSR: The parolee informs their supervising Parole Agent of housing vulnerability. The agent submits an internal referral to an IDOC Reentry Coordinator or an affiliated community-based organization.

2. Coordinated Entry Intake:
- In Chicago/Cook County, the individual must complete a Coordinated Entry System (CES) assessment (often via the Chicago Continuum of Care / 311 or an intake center like Catholic Charities or the Center for Housing and Health). Returning residents receive prioritization points under vulnerability indexing.

3. Provider Enrollment & Landlord Liaison:
- Once matched with a Rapid Re-Housing or R3-funded agency, a housing navigator assists the client in identifying a private landlord willing to accept justice-impacted tenants.
- The program issues a direct landlord subsidy (typically paying 70%–100% of the rent initially, stepping down as the participant gains employment).

4. IDOC Host Site Verification:
- Before the individual can sign the lease or move, the local Parole District office must inspect and approve the physical address to ensure it complies with any special conditions of MSR (e.g., proximity restrictions or exclusion zones).

5. Retention & Independence Transition:
- To maintain the subsidy, the individual must meet monthly case management benchmarks (income verification, job placement progress, or disability benefit enrollment).
- As earned income stabilizes, the tenant's share increases until they assume full lease responsibility.

IMMEDIATE ACTION STEPS FOR CLINICIANS AND CASE MANAGERS

- Submit Coordinated Entry Directly: Do not rely solely on the parole officer for housing placement. Link the individual directly to an access point (e.g., Catholic Charities, Safer Foundation, or the Chicago Homelessness Prevention Call Center via 311) to initiate a standardized housing assessment.
- Request an IDOC Reentry Housing Voucher/Referral: Have the client formally request a consultation with their assigned parole district's Reentry Specialist (distinct from the armed field agent) to identify open, state-contracted beds.
- Tap R3-Funded Providers: Contact local non-profits funded under the current R3 cycle in the client's home zip code, as these grants frequently maintain flexible emergency rental funds not bound by federal HUD housing disqualifications.
