The proposal

Temporary use, on the owner's terms, until redevelopment is ready

We are not asking anyone to change their plans. We are asking to put an idle site to good use for the time it would otherwise sit empty, under a written license or lease that the owner controls.

What we're asking for

  • One in-person meeting with the owner's representative.
  • A short-term written agreement (license, lease or similar) for community and cultural use.
  • A clear notice period, so we can wind down in an orderly way when construction is near.

What we commit to

  • Keep the property clean, maintained, secured and occupied rather than vacant.
  • Carry liability insurance with the owner named as additional insured.
  • Get every city permit and license required before hosting the public.
  • No permanent alterations without the owner's written approval.
  • Be a good neighbor: posted hours, sound limits, litter patrol and a resident contact line.
  • Vacate on the agreed notice and leave the site clean.

How it would unfold

Step by step, with the owner's sign-off at each stage

  1. Meet and agree

    Sit down with the owner, walk the site together and put terms, insurance and notice period in writing.

  2. Stabilize and clean

    Cleanup, safety and code inspections, security and insurance in place before anyone else comes on site.

  3. Open small

    Permitted, low-impact programs first: exhibits, markets, classes and community meetings.

  4. Full seasons

    Seasonal programming and anchor events, coordinated with SelassieFest and the 2027 festival calendar.

  5. Hand back

    When the owner gives notice that redevelopment is ready, we wind down and return the site clean. The center moves on to its permanent home.

Who we are

Organizers who already bring the community together

The Caribbean Cultural Center is being organized as Yawd LLC, doing business as Caribbean Cultural Center. "Yawd" is Jamaican for yard, or home, and our venue brand is The Yawd: Come Home.

It is led by Stephen Henry, President of Ras Tafari Inc., a Chicago-area 501(c)(3) public charity and the producer of SelassieFest, a roots reggae and cultural festival. This site would give that work a year-round address.
