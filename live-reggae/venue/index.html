What your night looks like

One continuous Jamaican party, about 3 hours 20 minutes, with two live-band peaks. The DJ isn't a break between sets, the DJ plays along with the band: you hear a song you know, the DJ turns it inside out, and the band catches it and carries it somewhere you didn't expect.

30 minDJ welcome Soul, funk and Jamaican classics fill the room and build movement.
60 minLive set 1 Reggae authority, lovers rock and soul, with the first surprises.
25 minDJ interval Dancehall and crossover hits keep the floor full while the band rests.
60 minLive set 2 Dancehall pressure, bigger crossovers and a communal finish.
25 minDJ afterparty Sustains the peak and closes the night on purpose.

Songs your guests already know, played like they've never heard them

  • “Bam Bam” into “Lost Ones.” The band lays down Sister Nancy's “Bam Bam,” the DJ brings in Lauryn Hill, the singer answers and the band brings everyone home.
  • “Murder She Wrote” into “Post to Be.” The DJ reveals the R&B connection and the band takes the last chorus back.
  • The Diwali medley. “No Letting Go,” “Never Leave You” and “Get Busy” as one developing performance, singer and DJ trading the lead over a live riddim.
  • “Billie Jean” into “Uptown Funk.” The DJ sets the riff, then the musicians enter piece by piece and turn it into a reggae arrangement.
  • “Rich Girl,” twice. The reggae original first, the pop remake second, the live band last.
  • Country, Jamaican style. “Take Me Home, Country Roads,” “The Gambler,” “Jolene” and “Tennessee Whiskey.” Jamaica has loved country storytelling for generations.
  • Neo-soul and lovers rock. Erykah Badu, Musiq Soulchild, Gregory Isaacs and Beres Hammond, with live guitar and keys answering.
  • The finish. “Could You Be Loved” closes set 1. “Three Little Birds” into “One Love / People Get Ready” closes set 2, with the whole room singing.

Built for your room

We keep the anchor moments and rotate three or four sections each booking, so a returning guest recognizes the show and still hears something new. Want more roots, more lovers rock, more women's voices, more hip-hop or more pop? Tell us your crowd and we'll tilt the night. The song plan is our proposed program, and the arrangements are rehearsed and adjusted to the singer and the room.
