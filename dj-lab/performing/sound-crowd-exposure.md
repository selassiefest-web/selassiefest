## Our commitments

These are policies Ras Tafari Inc. commits to. Formal board adoption is a launch requirement.

- **Headphones are capped.** Headphone volume is capped on every Lab laptop, in class and at events.
- **Earplugs are free.** Children (and parents) can have them at any time, with no need to ask twice.
- **Speakers are checked with a meter.** The main speaker's level is checked with a sound meter. The exact target level and where it's measured will be written into each event's plan before the first event.
- **There is a no-go zone.** Children stay out of the zone directly in front of the speakers, whether they are performing or watching.
- **Quiet breaks.** Performing children get time away from the loud area before and after their set.
- **We coach a "60/60"-style habit.** Children learn to keep their headphone volume low and to take breaks. The WHO guidance below is the reason.

## What the experts say

### World Health Organization (WHO): safe listening

- Sound below **80 dB** is unlikely to damage hearing.
- The safe weekly listening time is about **40 hours at 80 dB**. It drops to **4 hours at 90 dB** and **20 minutes at 100 dB**.
- Keep personal device volume **below 60% of maximum**.
- At concerts and clubs, **stay away from loudspeakers**, wear earplugs, and **rest your ears in a quiet space for about 10 minutes every hour**.
- A simple test: if you have to raise your voice to be heard by someone an arm's length away, it is too loud.

### NIOSH: the workplace benchmark

- The U.S. National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health recommends limiting workplace noise to **85 dBA averaged over an eight-hour workday**.
- That limit was set for adult workers. Children's hearing deserves more caution, so we treat 85 dBA as an upper reference point, not a target.

## How this works on event day

| Moment | What we do |
|---|---|
| Before the event | We pass on the speaker-level plan and the no-go zone to the event's sound crew. |
| Check-in | Earplugs are offered. The child's headphones are checked on a capped Lab laptop. |
| Waiting to perform | Children wait in a quieter spot with their named Lab adult, away from the speakers. |
| On stage | The DJ listens through capped headphones. The Lab adult watches for signs of discomfort, such as covering ears or wincing. |
| After the set | A quiet break before rejoining the crowd. |
| Watching other acts | Children stay back from the speakers. Earplugs stay available. |

## Crowds

- Big crowds can feel overwhelming. A child can ask to leave the performer area for a calm spot at any time. A Lab adult goes with them and stays in view of others. See [Cool-down space](/dj-lab/safety/cooldown-space.html).
- In class, children practice playing for a "crowd" of classmates (for example, the "crowd reading" move break). That way the first real crowd isn't a surprise.
- If a child changes their mind because of the noise or the crowd, that's fine. See [Opting out](/dj-lab/performing/opt-out.html).
