title: Peer-Harm Protocol
description: What Rainbow DJ Lab adults do when one child harms another, including bullying, fighting, unwanted touch and online harm, step by step.

## Status

These are policies Ras Tafari Inc. commits to; formal board adoption is a launch requirement. The Lab is **proposed**. No staff are hired yet.

## What this covers

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Bullying** | Unwanted, aggressive behavior where there's a real or perceived power imbalance, and it happens repeatedly or is likely to (StopBullying.gov). It can be **verbal** (teasing, name-calling, threats), **social** (leaving someone out on purpose, spreading rumors) or **physical** (hitting, tripping, taking or breaking things) |
| **Fighting** | Any hitting, kicking, pushing to hurt, or wrestling that isn't play |
| **Unwanted touch** | Any touching another child doesn't want, including touching private areas, pulling clothes, or sexual comments or gestures |
| **Online harm** | Cyberbullying between Lab kids: mean messages, posting or sharing photos or video of another child without permission, group-chat exclusion, threats. This includes things that happen outside class but spill into it |
| **Hate or bias** | Insults about race, ethnicity, religion, disability, gender, sexual orientation, body or family |

## Prevention

- Two adults always present and actively moving between stations.
- Jobs rotate and the class agreements are taught at session 1.
- **No phones out during class** for kids, and no photos or video of other children. The Lab's app stores no photos or video.
- Restroom buddy system ([Restroom Supervision](/dj-lab/safety/restroom-supervision.html)).
- Kids are taught: "If someone is hurting you or someone else, tell any adult. You won't be in trouble for telling."

## Steps when harm happens

1. **Stop it.** The nearest adult says "Stop" clearly and steps between the children if needed, without hitting or grabbing.
2. **Separate.** The children go to different spots, both in view of an adult. The second adult supervises the rest of the class (ratio holds).
3. **Care for anyone hurt.** First aid, and **911** for serious injury.
4. **Listen to each child separately,** in view of the other adult. Ask "What happened?" Don't make the children apologize on the spot, and don't make a child face the child who harmed them.
5. **Write it down** the same day: who, what, when, where, witnesses, and the children's words.
6. **Call both families the same day.** The lead adult calls the parent of the child who was harmed **and** the parent of the child who caused harm. Each family is told what happened to *their* child and what the Lab is doing. We don't give one family the other child's private details (such as medical information or discipline history) beyond what's needed for safety.
7. **Decide next steps** using the [Discipline Ladder](/dj-lab/safety/discipline-ladder.html). Fighting and unwanted touch are safety violations and go straight to a parent call. A pause from the program may follow.
8. **Report when required** (below).
9. **Written incident report** within 24 hours ([Incident Reporting](/dj-lab/safety/incident-reporting.html)).

## Reporting obligations

- **Every staff member is a mandated reporter** under the Illinois Abused and Neglected Child Reporting Act. If peer harm points to possible abuse or neglect of either child (for example, sexual behavior between children that suggests a child has been exposed to abuse, an injury that suggests harm at home, or a child left without care), staff call the **DCFS Hotline, 1-800-25-ABUSE (1-800-252-2873)** directly, without needing anyone's permission. **When unsure, call and let the hotline decide.**
- **Immediate danger, a serious assault, a weapon or a threat to hurt someone:** call **911**.
- Staff don't investigate abuse themselves or question children in detail. That's the job of DCFS and the police.
- The [Youth Protection Policy](/dj-lab/safety/youth-protection-policy.html) covers anything involving an adult.

## Online harm specifically

1. Ask the child to show an adult (and the parent) but **not** forward or repost the content.
2. **Never forward, save or share sexual images of a child,** even to "report" them. Tell the parent, and call 911 or report to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children's CyberTipline (report.cybertip.org).
3. Help families use the app or platform's report and block tools.
4. If there's a threat of violence or self-harm, call 911.

## Follow-up

| When | Who | What |
|---|---|---|
| Next session | Lead adult | Checks in privately with the child who was harmed: "How are you doing? Is it still happening?" |
| Next session | Lead adult | Sets up stations so the children aren't paired, if the family of the child who was harmed wants that |
| Within 1 week | Lead adult | Calls the family of the child who was harmed to check in |
| Ongoing | All adults | Watch for retaliation. Retaliation is treated as a new safety violation |
| After serious cases | Stephen Henry | Reviews what happened and what to change |

## What you'll be told

- **The same day, by phone:** what happened to your child, what we did, and the next steps, whether your child was harmed or caused harm.
- **Within 24 hours:** a written incident report.
- **Within 1 week:** a follow-up check-in (for the family of the child who was harmed).

If you're not satisfied with how it was handled, see [Report a Concern](/dj-lab/safety/report-a-concern.html) and the [Grievance Procedure](/dj-lab/safety/grievance-procedure.html).
