Behavior & Emotional Safety

How the Rainbow DJ Lab builds a calm, predictable, kind classroom where every child can make mistakes, say no, and ask for help.

Status

These are policies Ras Tafari Inc. commits to; formal board adoption is a launch requirement. The Lab is proposed. No staff are hired yet. Every coach will be trained on this page before their first class.

Our approach

We use two well-known ideas:

  • Trauma-informed care. We assume any child might be carrying something hard we don't know about. So we focus on safety, trust, choice and respect, not on control. (The federal agency SAMHSA describes six principles: safety; trustworthiness and transparency; peer support; collaboration and mutuality; empowerment, voice and choice; and attention to cultural, historical and gender issues.)
  • Positive behavior support. We teach and praise the behavior we want, clearly and often, so that correcting a child is rare.

What it looks like in class

Practice What it means for your child
Same shape every session Every class runs the same 9 blocks (Drop In, Mission Card, Watch Me Think, Deck Time 1, Move Break, Deck Time 2, Wordplay Lab, Spotlight, Check Out). Kids know what's next
A few simple class agreements Taught at session 1 and posted, for example: respect the gear, respect each other, respect the music
"Data!" not failure When a mix crashes, the DJ names one reason and tries again. Mistakes are learning, out loud, with no teasing
Choice Kids pick their challenge level (Mild, Medium or Spicy) in Deck Time 2
No forced spotlight Spotlight is volunteers only. Coaches never call on a child who didn't sign up
No grades, no rankings Kids rate themselves (I got it / Getting there / Not yet). Passport stamps celebrate skills, not compare kids
Rotating jobs DJ, Co-pilot, Hype and Logger rotate every 3 minutes 45 seconds, so everyone gets a turn and a role

Specific praise "You found the downbeat," not just "good job." Coaches aim for many more positive comments than corrections

How coaches respond to big feelings

  1. Notice early. A child who's quiet, frustrated or withdrawn gets a calm check-in: "Want to keep going, take a break, or switch jobs?"
  2. Stay calm and close (not crowding). A low voice, at the child's level, in view of the other adult.
  3. Name it, don't shame it. "That mix was frustrating. That's Data!"
  4. Offer a choice, including the cool-down space.
  5. Reconnect. When the child is ready, they rejoin without a lecture.

If behavior needs correcting, coaches follow the Discipline Ladder. If one child harms another, they follow the Peer-Harm Protocol.

Things we never do

  • Physical punishment of any kind, or rough handling.
  • Shaming, name-calling, sarcasm about a child, or public "call-outs."
  • Withholding food, water or the restroom.
  • Isolating a child or leaving them alone, or locking them anywhere.
  • Making a child perform or speak in front of the group.
  • Commenting on a child's body, family, money, religion, race, gender or how they talk.
  • Keeping secrets with a child. (Adults may say "I can't keep that secret, but I'll help you.")

When a child tells us something worrying

If a child shares something that suggests abuse, neglect or danger, staff listen calmly, don't press for details, don't promise secrecy, and report directly to the DCFS Hotline, 1-800-25-ABUSE (1-800-252-2873), or 911 for immediate danger. Every staff member is a mandated reporter (Mandated Reporter Training).

Working with families

  • At registration, you can tell us what helps your child (triggers, calming strategies, sensory needs, an IEP or 504 plan strategy you'd like us to use). We use it and keep it private.
  • If something is going on at home or school that might affect class, you can tell Stephen Henry privately.

What you'll be told

  • At pickup: a quick, honest word about the session, including a rough moment if there was one.
  • A phone call: if your child was upset enough to need extra help, or any time the Discipline Ladder reaches the parent-call step.
