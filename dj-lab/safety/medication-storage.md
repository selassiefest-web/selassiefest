title: Medication Storage & Administration
description: How the Rainbow DJ Lab accepts, stores, gives, records and returns a child's medicine.

## Status

These are policies Ras Tafari Inc. commits to; formal board adoption is a launch requirement. The Lab is **proposed**. No staff are hired and no class dates are set.

## The rules

1. **Original container only.** Medicine comes in its original pharmacy container with the prescription label (child's name, medicine, dose, prescriber). Over-the-counter medicine comes in its original package.
2. **Signed authorization first.** A parent or guardian signs a medication authorization on the [Medical & Allergy form](/dj-lab/enroll/forms/medical-allergy.html) before any medicine is accepted.
3. **Locked, except rescue medicine.** Routine medicine is kept in a locked box that stays with the lead adult. **Rescue medicine** (an epinephrine auto-injector or an inhaler) is never locked. It stays within reach of the trained adult at all times.
4. **No medicine in kids' bags.** Children don't keep medicine in backpacks, with one exception: if a child's own plan and their prescriber say they carry their own inhaler, and the parent agrees in writing, the trained adult still knows where it is.
5. **Given only as labeled.** We give the dose and time on the label or plan. We never change doses.

Most classes are 90 minutes (fast-track workshops are 2 hours), so routine medicine during class should be rare. If a dose can be given at home, please give it at home.

## Hand-off at sign-in

| Step | Who | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Parent | Hands medicine to the lead adult. Medicine is never given to a child to carry in |
| 2 | Lead adult | Checks: child's name on label, expiration date, dose matches the authorization |
| 3 | Lead adult | Puts routine medicine in the locked box, or gives rescue medicine to the trained adult |
| 4 | Both | Lead adult notes it on the medication log (time received, who received it) |

## Giving medicine

1. Check the "five rights": right child, right medicine, right dose, right time, right way (by mouth, inhaled, injected).
2. Give it where another adult can see.
3. Record it right away: medicine, dose, time, who gave it, and how the child responded.
4. For rescue medicine used in an emergency, follow the child's plan and the [Epinephrine Protocol](/dj-lab/safety/epinephrine-protocol.html). After epinephrine, we always call 911.

## Returning medicine

- At sign-out, routine medicine is handed back to the authorized adult picking up, not to the child.
- Expired medicine is returned to the parent, never thrown away by staff.
- If medicine is left behind, Stephen Henry calls the parent the same day.

## Mistakes

If a dose is missed, given at the wrong time, or given to the wrong child:

1. Check the child. If they're unwell, call 911. For a possible overdose or wrong medicine, call the **Illinois Poison Center at 1-800-222-1222** (call 911 if the child is unresponsive, having trouble breathing or seizing).
2. Call the parent right away.
3. Write an incident report within 24 hours ([Incident Reporting](/dj-lab/safety/incident-reporting.html)).

## What you'll be told

- At pickup: whether any medicine was given, when, and how much.
- Immediately by phone: any rescue medicine used, any mistake, or any reaction.
