Before Day One

What to do, sign and talk about with your child before their first Rainbow DJ Lab class.

First: the Lab isn't running yet

Rainbow DJ Lab is proposed. The Chicago Park District has not approved it, the Rainbow Beach PAC vote is pending, no dates are set, and no staff are hired. No class runs until every item on the launch checklist is met. Everything below describes how it will work once it does.

Your checklist

1. Register

Registration will be on the Register page. At registration you give verifiable parental consent for the limited information the Lab collects about your child. See COPPA compliance. You'll get a confirmation email.

2. Fill in the forms

All parent forms will be available in English and Spanish. See Forms.

  • [ ] Registration
  • [ ] Emergency contacts
  • [ ] Medical and allergy, plus your child's FARE Food Allergy & Anaphylaxis Emergency Care Plan if they have one
  • [ ] Authorized pickup: every adult who may collect your child. For a 9-year-old (Junior DJs), drop-off requires this signed list.
  • [ ] Media consent
  • [ ] Performance consent
  • [ ] Digital tracking consent
  • [ ] Code of conduct: read it together with your child

If your child takes medication during class time, it must come in its original pharmacy container with a signed authorization. See Medication storage.

3. Tell us what helps

If your child has sensory needs, a disability, or a learning difference, tell us now. IEP or 504 information is welcome but never required. See Accessibility.

4. Come to orientation

See Orientation. It's your chance to see the space, meet the adults, and ask anything.

5. Plan the trip

See Locations and Transportation and parking. Bring a photo ID every time you pick up. Every adult on your pickup list must bring one too.

What your child needs to bring

Nothing required. Class is free and all equipment is provided: controller, laptop, headphones and earplugs. Your child may bring their own ear defenders if they prefer them, and a water bottle is a good idea. Outdoors in summer, see Sunscreen and hydration.

Get your child ready

A 9-year-old usually wants to know three things: Who will be there? What will I do? What if I mess up?

  • Read the kids' pages together: My first day, Rules for me, and If you feel unsafe.
  • Explain "Data!" In the Lab, a mistake is called "Data!" Your child names one reason it went wrong and tries again. Nobody gets in trouble for a crashed mix.
  • Explain Spotlight. Playing on the big speaker is volunteers only. Nobody will make them do it.
  • Talk about the buddy system. Kids go to the restroom in pairs, and an adult waits at the entrance. See Bathroom and buddy.
  • Name their safe adults. Make sure your child knows who's on the pickup list, and that they should never leave with anyone else. See Who to talk to.
  • Start thinking of a DJ name. They'll build one in week 1: their name + a sound + a color.

Keep illness rules in mind

Keep your child home with a fever of 100.4°F or higher. They can come back when they've been fever-free for 24 hours without fever-reducing medicine and their symptoms are improving. After vomiting or diarrhea, keep them home until they've been symptom-free for 24 hours. See Communicable disease.
