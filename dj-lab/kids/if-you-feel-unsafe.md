title: If you feel unsafe
description: What to do if something feels wrong. It is never your fault, and grown-ups will help.

## It is never your fault

If something makes you feel scared, weird or uncomfortable, **it is not your fault**. Not ever. You won't get in trouble for telling.

## Tell a grown-up you trust

You can tell:

- **your mom, dad or the grown-up who takes care of you**
- **any coach at the Lab**
- **a teacher, a school counselor or another grown-up you trust**

If the first grown-up doesn't listen, tell another one. Keep telling until someone helps.

You can say it in your own words. Here's one way: "Something happened and I need help."

## No secrets about touching

**No grown-up should ever ask you to keep a secret** about:

- touching
- private parts
- private messages, texts, pictures or chats

Those are never OK to keep secret. Tell a grown-up you trust, even if someone said not to.

A **surprise** is different. A surprise is happy, like a birthday present, and everyone finds out soon. A secret that makes you feel bad is one to tell.

## Online and on phones

If anyone sends you something that feels wrong, don't answer. Show a grown-up you trust.

## In an emergency, call 911

If someone is hurt or in danger right now, **call 911**. You can call from any phone. Say:

- your name
- where you are
- what's happening

## For grown-ups helping a child

If a child tells you about abuse or neglect, call the **Illinois DCFS Child Abuse Hotline: 1-800-25-ABUSE (1-800-252-2873)**. If the child is in immediate danger, call **911**.

You can also contact Stephen Henry, who leads the Lab, at 414-909-3279 or stephen@selassiefest.com. But you don't need anyone's permission to call DCFS or 911. See [Report a concern](/dj-lab/safety/report-a-concern.html).

## Remember

- You are brave for telling.
- You will be believed and helped.
- Your body belongs to you.
