Curriculum

All 14 sessions of the Rainbow DJ Lab full course, the Passport skills for each, and how every 90-minute session is shaped.

The full course at a glance

  • 14 sessions, 90 minutes each, two sessions for each color of the rainbow: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet.
  • One Mission per session: a single goal your child can say in one sentence.
  • 3 Passport skills per session, 42 across the course. Six stamps complete a color badge.
  • No grades and no tests. Kids rate themselves and mentors award stamps.
  • The course ends with the Rainbow Showcase, which families attend.

This curriculum is already written and runs in the live class app. Class dates are not set yet. See the schedule.

Listen-back days are sessions 6, 10 and 13. Each child records 2 minutes of mixing and reviews it with a mentor: one glow, one grow, one goal.

Session 14 is the Rainbow Showcase. Families attend. Every child plays a 10-minute set, or 5 minutes with a partner if they prefer. Every performer gets glow cards.

The shape of every session: 9 blocks

Every 90-minute session follows the same pattern, so children always know what comes next. The live class app shows the current block on screen.

Block Minutes What happens
1. Drop In 0–7 Kids arrive to music already playing and warm up by clapping on beat 1 and stomping on beat 4.
2. Mission Card 7–12 Everyone hears today's one goal and predicts whether it will be easy, medium or hard for them.
3. Watch Me Think 12–20 The coach demonstrates while saying their thinking out loud, so kids hear how an experienced DJ makes decisions.
4. Deck Time 1 20–35 Hands-on time at the controllers. Everyone starts at the Mild level.
5. Move Break 35–40 A movement game set to music. It's part of the lesson, not a reward: it resets attention.
6. Deck Time 2 40–55 Another round. Each child picks Mild, Medium or Spicy, and can switch any time.
7. Wordplay Lab 55–70 Word games with song titles and themes: puns, title chains, shout-outs. Clean words only.
8. Spotlight 70–82 Volunteers play 60 seconds on the main speaker. Classmates give one glow and one grow.
9. Check Out 82–90 Kids rate their progress with a traffic light, write "Next time I'll…", earn stamps, and put gear away.

How deck time works

  • Groups of 4 share one controller. Up to 3 stations makes a class of up to 12 children.
  • Jobs rotate every 3 minutes 45 seconds, so nobody waits around:
  • DJ: hands on the decks, trying the Mission.
  • Co-pilot: headphones on, counts the beat out loud, asks the DJ "Did it work?"
  • Hype: plays the crowd and practices one MC line on the beat.
  • Logger: writes in the Mix Log: which songs, what happened, what to change.
  • Challenge levels: in Deck Time 2, each child chooses Mild, Medium or Spicy. In session 1, for example, Mild is "fade from one song to the other," and Spicy is "bring song 2 in exactly on the chorus of song 1."
  • "Data!" is our word for learning from a mistake. When a mix crashes, the station shouts "Data!" and the DJ names one reason before trying again.

How we measure progress

  • Traffic-light self-rating: I got it / Getting there / Not yet.
  • A "Next time I'll…" line, written by your child.
  • Passport stamps from mentors, for the 3 skills in each session.
  • No grades, no tests, no rankings. Red ("Not yet") is treated as useful information, not failure.

Spotlight is always voluntary

Spotlight is volunteers only. Coaches never call on a child who didn't sign up. A child who would rather not play on the big speaker can give glows instead. See Performing: opt out.

Gear and music

  • Controllers: Hercules DJControl Inpulse 300 MK2. Lights under the jog wheels guide beatmatching in the early sessions.
  • Software: Serato DJ Lite from session 9 on.
  • Hearing: headphone volume is capped on every laptop, and earplugs are available free. See Hearing safety.
  • Clean lyrics only: radio edits, checked when crates are built.
