Where every beat belongs.

Rainbow Wednesday is a South Side Chicago initiative that brings generations together through music, technology, education and cultural expression. Through Ras Tafari Inc., Rainbow Beach becomes a meeting point between Chicago's musical heritage, music technology from around the world, the next generation of creative talent, and a community that deserves access to all of it.

Rainbow Beach is where the idea takes root. Chicago is where it is shown. The world is where it can travel.

Four pillars. One community.

Education

Professional DJ instruction, music production, rhythm, technology, confidence and creative development.

Accessibility

Deaf, hard-of-hearing and hearing participants experience music together through touch, light and sound.

Culture

Chicago house, Jamaican sound-system heritage, reggae, hip-hop, Afrobeats and conversation across generations.

Community

Free experiences, family participation, youth leadership, neighborhood partnerships and public ownership.

These pillars live in the program itself. Accessibility shapes how the class is designed from the first day, and children are always learning, creating and performing.

Where wearable sound technology changes everything

A young participant stands at the DJ station. The instructor introduces a four-count rhythm.

A hearing student hears the beat. A Deaf student feels its pulse through a wearable vibration device. The screen displays the four-count. The lighting follows the musical phrase.

They work together to move from one track to the next. They are not in separate activities. They are learning the same musical concept, sharing the same challenge and the same accomplishment.

That is the experience we are here to build.

The experience, four ways in

Two 20-minute rotations, with 8 to 12 participants wearing tactile devices in each (a planning target; final numbers follow the room, the equipment and our advisors' feedback). The DJ and the screen share one sightline, seats are provided, and a staffed table handles fitting and cleaning.

See the beat

A screen shows the beat count, track changes and instructions. Light follows the musical sections.

Read what is happening

Host captions and cues such as "count four," "new track coming in," "bass drops here." A short original piece by a Chicago artist gives us a repeatable test track.

Feel the beat

Wearable devices turn the rhythm into vibration and light, with a staffed fitting table. Participation never requires wearing one.

Try the decks

An optional coached challenge: follow a visual four-count, start a second track, use the tactile cue if it helps. Participants tell us what they could follow and what to change.

Three ways to bring the equipment

RouteWhat it is
A. Original demonstrationWorking Ontenna demo units on a partner-backed loan or staffed demonstration, with AlphaTheta's Yokohama team and the Fujitsu project. Kaga FEI ended sales of the device on March 31, 2026, so a loan is the realistic route to the original units.
B. Staffed haptics activationA U.S. company deploys a staffed tactile-music setup for our date.
C. Our own Chicago setupWe bench-test two to four commercially available haptic wearables on a wired DJ feed before buying more, checking fit, timing and suitability for each age.

All three use the same room layout, DJ set, visual cues and accessibility plan. The original Club YOXO leaned on a lyric-visualization feature that has since been retired, so we are building our own captions and visual-cue system whichever route comes through.

How the first nights unfold

NightWhat happens
Rainbow Wednesday ZeroA closed rehearsal with families already in our circle, using stand-in tactile devices and flashing lights, so the room, the consent language and the fitting routine are real before the original devices arrive. We document what works for different hair, collars and cuffs, and what does not.
Night oneA closed pilot with the full setup. One song with the speakers nearly off and the wearables only: the moment the technology earns its place. Then the decks, four young people to a controller.
Night twoOpen, with Deaf and hard-of-hearing youth invited through our community partners. A Deaf guest takes the last 15 minutes as the person who judges whether the vibration matched the room.

The ground rules

  • A Deaf co-host decides how the devices are introduced, how consent works and whether anyone is photographed. Ras Tafari Inc. owns the night and the child-safety rules.
  • The devices carry rhythm, pattern and loudness. They do not turn speech into words, so an ASL interpreter and captions are booked separately.
  • Every participant can opt out of wearing a device at any time. Hearing and Deaf participants wear them alike.
  • A trained adult runs the controller. Devices are inventoried, charged before doors and stay on site: nobody takes one home.
  • No partner receives children's personal data or the right to film children without family consent.
