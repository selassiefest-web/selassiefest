The practice company must be a professional LLC (PLLC), not a regular LLC

Illinois law says an LLC providing a licensed profession like dentistry “must be formed in compliance with the Professional Limited Liability Company Act” (805 ILCS 180/1-25). That means:

• Its name must include “PLLC”. • Every member and manager must be an Illinois-licensed dentist. • Its filing must state that its purpose is dentistry, so it is filed on paper, not with the quick online form. • It needs an IDFPR registration before opening.

A PLLC can still be taxed as an S corporation, exactly as planned. The building company can be an ordinary LLC.

Company 1

Building LLC

  • Owns the real estate. Any owner allowed.
  • Ordinary Illinois LLC, filed online ($150).
  • Taxed as a “disregarded entity” by default if he is the only owner.
Company 2

Dental PLLC (S corporation)

  • Buys and runs the practice. Dentist owners only.
  • Professional LLC, filed on paper ($150), plus IDFPR registration ($50).
  • Elects S-corporation tax status on IRS Form 2553.
The PLLC leases the building from the Building LLC under a written lease at fair market rent (the attorney drafts it).

The sequence at a glance

  1. Hire the team. A dental-practice transition attorney and a CPA, before signing the purchase agreement.
  2. Form both companies. Building LLC online; dental PLLC on paper with a dentistry purpose clause.
  3. Get two EINs on two different days. The IRS issues only one EIN per person per day.
  4. Register the PLLC with IDFPR before the practice opens under new ownership.
  5. File Form CBS-1 at least 10 business days before closing so you don't inherit the seller's unpaid Illinois taxes.
  6. Close. Deed the building into the Building LLC; the PLLC buys the practice assets.
  7. Within 30 days: notify IEMA about the X-ray machines and register with IDES as an employer.
  8. Within 2 months and 15 days: file Form 2553 for S-corporation status.
