Heavy cast-iron top grates are the reason for this one — they will carry a loaded Dutch pot without flexing, which a stamped-steel grate will not. Independent manual controls on each burner mean we can fry on one and hold oil or a second pot on the other.

On certification: inspectors say "NSF," but the Food Code language is equipment certified by an ANSI-accredited program. ETL‑Sanitation is exactly that, and satisfies the same requirement. Worth knowing the phrase if anyone questions it on site.

There is no one-burner model in this line. Atosa's countertop hot plates start at two burners, which suits the operation anyway.

Read this before buying a tank

A 20 lb propane tank cannot feed this burner

Propane has to boil inside the cylinder to produce vapour, and the cylinder draws that heat through its own walls. Pull harder than it can vaporize and the tank frosts over, pressure collapses, and the flame starves — which on a fry station means the oil temperature dies in the middle of a rush.

ShortfallRoughly 22,000–29,000 BTU/hr

Run a 40 lb or 100 lb cylinder, or manifold two 20s. This catches people constantly because the burner and the tank are bought separately and nothing in either box warns you. It gets worse as the tank empties and worse again in cold weather — which matters for any event later in the season.

Fire code at a temporary food event

  • Cylinder outside the tent, roughly five feet clear of the fabric and away from exits.
  • Secured upright so it cannot tip, and barricaded off from the public and foot traffic.
  • Hose protected from heat, foot traffic and vehicle paths.
  • Class K wet chemical extinguisher at the fry station. This is the single most common write-up at fry setups, because crews bring the ABC extinguisher they already own and it does not cover cooking oil.
  • Pot and burner away from public reach, on a noncombustible surface at working height.

Before we place the order

  • Order the LP model, not natural gas plus a conversion. The conversion is orifices and a regulator, and on a gas appliance it is work for a qualified tech, not a shortcut to save money.
  • Confirm the delivered price. Same spread problem as the juicers — $481 at one dealer, $572 plus $137 freight at another.
  • Plan a wind break. The ACHP-2 uses a standing pilot, and wind at an open park site will blow it out. A three-sided enclosure solves it; nothing else does.
  • Ask CDPH about the burner and the cane crusher in the same call. Confirm ETL-Sanitation satisfies them here, and settle the crusher question at the same time rather than making two calls.

The honest alternative

A jet or banjo burner — Bayou Classic, Concord and similar, $100 to $140 — is what a great many Caribbean vendors actually run under a Dutch pot. Far more heat, far less money, and better behaved in wind than a countertop range with a standing pilot.

It carries no sanitation listing, so for a permitted operation selling to the public it is the same gamble as the uncertified cane crusher: it may pass, it may not, and that is the inspector's call rather than ours. If we are calling CDPH anyway, it costs nothing to ask about both.
