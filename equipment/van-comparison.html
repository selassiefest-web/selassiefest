What decides it

The Woodstock van wins on nearly every axis

For $400 more than the Sprinter we get 70,000 fewer miles, a gas engine instead of a diesel with a multi-thousand-dollar repair already coming due, a fresh transmission, the same 16 ft box, and a seller with a real track record.

The only thing it loses on is distance — Woodstock is the farthest of the three.

The Crest Hill van should come off the list

At $9,000 it is roughly double what comparable vans are selling for in this market right now, and its listing contradicts itself on mileage by a factor of ten.

An odometer reading that disagrees with the seller's own description is how vehicles end up with a permanent title brand. Not worth the drive.

Before we commit to the Woodstock van

  • Get the transmission receipt. Ask whether it was new, remanufactured or used — the warranty differs enormously — and why it needed one at 145,000 miles.
  • Confirm whether Jose is a licensed dealer. The reviews describe a shop, not a private seller. That is mostly good for us, but it changes the paperwork and may add sales tax.
  • Pay for an independent inspection. About $150 on a lift. On a $5,900 vehicle it is the highest-return money in the process, and it gives the board a documented basis for the purchase.
  • Title it to Ras Tafari Inc., not to an individual — Illinois will only transfer a title into the name on the bill of sale, and this cannot be corrected later without a second transfer.
  • Get the commercial insurance quote first. A vehicle titled to the organization needs a commercial policy; a personal policy will not cover it.

Applies to either box truck

  • Plates. A 3500 chassis at roughly 11,000 lb gross weight registers as a second-division Class D vehicle in Illinois — about $238 a year rather than a passenger plate.
  • Height. Both box trucks stand 10 to 11 feet tall. No parking garages, watch low clearances, and neither will sit in an ordinary parking space.
  • Insurance. Commercial rates on a box truck run higher than on a cargo van. Worth pricing before we choose.
  • The upside. A 16 ft box carries the full sound system, tents, tables and the cook setup in a single trip — and with a rear door at box height, a ramp lets us load the juicing equipment, which ships on pallets.
