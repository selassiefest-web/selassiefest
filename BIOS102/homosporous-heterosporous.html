Big picture

Two kinds of apartments

Homosporous = studio: one room, everything happens in it. Heterosporous = duplex: two units, two jobs. Tap both floor plans to continue.

  • 1 spore type grows into 1 gametophyte that usually makes both sperm and eggs.
  • The gametophyte is bigger and free-living (it feeds itself).
  • Examples: most ferns, horsetails, club mosses.
  • Micro = male. Small spore, male gametophyte, sperm.
  • Mega = female. Big spore, female gametophyte, eggs.
  • Gametophytes are tiny and stay inside the spore wall, fed by the parent plant.
  • Examples: Selaginella, Isoetes, water ferns, all seed plants. This is the stepping stone to seeds.

Exam traps

Where people lose points

Open all three to continue.

Is heterosporous the same as dioecious?

No. Heterosporous is about the spores (two sizes). Dioecious is about separate male and female whole plants in seed plants. An oak is heterosporous but not dioecious.

Do homosporous plants always have bisexual gametophytes?

Usually, not always. Some liverworts like Marchantia are homosporous, yet each gametophyte grows up male or female. So the deciding test is how many spore types, not what the gametophyte does. Check how your instructor words this on exams.

Why does heterospory matter?

Keeping the tiny female gametophyte inside the parent is the first step toward the ovule, and an ovule becomes a seed. No heterospory, no seeds.
