Big picture

Two phyla, same five jobs (plus germ layers)

Both phyla are animals without a coelom (acoelomates, handout p. 79). The handout does not divide them into organ systems, so we organize by job: feeding, support, movement, sensing, reproduction. Tap both phyla to continue.

  • Pore-bearer. “Porifera” means “to bear pores.” Water enters the incurrent and radial canals, reaches the spongocoel, and exits the osculum. Handout p. 81
  • Loosely assembled. Cells do not form definite tissues; they can survive and reassemble even after being forced through a fine mesh. No mouth, no digestive tract. p. 81
  • Filter feeders. Heterotrophs, adult is sessile, so people mistake sponges for plants. Collar cells line the inner surface and look like choanoflagellates, the protists sponges are thought to come from. p. 81
  • Classified by skeleton: calcium carbonate (Calcarea), silica/glass (Hexactinellida), spongin protein (Demospongiae). pp. 81-83
  • Stingers. Cnidocytes hold barbed, harpoon-like nematocysts. Hydroids (Portuguese man-of-war), jellyfish, sea anemones, corals. Handout p. 86
  • Two germ layers (ectoderm and endoderm), no mesoderm: epidermis, mesoglea, gastrodermis around a gastrovascular cavity. pp. 86-87
  • Two body forms: polyp (sessile) and medusa (free-swimming). Some classes have only one form; others alternate. p. 86
  • Nerve nets, no brain or nerve cord. Contractile cells shorten, lengthen and bend the body; the water-filled cavity is a hydrostatic skeleton. p. 86
Study focus: germ layers

Porifera has NO germ layers. Cnidaria has TWO.

This is one of the most testable differences. Germ layers are the embryonic layers that build the adult body. Think of a ladder:

Animal groupGerm layersWhat it makes
Porifera (sponges)None. Cells are loosely assembled and form no definite tissues.Specialized cells only (collar cells, amoebocytes, porocytes, epidermal cells), not true tissues
CnidariaTwo (diploblastic): ectoderm + endoderm. No mesoderm.Epidermis from ectoderm; gastrodermis from endoderm; mesoglea between them
Animals with a mesodermThree: ectoderm, mesoderm, endodermMuscle, skeleton and circulatory system come from mesoderm (you meet these phyla later)

Handout pp. 81, 86-87 Sponge cells “do not form definite tissues”; cnidarians “lack a mesoderm germ layer” and “have two embryonic germ layers, the ectoderm and endoderm.” Add-on The handout never uses the words “sponges have no germ layers,” but that is the standard textbook conclusion from having no true tissues. The three-layer row is also standard biology (review question 3). Confirm the wording with your instructor.

Memory line: Sponge = zero layers, jelly = two layers, no mesoderm. Epidermis = ectoderm, gastrodermis = endoderm.

Exam traps

Where people lose points

Open all five to continue.

Sponges have an “epidermis” and a skeleton. Doesn’t that mean they have germ layers?

No. Sponges have no germ layers and no definite tissues; their “epidermal cells” are just one of several specialized cell types. Cnidarians are the first to have two real germ layers (ectoderm and endoderm), and they still have no mesoderm. If a question asks how many germ layers: sponge 0, cnidarian 2.

Both phyla have “epidermal” cells. So is the epidermis a good way to tell them apart?

No. Sponges list epidermal cells among their specialized cells (p. 81), and cnidarians have an epidermis from ectoderm (p. 87). The difference is that sponge cells form no definite tissues, while the cnidarian epidermis is part of a real two-layer body wall.

The osculum is the sponge’s mouth. True?

False. Sponges have no mouth or digestive tract. Water exits through the osculum, the open end. Cnidarians are the ones with a mouth and a gastrovascular cavity.

Are cnidarians all free-swimming, and sponges all stuck in place?

Not quite. Adult sponges are sessile, but a cnidarian can be a sessile polyp or a free-swimming medusa. Anthozoa (corals, anemones) are polyps only, Scyphozoa (jellyfish) are mostly medusae, and Hydrozoa like Obelia have both. Sponge larvae swim, too (p. 81).

Which one is hermaphroditic?

Sponges: most make both eggs and sperm in one animal. The handout describes Hydra as male (testis) or female (ovary). Both phyla also reproduce asexually by budding; only sponges make gemmules.
