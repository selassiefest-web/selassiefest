Lab Exam Study Strategy: Build Your Own Exam

Create and take your own exam

Do not reread notes repeatedly. Use the weekly quizzes as templates. They closely reflect the style, vocabulary, and concepts emphasized throughout the course.

Focus on "why," not just "what"

For every term, ask yourself:

  • What is the structure?
  • What is its function?
  • Where is it found?
  • Is it haploid or diploid?
  • Which generation does it belong to?
  • Is it homologous or analogous to a similar structure in another group, and what does that show about how the two groups are related?

Example. Pick any reproductive structure from the course. Instead of memorizing its name, ask:

  • What does it produce?
  • Is it male or female?
  • In which groups is it found?
  • What happens to what it produces?

Turn each unit into your own question bank

This guide covers how to study. Your quizzes, lab manual, and lecture notes are the source of the content. For each unit, use the same prompts to write your questions.

Units to cover

  • Cyanobacteria and the cell types of the three domains
  • Protists and fungi
  • Mosses and liverworts
  • Ferns and other seedless vascular plants
  • Gymnosperms and angiosperms
  • Microscope use and scientific method

Prompts for every unit

  • How does this group get its energy and nutrients?
  • How does it reproduce, and what role does water play?
  • Which life cycle stage is dominant, and is it haploid or diploid?
  • Which structures belong to the gametophyte, and which to the sporophyte?
  • What features separate this group from the one studied before it?
  • What did you see or do with this group in lab?

Make a two-column table of structure and function for each unit. Cover one column and fill it in from memory, then check it against your sources and fix what you missed.

Practical lab stations

A large portion of the exam involves identifying specimens, slides, and microscope structures from your lab sessions. Use your lab manual and your own lab notes to list every organism, slide, and specimen you handled in lab. For each one, be able to:

  • Identify it on sight, under the microscope and as a living or preserved specimen.
  • Name its group (domain or kingdom) and whether its cells are prokaryotic or eukaryotic.
  • Label its structures and state the function of each.
  • Say whether each structure is haploid or diploid, and which generation it belongs to.
  • Use the microscope correctly and name its parts and what they do.

Best study method

  1. Make a blank exam using the quizzes and labs as your guide. The quizzes show the types of questions you should be able to answer.
  2. Take your own exam without using notes.
  3. Exchange exams with another student and take each other's exam.
  4. Grade the exams together.
  5. The most important step: go over every question and every answer. If you cannot explain why an answer is correct, continue studying that topic.

What successful students do

Successful students can:

  • Identify structures on diagrams.
  • Explain the difference between haploid and diploid stages.
  • Distinguish gametophytes from sporophytes.
  • Explain homosporous versus heterosporous life cycles.
  • Identify specimens and microscope slides.
  • Explain the function of structures instead of simply memorizing names.
